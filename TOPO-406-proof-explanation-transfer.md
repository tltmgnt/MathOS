# TOPO 406 — Proof, Explanation & Transfer

**Status:** Proposed curriculum module

## Purpose

Train learners to turn a correct result into a reusable method for reasoning, system diagnosis, and intervention.

## Learning objectives

By the end of the module, learners should be able to:

1. Distinguish a claim from its evidence and assumptions.
2. Reconstruct a proof or argument independently.
3. Identify the decisive mechanism rather than memorize a conclusion.
4. Test generalizations using counterexamples and boundary conditions.
5. Translate formal reasoning into a practical, measurable intervention.
6. Assess whether an intervention worked and document when the method fails.
7. Separate formal correctness from empirical validity.

## Worked example: sum of the first n positive odd integers

Claim:

\[
1+3+5+\cdots +(2n-1)=n^2.
\]

Algebraic verification:

\[
\sum_{k=1}^{n}(2k-1)=2\sum_{k=1}^{n}k-n
=2\frac{n(n+1)}{2}-n=n^2.
\]

Structural explanation: each new odd number adds the next outer layer to a square. The first term creates a 1-by-1 square; adding 3 creates a 2-by-2 square; adding 5 creates a 3-by-3 square, and so on.

Transfer question: what other problems can be solved by recognizing predictable increments or nested structure? The learner must also identify when this analogy does not apply.

## Capstone assignment

Choose an AI-generated solution to a mathematical or systems problem. Verify the reasoning, explain the central mechanism in plain language, identify assumptions and limitations, construct at least one counterexample or boundary case, and test whether the method transfers to a second problem.

## Assessment rubric

| Dimension | Evidence of achievement |
|---|---|
| Correctness | Valid reasoning, accurate calculations, assumptions stated |
| Explanation | Clear account of the decisive mechanism |
| Counterexamples | Meaningful tests of boundaries and failure conditions |
| Transfer | Application to a second problem with limitations explained |
| Validation | Appropriate measurable test and interpretation of results |
| Reproducibility | Another person can inspect and repeat the work |

## Acceptance principle

A result should not be accepted solely because it sounds plausible or was generated by an AI system. It must be checked at a level appropriate to its use and risk.
