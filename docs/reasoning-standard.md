MathOS Reasoning Standard

Purpose

MathOS converts candidate answers into inspectable, explainable, and testable knowledge. Every substantial conclusion should state its evidence, assumptions, limitations, and verification status.

Reasoning Pipeline

1. Define the problem, objective, constraints, and terms.
2. Generate a candidate solution or hypothesis.
3. Verify calculations, logic, assumptions, and boundary conditions.
4. Explain the mechanism that makes the solution work.
5. Challenge the conclusion using counterexamples and alternative explanations.
6. Transfer the method to related problems and document its limits.
7. Validate predictions against independent evidence or observed outcomes.
8. Revise conclusions when evidence contradicts expectations.

Evidence Status

* Verified: Passed the specific documented verification checks.
* Supported: Backed by relevant evidence but not conclusively established.
* Hypothesis: A proposed explanation awaiting meaningful testing.
* Untested: Required evaluation has not yet occurred.
* Rejected: Contradicted by valid evidence under the stated conditions.

Never claim broader verification than was actually performed.

Mathematical Verification

State definitions, assumptions, and the proposition being proved. Check logical inferences, calculations, edge cases, and counterexamples. Use formal proof assistants when appropriate. Distinguish informal explanations from machine-checked proofs. Passing sample tests alone does not prove a general theorem.

Empirical and Causal Validation

Distinguish correlation, prediction, and causation. Identify alternative explanations and potential confounders. Define measurable outcomes, document uncertainty, and compare predictions with observed results. A logically valid model may still fail to represent reality.

Transferability

Document the conditions under which a method applies, its required inputs, assumptions, failure conditions, counterexamples, and evidence from related problems.

Multi-Agent Review

Agents should contribute meaningfully different methods or evidence. Agreement is not independent confirmation when agents share assumptions, data, or failure modes. Arbitration should evaluate evidence quality, uncertainty, and consequences rather than merely average scores.

Human Oversight

Identify material uncertainty, operational risks, and required human approvals. Preserve accountability and protect sensitive information. Do not present unsupported conclusions as established facts.

Acceptance Rule

A result is ready for use only when its verification status, assumptions, limitations, and intended application are clear.

Core principle: Verify what can be verified, explain why a method works, test where it applies, and identify where it fails.
