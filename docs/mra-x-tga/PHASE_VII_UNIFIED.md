# Phase VII — unified mathematical execution

Run from the repository root: `python tools/mra_x_unified.py --output unified-evidence.json`. Python 3.13, NumPy 2.3.5 and SciPy 1.17.0 were used locally. The new opt-in controller combines structural partitioning, Phase II prediction, Phase V exact order synthesis (through Phase VI certificates), logical execution, Phase III tape audits and the Phase II/III frontier's HiGHS adapter. It does not replace all older campaign controllers or modify curriculum acceptance.

## Correctness argument (informal)
Every nonconstant factor lies within one connected component of the primal graph. Constants are summed separately, and an infeasible constant or component makes the whole problem infeasible. Otherwise global optimum is the sum of component optima plus constants, and the union of component witnesses attains it. An elimination in one component cannot change another component's factor scopes. Hence for the reference kernel the enumeration cost of any global order equals the sum of costs of its restrictions to the components. If each component order attains its local certified lower bound, their concatenation attains a global lower bound for enumeration cost. This is not a proof of globally optimal runtime, nor is the proof mechanized.

Components of at most 10 variables receive checked subset certificates; larger components receive heuristic order selection, but exact factor elimination and solution-trace replay still apply. Tape replay is optional and limited by the specialized compiler's envelope. The aggregate enumeration budget bounds the proposed solution-kernel enumeration count, not certificate generation, verification, runtime, memory or tape transitions. The declared input envelope of 256 variables, domains 1–4 and 2048 factors is an admission limit, not a guarantee of successful solving. No uniform machine or original RE-X integration is claimed.

## Executed evidence
24 structured synthetic problems: 20 disconnected hypergraph networks of 12–48 variables, four connected chains of 128 variables. Across all 104 components, 100 exact-order component certificates and 100 specialized tape audits were checked (19,200 inequalities and 859,987 transitions in primary tape runs). All 24 exact solutions matched the logical reference and SciPy/HiGHS, including 19 feasible and five infeasible problems; the infeasible examples used conflicting nonempty unary factors, so HiGHS actually ran. Witness costs were checked for feasible cases. All 20 disconnected cases have globally certified enumeration orders; the four chains have verified exact solutions, not certified optimal orders. 9,917 logical evaluations versus 10,168 for min-fill (251 saved, about 2.47%). Six boundary, budget and tampered-flag controls passed. The two complete evidence files were byte-identical locally with the same dependencies. External witness choices may differ across solver versions. No runtime or peak-memory comparison was performed.

The report exports full input instances, solution traces and component-order certificates. Its verifier replays claimed tape transition counts. The verifier is a separate algorithmic path but uses shared trusted modules, not an independently certified checker or hardened untrusted-input service. SHA-256 fingerprints are integrity aids, not signatures.

Original RE-X integration, Lean kernel certification, fully independent review, external benchmarks beyond HiGHS, integrated incremental and evolution scheduling, and sandboxed source-code evolution remain open. The PR remains subject to review; no 10/10 claim.
