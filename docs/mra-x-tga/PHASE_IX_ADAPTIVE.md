# Phase IX — adaptive structural edit reasoning

Run `python tools/mra_x_adaptive.py --output adaptive-evidence.json`. Fixed domains (up to 64 variables, each size at most four); factor insertion, deletion, duplication, permutation and cost edits are allowed. Compare predicted-order, recursive-separator-order and previous-order options using a conservative cache-miss estimate; choose the smallest upper bound among options that satisfy the full enumeration budget. Reuse projections and backpointers when the eliminated variable and entire bucket factor multiset are identical, regardless of order. Rebuild candidate hierarchies on each solve; this is not local tree repair. Separators of size three are considered in private regions of at most 20 variables. Larger regions search sizes one and two. Unsplittable leaves can be larger.

## Informal correctness
With fixed domains, the output table and deterministic witness rows of eliminating a variable depend only on the complete multiset of its input factors. Reusing identical bucket contents is equivalent to recomputation even across orders. Induction along the selected order preserves the logical reference's trace. The estimator replaces outputs of predicted misses with unknown tables; it only counts a hit if all input tables are known and exactly equal to the cached key. Induction establishes that every predicted hit really is a hit, hence observed candidate evaluations cannot exceed the estimated upper bound. The bound excludes planning, key comparison, copying, verification and runtime. Neither argument is mechanized.

## Local evidence
12 workloads (chains, cycles, overlapping ternary factors; 8–64 variables), 12 initial solves plus 108 updates. All 120 traces matched fresh fixed-order execution and the Phase VI checker; all objectives agreed with three comparison paths, 30 also with exhaustive enumeration. 96 feasible and 24 infeasible results, zero disagreements. 2,123 reused buckets; 18 order changes. Five controls cover a size-three separator in K(3,5), empty and singleton problems and budget rejection. Two runs reproduced all non-performance evidence and both digests verified. Policy choices: predictor 82, previous 30, hierarchy 8.

Combined setup and update logical enumeration: adaptive 13,171, fixed min-fill cache 19,744, fixed hierarchical order cache 13,025, fresh min-fill 32,850. Adaptive is about 33.29% lower than fixed min-fill, but 1.12% higher than fixed hierarchy. On updates alone adaptive 9,892, fixed min-fill 16,416, fixed hierarchy 7,741, fresh min-fill 29,522. Estimated adaptive upper bound 13,501; actual 13,171; same-selected-order full recomputation 34,813. No guaranteed superiority over alternative orders or workloads.

Exploratory timings with tracemalloc enabled, one separate 32-variable cyclic workload of ten solves, three sequential repetitions per strategy including initialization/planning. First-run median: adaptive 277.16 ms, fixed min-fill cache 57.65 ms, fresh min-fill 82.51 ms. Median Python traced heap peaks approximately 455,632, 487,872 and 286,280 bytes respectively. These measurements are instrumented, not native RSS or controlled benchmark results. Fixed hierarchy was not timed. Adaptive was substantially slower on this workload despite fewer enumeration evaluations; no default policy promotion.

No original RE-X integration, Lean proof, independent replication, local structural repair, arbitrary-code evolution or broader matched solver benchmarks are claimed. The original acceptance gates remain open.
