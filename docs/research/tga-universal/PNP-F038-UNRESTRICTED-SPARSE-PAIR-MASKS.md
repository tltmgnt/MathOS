## 1. Result

Use the inherited full nine-group normalized family, n=19m-7, L=n+1+2n^2+8 binom(n,3)=Theta(m^3), arbitrary negative cross clauses of widths 1..3, fixed one-hot/OR/guard clauses and actual pins. For an ordered pair of groups u,v define F_e(a) to be the set of v digits b for which the selected cross clause forbids (u=a,v=b) in context e (empty for the pair clause, or (g,c) for a third group and digit). Define

    D(u,v) = min(m, max_a [ |F_empty(a)| + sum_{g not in {u,v}} max_c |F_(g,c)(a)| ]),
    D = min_{u != v} D(u,v).

For every fixed epsilon in (0,1/10], all SAT members satisfying D<=m^(1/10-epsilon) eventually fit the original exact cubic stage. A fixed original three-symbol single-tape witness program on this promise has charged time

    O(m^(62/7) (D+1)^(10/7) polylog(m+2))
      = O(L^(3-10epsilon/21) polylog(L+2)),

where the last bound applies to the stated promise, and visited span O(m^3 polylog m). The first bound is conditional on the internal mask-count gate below, which holds eventually on the promise. No randomness, uniqueness promise, new input variables or change to U is required. This is a different input-sensitive sufficient condition from the greedy color count K. In particular there are complete-graph unique instances with K=m, full contextual domains and global fanout W>=(m-2)^9, yet D=1. They fall within this new cutoff. ## 2. Exact mask cover of the solution relation

For a fixed ordered pair u,v and a tuple s on the other seven groups, define the active forbidden neighbor set for u digit a:

    F(a,s) = F_empty(a) union union_{g not in {u,v}} F_(g,s_g)(a).

It has at most D(u,v) elements. Pair-dependent clauses are satisfied exactly when the chosen v digit is outside this union. The bound is computed from raw selector bits; it need not equal the actual maximum union size.

Let a family of subsets B of [0,m) have the following separation property: for every b and every F not containing b with |F|<=D, at least one B contains b and is disjoint from F. For each B create a branch as follows:
* Keep all original selected clauses not involving both u and v.
* Remove every original selected clause involving both u and v.
* Forbid v digits outside B using negative units.
* For each removed clause whose v digit lies in B, delete its v literal and select the resulting negative unary or binary clause.

All replacements use actual positions in the original normalized selector. Duplicate projected clauses are OR-deduplicated. Original fixed clauses and pins are unchanged. The resulting graph has no u,v edge.

For a full tuple (a,b,s), the new conditions are exactly the unchanged constraints, b in B, and B intersect F(a,s) empty. Soundness follows because b in B then implies b not in F(a,s). Conversely, for any original solution, the separation property produces a B making all the new conditions true. Thus the UNION of branch model sets is exactly the original model set, including the full original witness extension. Unlike the preceding color partition, this union need NOT be disjoint. A unique original witness may survive several branches, but every SAT branch is then uniquely satisfiable with that same witness. A matching branch index is essential when joining left and right separator messages; mixing different masks is not sound.

## 3. Uniform deterministic mask construction

When D=0 use the single full mask. Otherwise let h=ceil(log2 m)=(m-1).bit_length() and q be the least power of two strictly greater than D*h. Thus D*h<q<=2D*h. Use a field GF(q). Write each digit a in base q and interpret its digits as coefficients of a polynomial P_a of degree at most h-1 (the loose bound suffices). Distinct a give distinct polynomials, and for any b!=a the nonzero difference P_b-P_a has at most h-1 roots in the field. This root bound follows by repeated division by X-x at distinct roots. The union of bad evaluation points over at most D forbidden digits therefore has size <=D(h-1)<q.

For each field point x and value y, define the mask B_(x,y)={a:P_a(x)=y}. For desired b outside F, choose an evaluation point where P_b differs from all P_a with a in F, and choose y=P_b(x). That mask contains b and misses F. The list of all q^2 masks has the required property. Empty masks and repeated masks are harmless; the theoretical program keeps the fixed q^2 index list without deduplication. Define J=1 for D=0, and J=q^2 otherwise, so J=O((D+1)^2 log^2(m+2)). This is an explicit uniform family, not an existential random isolation list or advice.

The field construction uses the elementary existence of a monic irreducible polynomial of every positive degree k over GF(2). For completeness, in an algebraic closure the roots of X^(2^k)-X form a field of 2^k distinct elements (the derivative is -1 and the roots are closed under field operations by the Frobenius identity). A root of minimal degree d has d dividing k, by its Frobenius orbit length. The elements in proper subfields are at most sum_{d=1..floor(k/2)} 2^d < 2^k for k>=2; k=1 is immediate. Thus some root has degree k. A deterministic bit algorithm enumerates monic degree-k binary polynomials with constant coefficient one and tests division by every monic polynomial of positive degree <=floor(k/2). A reducible polynomial has a factor in that range, so the first passing polynomial is irreducible. There are O(q) candidates and O(sqrt(q)) trial divisors; each division costs poly(k) bit steps, giving O(q^(3/2) polylog q) time with O(polylog q) scratch. Use polynomial residue multiplication and Horner evaluation over GF(q) with polylog(m+2) bit operations per digit evaluation on the promise below. The computation does not assume indexed field lookup tables.

## 5. Exact cubic consequence

Substitute J=O((D+1)^2 log^2(m+2)) into the batched time bound. If D<=m^(1/10-epsilon) for a fixed 0<epsilon<=1/10, J=O(m^(1/5-2epsilon) polylog m), so J^5<=m eventually and time is O(m^(9-10epsilon/7) polylog m)=O(L^(3-10epsilon/21) polylog L). This is uniform in masks and pins. All algorithms above are fixed finite control, not a separate program for every field size, mask, or epsilon; the description length ell is fixed. The exact stage 3 ceil(log2 L) allocates this description at least L^3/2^ell transitions once eligible. Strict polynomial slack eventually dominates logarithms and the fixed penalty. No numerical threshold or compiled table is supplied. At the equality D=m^(1/10) this construction does NOT establish exact cubic inclusion. The two independent K and D cutoffs may be combined by the existence of their separate fixed descriptions. Any unbounded sequence of actual cubic misses must eventually have D>m^(1/10-epsilon) for every fixed epsilon in the stated range, in addition to earlier necessary conditions. No such sequence is constructed here.

## 6. A complete high-conflict, full-width unique control

For all m>=3, let T consist of the four triples on groups {0,1,2,3} and the five cyclic consecutive triples on groups {4,5,6,7,8}. On each triple select precisely the forbidden tuples for the following relation: even parity of nonzero digit indicators, and when two digits are nonzero, they must be distinct. For every pair NOT contained in any triple in T, in increasing group order, forbid exactly (a,(a+1) mod m) for all a. Select no other cross clauses. Pair restrictions do not affect any constrained triple in T because no pair inside it receives a binary clause.

The parity equations force every indicator to zero: on the four-group region sum its four equations, obtaining zero total parity and hence each omitted bit zero; on the five-group region successive equations give period three, which combined with period five forces a constant bit and then zero. The shifted binary exclusions allow the all-zero tuple. Thus the sole tuple is all zero, with a unique inherited full assignment for every pin pattern.

Every pair is an edge, either within a selected parity triple or through its own shifted binary exclusions. For a pair within T, choose third-digit context zero on one containing parity triple. The zero row has allowed columns {0}, and each nonzero row a has all nonzero columns except a. These are m distinct nonempty rows for m>=3, in either endpoint order. For all other pairs the binary context already has m distinct nonempty rows (each excludes a different column), in either order. Hence EVERY ordered context-conflict graph is complete and K=m. Yet a pair crossing the two parity regions has only one forbidden v digit per u digit, in the binary context, so D<=1; no pair has D=0 because every graph edge has a selected clause. Therefore D=1.

Every digit has support in every closed local table. On a constrained triple use all zero or two distinct nonzero digits plus zero. On other supports of width<=3, only shifted pair exclusions can be active: for a specified digit choose the next digit avoiding at most one value and the third avoiding at most two, possible at m>=3. Every group is in a parity triple; its raw and contextual signatures are incomparable by the context witnesses used in the prior parity controls. Both static and contextual modes retain all m digits. Directional fanout maxima are >=m-2 on a constrained triple (one zero and one nonzero parent), and on other local supports all-zero parents are compatible and remove at most two possible appended digits. Thus W>=(m-2)^9. This family separates the new sufficient condition from all four earlier structural tests (width, fanout, graph and color count), but its all-zero solution remains cheap; it is not evidence of computational hardness.
