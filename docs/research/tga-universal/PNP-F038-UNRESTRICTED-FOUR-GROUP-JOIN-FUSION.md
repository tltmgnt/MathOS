## 1. New cutoff

In the inherited nine-group family, n=19m-7 and L=Theta(m^3), let J_P be the join of all selected width-1..3 cross constraints supported within a four-group set P. More explicitly, J_P consists of all tuples in [0,m)^P violating none of these constraints. Let R=min_{|P|=4}|J_P| and rho4=R/m^4. For every fixed 0<epsilon<=1, all SAT inputs with rho4<=m^(-epsilon) eventually fit the original exact cubic stage. A fixed original three-symbol single-tape machine has time

    O((m^7 + m^5 (R+b) b^delta) polylog(m+2)),
    b=Theta(m^(3/(3+delta))),

and visited span O(m^3 polylog m), for any fixed rational 0<delta<1. Choosing delta<epsilon/2 yields O(m^(9-epsilon/2) polylog m)=O(L^(3-epsilon/6) polylog L) on the promise. The solver uses the prior six-group fixed-depth recursive tiling result with exponent 6+delta, not an unproved exact O(b^6) bound. It handles unique and multi-model SAT. Unlike the preceding pair/triple fusion, the joined relation may contain nearly m^4 entries; storing it in full would defeat the workspace bound.

## 2. Detect and stream the join

Read the actual normalized unary header and canonical pool, preserve the original selector, extract selected cross records and guarded pins using the earlier parsing routines. Use fixed sequential bit tapes with a fixed finite number of heads. For each of the 126 four-group sets, enumerate all m^4 tuples lexicographically, batching at most C=m^3 tuples at a time. Assign IDs in enumeration order. For every support contained in P, emit (projection,ID) queries, sort by projection, merge against the selected forbidden tuple list, and flag matching IDs. Sort flags by ID and OR-merge into the original ordered batch. The surviving tuples are exactly J_P. No need to produce a dense m^4 table. There are a constant number of supports and each original list has O(m^3) records, so external sequential merge sorting, sequential comparison, flagging, and rewinds cost O(C polylog m) bit operations per batch, including a short batch. Total O(m^4 polylog m) bit time and O(m^3 polylog m) visited span for all supports (126 is constant). Count survivors by binary counters, retaining only the minimum R and its support, with deterministic tie break. If any count is zero, halt on blank, correctly rejecting UNSAT.

Run the same enumeration once more for the chosen P, this time yielding survivors in lexicographic order in consecutive chunks of b entries. Maintain a partial chunk across batch boundaries; flush only when full, or once at the end. Thus exactly ceil(R/b) chunks, not one extra short chunk per batch. The generation time is still O(m^4 polylog m) bit operations, span O(m^3 polylog m). The fixed-track simulation with sweep overhead O(m^3 polylog m) per bit step, including initial raw expansion, charges O(m^7 polylog m) physical moves. It may be paused at a completed simulated transition with all simulated head markers, batch cursors, counters and pending chunk preserved. On yielding a chunk, copy its O(b polylog m) bits to a separate worker component by per-bit shuttles over the used span (O(m^3 b polylog m)), then run the worker until this chunk is exhausted. Resume the generator from its preserved head markers and control state. All components belong to one fixed finite product alphabet encoded by constant-width blocks of the actual symbols 0,1,2. Worker activity does not move or overwrite the generator components. The copying and return cost per chunk is dominated by at least one tile extraction below. No complete J_P stream is written, and the generation is not restarted for every tile.

## 3. Six-group fusion and exact local semantics

For each yielded chunk, enumerate the block indices of the remaining five groups, with block width b and possible final short intervals. Encode the four-coordinate tuple by its chunk index j; the remaining five local digits are translated by their block offsets. For each original selected cross clause, discard it if a specified unfused digit is outside the tile. Otherwise, if it meets P, emit one local negative clause for every chunk entry agreeing with all its specified P digits, replacing these literals by the single fused digit; if disjoint from P, emit just its translated clause. Support width cannot increase, including clauses meeting P in multiple groups. Add negative unary clauses forbidding nonexistent chunk entries and out-of-range unfused digits; OR-deduplicate. The local formula has precisely six groups, each of width b, with inherited fixed one-hot/OR/pin/guard clauses and local zero pins, in the same normalized syntax as the earlier distributed family.

An in-range local tuple maps to a unique original tuple, and it violates an original selected cross clause iff it violates an emitted translation of that clause. Padding excludes all other local tuples. Every original solution is in J_P and thus in exactly one chunk and one tile, proving a disjoint bijection across local solution sets. Local auxiliary and pin assignments are not copied: on success recover the original tuple, actual pins and fixed extension, and output the original contiguous n-bit witness with the physical halt head at its first bit.

## 4. Charged extraction and tile bound

Choose b as the least integer >=2 with b^q>=m^p (capped at m), where the fixed rational p/q=3/(3+delta). The number of tiles is ceil(R/b) ceil(m/b)^5=O((R+b)m^5/b^6). For each tile scan the stationary selected cross records using the inherited carry-shift mobile workspace, holding the chunk map, counters and translated records in O(b^3 polylog m) blocks. For a fixed original support, each emitted local clause identifies at most one original selected record: its fused index identifies the chunk tuple and its other digits identify the unfused values. So even counting duplicates across the constant number of supports, only O(b^3) records are emitted. Reading each original record, scanning the chunk map locally, and charging O(b^3 polylog m) per actual append, including returning to its marked map cursor, costs O(m^3 b^3 polylog m) total, with O(b^6 polylog m) append work dominated by that term. This is the parent sparse fusion extraction, with the chunk supplied by the paused generator instead of a full stationary allowed stream. Rewinds and preserving all other components are included. There is no additional global sweep per emitted record beyond this bound.

Construct the actual six-group local raw selector by sequential queries over the bounded translated list, O(b^6 polylog m) moves as in the earlier tiling proof. Run the fixed six-group recursive-tiling solver with time O(b^(6+delta) polylog b) and span O(b^3 polylog b). Its existence for every fixed delta>0 was established in the recursive-tiling note, using a sufficiently large FIXED depth. Encode its physical symbols on a local component and follow its head with constant overhead per local step; mark used extents for cleanup. Do not sweep the entire outer tape per local transition. Reuse the same components after each local failure. The worker and generator share one physical head and a fixed number of tracks. All per-chunk copy and cleanup costs are dominated by the tile bound. Output reconstruction is O(m^4 polylog m) by shuttles, on success only; on exhaustion halt on blank.

The total time is O((m^7 + (R+b)m^5/b^6*(m^3 b^3+b^(6+delta))) polylog m). Balancing by b gives the stated bound. The initial m^7 term includes detection and the second streaming pass. For R<=m^(4-epsilon) and delta<epsilon/2, b<=m and epsilon<=1 imply R+b=O(m^(4-epsilon)), so total O(m^(9-epsilon/2) polylog m). All stationary, paused and worker components occupy O(m^3 polylog m) span. The machine is fixed independent of m, masks, pins and chosen P. At exact stage s=3 ceil(log2 L), its fixed description length ell is allocated at least L^3/2^ell transitions once eligible, so the strict polynomial saving dominates the penalty and logarithms eventually and uniformly on the promise. No explicit transition table, numerical threshold or stage execution is claimed.

## 5. Dense-table separation control

For multiples m of 8 with m>=16, on every pair i<j forbid the digit pairs with (x_j-x_i) mod m in [m/4,m/2). On triple (0,1,2), forbid exactly tuples with opposite parity of x0,x1 and x2!=x0; on (0,1,3), forbid exactly tuples with equal parity of x0,x1 and x3!=x0. No other cross constraints. All zero is a global solution. The graph is complete.

For every (x0,x1), one of x2 and x3 is forced to x0 by the two triple restrictions, so the join on {0,1,2,3} has R<=m^3, even before applying binary constraints. Thus rho4<=1/m. Yet EVERY closed pair and triple table has constant density bounded below uniformly in m. Indeed all pair assignments from I=[0,m/8) avoid the binary exclusions. For any nonspecial triple all assignments in I^3 are allowed; on either special triple, choose the parity pattern of the first two coordinates on which the third is free. For |I|>=2 each parity pattern accounts for at least |I|^2/4 pairs, and every third digit in I is allowed. Therefore each triple has at least |I|^3/4=m^3/2048 allowed entries and every pair has at least |I|^2=m^2/64. The preceding single-table sparsity test does not apply for any fixed positive saving exponent along this family.

All digits have local support: for special triples choose shared coordinates of the required parity (using the specified digit or its cyclic successor as needed), and choose all digits among those two adjacent values. They avoid binary exclusions since 0 and +/-1 mod m are outside the forbidden interval. Other tables use all equal digits. On every pair, all rows (and columns) have equal cardinality 3m/4 and are distinct, since the cyclic forbidden interval is proper, nonempty and has a unique cyclic starting boundary. Thus raw and closed signatures are pairwise incomparable in every group (already in binary contexts), and both contextual modes retain full width. Every pair's binary context forces m distinct nonempty allowed rows, so K=m in both orders. Its forbidden row degree is m/4, so previous D>=m/4. Every directional fanout is Omega(m): for special triples choose parent digits in I and use the free parity pattern, restricting the new shared digit at most to one parity (at least floor(|I|/2) choices); if the new digit is the third, choose the shared pair with the free parity and vary the third in I. On other tables choose all-zero parents and vary the new digit in I. Hence W=Omega(m^9). This is a SAT (not asserted unique) separation from the prior sufficient tests. The all-zero witness is easy; this is not evidence of computational hardness.

## 7. Disposition

Any unbounded sequence of actual exact-cubic misses within the nine-group family must eventually have rho4>m^(-epsilon) for every fixed 0<epsilon<=1. This is an additional necessary condition, not sufficient hardness. Next investigate five-group joins: a direct extension with six-group padding only yields slack when the five-group density saves more than one power of m, because local raw conversion and sweep costs have a sixth-power floor in the inherited bound. A stronger local interface or combined overlapping joins may improve that frontier. General nine-group cubic coverage, unbounded B_3 and P vs NP resolution remain OPEN.
