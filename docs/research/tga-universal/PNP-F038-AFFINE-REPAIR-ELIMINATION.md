## Exact local repair rule

Keep the previous provenance-checked affine extraction F=A AND B. If A is inconsistent, reject using its original XOR provenance. Otherwise parameterize its models as x=x0+Gt with t the free coordinate bits. We allow t in the ambient n-bit space, ignoring unused coordinates. For each residual clause C, the set of t falsifying C is an affine flat F_C (possibly empty), given by the simultaneous affine equations forcing all its literals false. Reduce these equations by Gaussian elimination. If F_C is empty, delete C with identity repair. Otherwise write its independent equations as a functional c_C on the direction space W_C. A clause identically false has W_C=0 and cannot be repaired by translation.

For nonempty F_C, a translation v is a valid deletion certificate for C if every t falsifying C is mapped to a point satisfying C and no other currently retained clause D which was true at t becomes false at t+v. The repair is conditional: if C is already true do nothing. Since translations in free coordinates preserve A, this is a model-repair rule establishing equisatisfiability of deleting C. It is a generalization of the single-variable flip used in blocked clause elimination; no priority or originality claim over existing elimination literature is made.

The necessary and sufficient conditions for this specific local (each-other-clause-preserving) rule are:
1. v restricted to W_C is nonzero.
2. For every other non-tautological clause D with nonzero restriction of v to W_D, F_C intersect (F_D+v) is empty.

Indeed, if D changes on its direction space, the two falsity flats F_D and F_D+v are disjoint. Thus a point in F_C intersect (F_D+v) is necessarily initially satisfying D and becomes falsifying after the flip. If v is zero on W_D, D is unchanged. Conversely if no such intersections occur, every initially true clause stays true. These tests are only linear-system consistency checks. The rule is stronger than necessary for preserving models of the conjunction of all remaining clauses: a witness to failure of a local pairwise test might violate a third clause. We make no completeness claim for arbitrary semantic deletion or arbitrary repair maps.

## Complete polynomial discovery within the translation rule

Let k=dim W_C. Enumerate the 2^k-1 nonzero values p of v on a basis of W_C. For each p choose any representative v0 realizing p by linear elimination. For each non-tautological D determine whether F_C intersects F_D+v0 by solving their joint equations. The intersection status depends only on p, not on the chosen representative: the two specifications on W_C and W_D have a common extension exactly when they agree on W_C intersect W_D, where v is already fixed by p.

If the intersection is nonempty, impose v|W_D=0. If it is empty, no additional restriction is needed. Together with v|W_C=p these are just linear equations for v. Solve them; any solution is a certified deletion direction. If every p fails, no translation satisfies the local rule. Empty falsity flats are dealt with separately. Consequently discovery is complete for the stated local rule and runs in 2^k poly(L), with no enumeration of the full affine assignment space. For width-three residual clauses k<=3, hence it is polynomial. The verifier only checks the proposed direction and local consistency tests; it does not need to repeat discovery or trust the solver's chosen p. Standard pure-literal deletion is a special case when no affine dependencies intervene, and the rule can also flip several free coordinates simultaneously.

## Composition, width, and end-to-end accounting

Greedily find a deletable clause in the current residual list, record its occurrence index and direction, remove it, and restart until no deletion is found. At most m clauses can be removed; polynomially many searches over at most m candidates suffice for width three. All affine source clauses remain intact. Reconstruct a model by starting from a model of the reduced formula and undoing deletions in reverse order, applying the conditional translation whenever the restored clause is false. ## Escape from the complete-graph separator obstruction

The previous monotone complete-graph family F_n=AND_{i<j}(x_i OR x_j) has optimal separator rank Omega(n) under its original clause representation. With no extracted affine equations, choose v to flip x_i when deleting clause (x_i OR x_j). Its falsity forces x_i=0, and every occurrence of x_i is positive. The flip can only help all other clauses, so every clause is deletable by the local rule, in any order. The reduced residual is empty and has width zero; reverse repairs from the zero model give a verified original witness. This overcomes the structural width obstruction on that family by logical transformation, without refuting the all-tree lower bound for the original representation.

For a non-coordinate illustration, take original variables c,a',c',a,b in that order, with extracted equations c=a XOR b, a'=a, c'=c, and residual clauses (a' OR a), (c OR c'). To delete the first clause while preserving the second, in free coordinates a,b the only successful translation toggles both a and b: toggling a alone can change c from true to false, and toggling b alone does not fix the first clause. Gaussian discovery finds this two-coordinate translation. The small example is easy under other mechanisms; it illustrates the expressiveness of the certificate, not a lower bound on competing solvers.
