## 1. New result

Use the full inherited nine-group normalized family with n=19m-7, L=Theta(m^3), arbitrary selected negative cross clauses of widths 1..3 and inherited fixed one-hot/OR/pin/guard clauses. For a support P of k=2 or 3 groups, let A_P be the set of digit tuples satisfying every selected cross clause whose support is contained in P. Let R_P=|A_P|, and define the minimum local allowed density

    rho = min_{P: |P|=2 or 3} R_P/m^|P|.

For every fixed 0<epsilon<=1, all SAT selectors with rho<=m^(-epsilon) eventually have a witness at the original exact cubic stage, uniformly over masks and pins. In particular a single pair with O(m^(2-epsilon)) allowed entries, or a single triple with O(m^(3-epsilon)) allowed entries, suffices (constant factors can be absorbed by reducing epsilon). The condition concerns total allowed entries, not maximum fanout. It allows a complete primal graph, maximal context-conflict count and large forbidden degree.

More precisely, choose a minimizing P, write k=|P|, R=R_P, r=10-k (7 or 8), and fix any positive rational delta<1. A fixed deterministic original three-symbol single-tape machine has runtime

    O((m^6 + m^(9-k)*(R+b)*b^delta) polylog(m+2)),
    b = Theta(m^(3/(r+delta-3))),

and visited span O(m^3 polylog m). If any A_P is empty, halt on blank: the original formula is UNSAT. For the promised density, choose delta<=epsilon/2. Since b<=m and k-epsilon>=1, the bound is O(m^(9-epsilon/2) polylog m)=O(L^(3-epsilon/6) polylog L). The machine may depend on delta (fixed recursion depth), but not on m, masks, pins or chosen support. No unrestricted nine-group cubic cutoff is claimed.

## 2. Exact fusion, including clauses that straddle the fused support

Every original solution restricts to an element of A_P. Order this set lexicographically, and interpret its distinct entries as a new coordinate with domain of size R. All other 9-k coordinates retain domain [0,m). This is an internal combinatorial representation, not an expansion of the actual external U input and not a claim that the fused variable has uniform domain m. Substitution into a selected negative cross clause of support H replaces all original literals in P intersect H by a single fused-coordinate condition when H meets P; the condition can be expanded into a list of negative local clauses, one for each fused digit whose P tuple agrees with all specified digits in P intersect H. Unfused literals are retained. The local support has size |H minus P|+1<=|H|<=3. If H is disjoint from P it retains its original width. This applies even if a clause contains two or three fused groups and even if the projections of different fused digits on a subset of P agree. No functional assumption or inverse lookup is required.

Avoid constructing the global fused selector, which could be much larger than the original. Divide A_P into consecutive chunks of at most b entries; split each unfused domain into consecutive b-wide intervals, with a possibly short last interval. For a tile, label its fused entries by their positions 0..length-1 and use offsets for unfused groups. Emit all translated clauses by the rule above: discard an original clause if any of its unfused digits is outside its tile interval; if it meets P, scan the tile's list of fused entries and emit a local clause for each entry matching its specified P coordinates. If it does not meet P, emit one translated clause. Add negative unary padding clauses for every nonexistent fused digit and out-of-range unfused digit of the uniform local domain [0,b).

The resulting local formula is in exactly the inherited r-group distributed family with n_b=(2r+1)b-r+2, fixed local one-hot/OR/guard clauses and zero local pins. All local selected cross clauses are negative on distinct groups with width at most three. OR-deduplicate repeated translations. Each in-range local tuple maps to exactly one original tuple, with its P coordinates in A_P, and satisfies the local clauses iff its image satisfies the original cross clauses. Proof: an original forbidden conjunction holds at the image exactly when one of its emitted local conjunctions holds, and padding excludes all other local tuples. Conversely, every original model belongs to exactly one tile and has exactly one preimage, since A_P is a list of distinct tuples. The inherited fixed extension is unique in both layouts. Thus the local model sets biject to a disjoint partition of the original model set. Local pins and auxiliary variables are not copied to the original witness; reconstruct the full original assignment from the recovered tuple and actual pins.

## 3. Preprocessing on sequential tapes

Parse the actual normalized unary header and canonical selector positions, extract all selected cross records and actual guarded pin bits, and retain the original selector on a stationary component. The actual clauses fixing the auxiliaries and pins are not part of the latent cross relation. Compute A_P for all 36 pairs and 84 triples: emit one initial record for each P tuple and a forbidden record for every completion to P of every selected clause supported within P. A width-w clause gives m^(k-w) completions, and there are at most O(m^w) such clauses on a fixed support. The total is O(m^3) records across the fixed number of supports. Sort by support and tuple with sequential external merge sorting, OR forbidden flags and complement. Retain the allowed entries in lex order and count R_P. Choose minimum density by comparing integer cross-products; ties use a fixed lex order. Empty tables certify UNSAT. O(m^3 polylog m) multitape bit steps and visited span suffice for these operations. The inherited fixed-track single-tape sweep simulation and initial raw conversion cost O(m^6 polylog m) original transitions. No random-access table or semantic oracle is needed. The chosen allowed stream (at most m^3 entries) and the original clause records remain stationary for the rest of the algorithm.

## 4. Physical tile extraction and local execution

Let b be the least integer >=2 with b^q>=m^p, capped by m, where the fixed rational p/q=3/(r+delta-3). It can be computed with binary arithmetic in the displayed bound. The tile count is

    ceil(R/b) ceil(m/b)^(9-k)
      = O((R+b)*m^(9-k)/b^r).

For one tile, use the inherited mobile carry-shift mechanism on a constant-width encoded product alphabet over the actual physical symbols 0,1,2. Only the bounded mobile work component moves with the stationary scan head; original records and saved pins do not move. First scan the chosen A_P stream to extract the current chunk using a counter, holding at most b tuples of fixed width k. Move the chunk and counters with the scan head along the original cross records. Test the unfused coordinates against their intervals, and scan the b fused entries to emit every matching translation, or emit a single record if H is disjoint from P. Binary fields and counters have O(log m) bits. The fused-entry list, tile metadata and output list occupy O(b^3 polylog m) blocks. To justify this output capacity even BEFORE deduplication, fix any original support H and any local translated clause. If H meets P, its local fused digit identifies one original P entry, and the unfused local digits identify the remaining original digits. Therefore at most one original record of that support generates that local clause. If H misses P the same injectivity follows from interval offsets. There are only constantly many original supports, so the total number of emitted records counting repeats is O(b^3). Padding has O(b) records.

A scan of the stationary O(m^3 polylog m) input (including the allowed stream and rewinds) carrying O(b^3 polylog m) workspace costs O(m^3 b^3 polylog m) physical moves: one-block carry shifts cost linear in the mobile extent; per original record, inspect the bounded fused map sequentially, taking O(b polylog m) work if scanned locally, and append translated records by sequential propagation. Even charging O(b^3 polylog m) per append (including repositioning and copying its record) gives O(b^6 polylog m) over all emitted records; this is dominated by m^3 b^3 since b<=m. The no-match scan can be performed without an output-list traversal per candidate fused digit: keep the fused-entry map contiguous in the mobile header and advance its cursor through it, returning from an append to the marked cursor. Only actual matches incur the charged append cost. All loop counters and record comparisons use at most polylogarithmic overhead. No multiplication by b for every emitted record plus a global outer-input shuttle is assumed.

Construct the complete local raw normalized selector from the translated list using the prior recursive-tiling method: enumerate the canonical local pool and test each position against the bounded list by sequential query propagation, with fixed local clauses/padding generated directly. Cost O(b^6 polylog m). Invoke the earlier recursive-tiling theorem for r=7 or r=8 with a sufficiently large FIXED depth, yielding a fixed local solver of time O(b^(r+delta) polylog b) and visited span O(b^3 polylog b). Incorporate both possible local solvers in the fixed outer finite control. Use separate encoded product components for the local machine's physical symbols, following its head at constant overhead per local step, marking its visited extent for cleanup. The original records, map and tile counters are preserved. On failure clear local workspace and advance. On success recover the local digits, look up the chosen fused entry by a scan of its saved chunk, reinsert unfused offsets, recover the original pins and construct the original n-bit contiguous witness. Per-bit shuttles within the O(m^3 polylog m) span suffice for O(m^4 polylog m) total output time. Halt with head at first witness bit; on exhaustion halt on blank. No extra physical alphabet, head, input-dependent recursion depth or advice is used.

The local solver and construction costs are dominated by O(m^3 b^3+b^(r+delta)) per tile (r>=7). The choice of b balances them up to constants. Multiplying by the tile count gives O(m^(9-k)(R+b)b^delta polylog m) plus preprocessing O(m^6 polylog m). All components are reused and the stationary data plus scratch span O(m^3 polylog m). This proves the stated machine-existence runtime. At stage s=3 ceil(log2 L), the fixed description length ell receives budget 2^(s-ell)>=L^3/2^ell once eligible. With delta<=epsilon/2 the strict saving in L dominates constants, polylogarithmic factors and the fixed description penalty eventually, uniformly on the promise. No numerical threshold or compiled transition table is supplied.

## 5. Strict separation from prior width, fanout, graph, color and sparse-forbidden tests

Let m>=3. Use the four parity triples on groups 0..3 and the five cyclic consecutive triples on groups 4..8 from the earlier controls. On each such triple allow exactly even parity of nonzero indicators, requiring two nonzero digits to be distinct if present. For every pair not contained in any of these triples, orient by increasing group index and forbid exactly those pairs (a,b) for which both a,b are nonzero and

    (b-a) mod (m-1) < floor((m-1)/2).

No other cross clauses. Both zero digits are always compatible on these binary supports. The parity systems force all nine indicators zero (four-group sum and five-group period-three argument from the parent). Hence the all-zero tuple is the unique solution. Every pair appears in a selected cross clause, so the graph is complete. Each parity triple has exactly 1+3(m-1)(m-2) allowed entries; no selected binary clause is supported within it. Consequently rho=O(1/m), and the new theorem applies, e.g. with epsilon=1/2 for all sufficiently large m.

Every group has full local support and pairwise incomparable contextual signatures: on a parity triple use all zero or two distinct nonzero digits plus zero, and the previous context witnesses distinguish all different digits in both directions. On any unconstrained triple or pair, setting all other digits zero satisfies all binary exclusions. Thus the static and contextual pruning retain full width m. Every direction has maximum fanout at least m-2 on a parity triple (one nonzero and one zero parent), and m elsewhere (all-zero parents), so W>=(m-2)^9. For pairs in a parity triple, choose third digit zero: the zero row allows only zero, and each nonzero row allows every nonzero digit except itself. These are m distinct nonempty rows in either order. On any other pair the zero row is full and nonzero rows exclude distinct cyclic intervals of floor((m-1)/2) nonzero digits; these intervals are proper nonempty and their starting points distinguish them, including after transposition. Thus K=m in the minimum over all ordered pairs. Every pair also has a context with at least floor((m-1)/2) forbidden digits for some row (at least m-1 for a parity pair). The previous sparse-forbidden D>=floor((m-1)/2)=Theta(m), even after minimization. This example therefore escapes all preceding structural promises listed above yet is covered by fusion. It is an easy unique control, not a hardness example.

## 7. Disposition

For every fixed epsilon in (0,1], any unbounded sequence of actual cubic SAT misses within this nine-group family must eventually have rho>m^(-epsilon), along with earlier independent necessary conditions. In particular all pair and triple closed local tables must have near-full polynomial exponent in their allowed size. This condition is not sufficient for hardness. Future work should investigate combined sparsity of several overlapping relations, including cases where no single local table is sparse but their small-support join is.
