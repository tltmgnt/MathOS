## Constructive track: guarded width-three resolution closure

Normalize source clauses, keep the first occurrence of each distinct clause (this chooses one sufficient guard, not all possible guards). A record consists of clause C, source occurrence support I and either an original occurrence or two earlier records and a pivot. Resolve C1 containing p with C2 containing not p, omitting tautological resolvents and results exceeding a fixed width w>=3. The new support is I1 union I2. By resolution soundness, the positive indicators of all source clauses in I imply C. Proof induction establishes the same for every record, including empty clauses. One pass resolves only original pairs; the fixed-point variant considers all pairs as new records are appended. Each normalized clause has at most one retained derivation. There are at most D(n,w)=sum_{j=0}^min(w,n) 2^j binom(n,j) distinct clauses. A pair-enumerating closure computes at most O(w D^2) candidate resolutions with polynomial overhead for set normalization, support unions, indexing and output. Each support has at most m entries; the proof DAG is polynomial size for fixed w. Verify records using their earlier parents, exact resolvent and support; independently check saturation by enumerating all relevant pairs in the resulting finite set (or only original pairs for one pass). Completeness here is only closure under the specified bounded-width resolution rule with no weakening, not all implied width-w clauses or all resolution proofs with arbitrary extra axioms. If source duplicates have different indicators, the retained first occurrence is a sufficient but possibly nonminimal guard; no guarantee of complete recovery for every query mode.

Extract complete parity classes from the retained clauses using the earlier verified syntactic extractor. Union their recorded supports to form guarded XOR rows, keeping the immutable original CNF indicator database. Every row is valid under the guard; its activation requires all positive guards among actual pins in the hybrid proof checker. Changing query modes therefore does not invalidate globally learned clauses. The producer and checker use the same deterministic ordered row table, reconstructed from the checked certificate. A hybrid proof step is not inferred from saturation counts alone.

On the previous three-copy cyclic gadget C=A OR l, each private u_j participates in A OR u_j and not u_j OR l. Resolving that pair immediately recovers C, independently of the additional cyclic implications and degree of u_j. Thus one width-three inference round, without knowing private variables, recovers every original parity clause on the split-cube family. All incidence equations are available under all-positive guards; the wrong external root pin yields one verified Gaussian contradiction clause without branching. Arbitrary positive witness construction is not established by the existence of recovered necessary rows.

## Alternative track: exact supplied-interface block projection and gluing

Given a specified set I of interface coordinates, form connected components of the primal graph on private variables (join two private variables when they occur in a common clause). Group every clause with private variables into its component; put interface-only clauses in singleton groups. Each group has a scope consisting of its private coordinates and the interface coordinates it touches. Different groups have disjoint private sets. If every group scope has at most h variables, enumerate its local assignments and retain one witness per feasible interface signature. A global interface assignment extends iff its restrictions belong to every group table: necessity by restriction, sufficiency by assembling the disjoint private witnesses. This also gives a certificate and reconstruction algorithm, costing 2^h poly(L) including discovering components, building tables and verifying all local witnesses. The algorithm refuses a group exceeding h, rather than silently assuming feasibility. The interface is supplied, not automatically discovered or guaranteed small.

For the cyclic hiding family, take the original incidence variables as I. Each private triple is one component with at most three boundary coordinates. ## TECT-X adversarial boundary: fixed-width implication discovery

There is an obstruction even to complete recovery of implied units by bounded-width ordinary resolution. Take an odd-charge bounded-degree Tseitin contradiction T with the external root pinned incorrectly, from the earlier resolution-certificate construction. Introduce a fresh guard h and replace each C by h OR C; split each length-four clause h OR l1 OR l2 OR l3 as (h OR l1 OR u),(not u OR l2 OR l3) with fresh u. Call the normalized 3-CNF Q. It is satisfiable at h=1 (set each u=0) and entails h=1 because the h=0 branch is equivalent to T. If Q has a width-w resolution derivation of the unit h, restrict it by h=0, then substitute u=not l1. The two gadget axioms become a tautology and C, and smaller guarded clauses become their originals. Restriction and literal substitution simulate resolution with no width increase allowing weakening: if a parent image is tautological but the resolvent image is not, the other image is a subclause of the resolvent; satisfied/tautological images are discarded. Thus T would have a width-w refutation (with weakening, which can be removed by tracking subclauses). The earlier Tseitin width lower bound grows with the family, so any fixed w eventually fails. This is a bound for ordinary resolution, not the combined guarded XOR system, which may discover additional algebraic facts. It is not a universal SAT runtime lower bound. The coNP-completeness of complete implied-unit discovery established in the preceding note also remains applicable. No escalation of w can be assumed polynomial if w is unbounded; the explicit D(n,w) must be counted.
