## Hybrid explicit-input domain

Input is a tagged system A of affine GF(2) equations and a 2-CNF B. The tags and original constraints are explicit, not a promise of efficient recognition of arbitrary CNF. Let I be the intersection of their variable sets, X the variables private to A, and Y those private to B. Solve inconsistency of A by Gaussian elimination, retaining the XOR combination of source equations for each derived row. If A is consistent, eliminate X first. The surviving rows with zero X coefficients describe exactly the feasible interface relation R on I. Indeed every model satisfies them, and any assignment of I satisfying them extends by back substitution through the private pivots. All elimination, membership and extension operations take polynomial time in the explicit input length.

Enumerate each nonzero vector on I of Hamming weight at most two. For each vector in the coefficient row space of the projected equations, recover its uniquely determined right hand side and an XOR combination of the original rows. Let E_2 be this list. If rank(E_2)=rank(projected equations), E_2 defines R exactly. The corresponding unary constraints and equalities/disequalities of two bits are expressible by units and binary clauses. Solve B AND E_2 with 2-SAT; if satisfiable, extend its model through A with the interface pinned. This yields an explicit uniform polynomial-time SAT and witness algorithm on the stated domain, without any bound on the number of interface variables, their treewidth, or the number of equations. Rank equality is checkable in polynomial time; if it fails the algorithm returns unsupported, not UNSAT. When the interface has k bits, an unconditional fallback enumerates 2^k pins and applies Gaussian elimination and 2-SAT separately; this is fixed-parameter tractable in k, but not a uniform polynomial bound for arbitrary k.

## Exact expressibility criterion (even with private 2-CNF auxiliaries)

For a nonempty affine Boolean relation R, the following are equivalent:
(1) it has a 2-CNF definition, possibly projected from private auxiliary variables;
(2) R is closed under coordinatewise ternary majority;
(3) its affine coefficient row space is generated by vectors of weight at most two.

(3) implies (1) by the encoding above. (1) implies (2) because 2-CNF models are majority closed, and projection preserves closure. For (2) implies (3), use the pair-seed majority composition already established in F038 witness composition: a majority-closed relation is determined by its unary and pair projections. Specifically for a candidate assignment a agreeing with every pair projection, choose a member of R matching a on each pair. Iterated majority of the three compatible seed/intermediate vectors extends agreement until all coordinates match, so a is in R. Zero or one visible bit is immediate. Projections of an affine relation are affine, and any affine relation on at most two bits is described by its implied unary/binary parity equations (including no equations for the full cube). These equations lifted to I are exactly among E_2. Thus E_2 defines R. For a consistent affine system, equality of solution sets implies equality of coefficient row spaces: the direction space is the orthogonal complement of the row space. Therefore rank equality follows. An empty affine relation is separately detected and expressible by an empty clause. No claim about non-affine relations is necessary.

## Polynomially checkable stronger certificates

On the supported domain an UNSAT certificate is either an XOR combination of original affine rows yielding 0=1, or a list of derived weight-one/two affine equations with XOR provenance plus two directed implication paths in the augmented 2-CNF, from a literal to its complement and back. A verifier checks each XOR combination (including that private coefficients cancel), constructs the binary encoding of the supplied equations, and checks each path edge against a source or derived clause. Soundness does not require verifying the rank-equality condition: implied equations plus a contradictory implication cycle suffice. Completeness on the supported domain follows from the 2-SAT implication-graph criterion. Gaussian elimination, row-span membership, and graph reachability construct these certificates in polynomial time there. This certificate system is stronger than requiring ordinary resolution on the original CNF: the preceding inconsistent Tseitin parity systems have a short XOR provenance certificate, despite their resolution lower bound. For explicit parity CNF input the promised parity groups can be checked and translated as in the preceding research note. No completeness outside the supported hybrid class is asserted.

## Unrestricted mixed-language barrier

Even a conjunction of degree-at-most-three XOR equations and binary clauses can encode arbitrary 3-SAT. For each nonempty clause (l_1 OR ... OR l_s), s<=3, introduce private bits t_1,...,t_s, impose XOR_j t_j=1, pairwise (not t_i OR not t_j), and implications (not t_j OR l_j). The XOR and exclusions force exactly one t_j true; the implications require its literal to be true. Conversely if the source clause is true, choose any true literal and set its indicator to one. Empty clauses can instead be encoded by the inconsistent affine equation 0=1. All new variables are private to their source clause. The construction is linear in source literal count and preserves the source model projection, not merely satisfiability. Hence tagged affine-plus-bijunctive SAT is NP-complete; polynomial algorithms for the isolated affine and isolated 2-CNF modules do not imply a polynomial algorithm for their arbitrary conjunction. This is a self-contained many-one reduction, not a new general dichotomy theorem.

This identifies a conditional obstacle to any polynomial-time equivalence-preserving transformation that always puts arbitrary mixed instances into the rank-equality domain with polynomial output length: such a transformation would imply P=NP. It is not an unconditional lower bound on arbitrary explicit-input algorithms. Nor does the rank criterion characterize every tractable mixed instance.
