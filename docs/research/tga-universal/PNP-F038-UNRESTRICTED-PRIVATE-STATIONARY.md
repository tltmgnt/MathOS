## 3. Reverse the stationary representation

For an active wide branch, do NOT build a stationary six-dimensional exception tree. Instead build a stationary three-dimensional private tree on V, with flags indicating whether its aligned subblock contains any private tuple satisfying all original supports wholly contained in V, including private padding exclusions. This permits completely arbitrary private-only constraints. Keep every other original support as a moving singleton record list (including projected-only supports); their count is O(t^3) per full tile at side t, without any sparsity or compression certificate.

The exception itself is represented in a moving frame at side t. It consists of:

* a dictionary of distinct restricted raw row strings on the local (u,v) square, at most 2t^2 strings of t^2 bits each;
* a map table on the local (g,K) four-coordinate tile, at most t^4 records, each with its four local digits and TWO local row identifiers, one for the original source and one for its actual representative.

All fields inside a frame have O(log(t+2)) bits. Global representative digits are NOT retained inside the recursive worker. The identifiers denote the entire restricted raw row, not just its distance or a selected bit. The row table includes all real source rows at the coarse root, whether or not actually used as source or replacement. At a given tuple the exception predicate is exactly old row bit zero and new row bit one at its two local free digits. Map entries for padded source/context digits are absent. On padded free digits both raw bits are zero, so no exception. Original projected padding units are also retained in the residual list. No approximate row quotient is used.

The moving exception representation, original list and temporary scratch together occupy O(t^4 polylog(t+2)) blocks per frame. Reserve this much at each positive private tree node. Its complete subtree extent at side t is

    O(sum_(s<=t dyadic) (t/s)^3*s^4 polylog(s+2))
      = O(t^4 polylog(t+2)).

Compact zero headers only reduce space. The private subtree need not duplicate the exception relation for all projected choices; its writable frames are reused sequentially.

## 4. Charged exact local exception positivity

A naive scan of t^6 exception candidates with t^4-bit frame queries would be too expensive. Instead compute a local Boolean matrix on row labels:

    D(i,j)=1 iff some local free pair has row_i=0 and row_j=1.

There are O(t^4) entries. For each old row i, copy it into a mobile O(t^2 polylog(t+2))-block scratch component within the current frame. Scan the stationary dictionary in new-label order, comparing each row's bits in aligned order with the old row. A local counter and linear scratch scan per stationary bit are sufficient; carry-shift the mobile component with the dictionary scan. Total dictionary span O(t^4 polylog t) and scratch extent O(t^2 polylog t) imply O(t^6 polylog(t+2)) physical work for one old row. Copy-in, rewind and clearing are within the same bound. The output for this old row has O(t^2) flags and can be appended by per-bit shuttles over the O(t^4 polylog t) frame, also O(t^6 polylog t). Over O(t^2) old rows, total O(t^8 polylog(t+2)) physical work. The completed pair matrix fits the reserved scratch space.

Scan the t^4 map records and test their pair labels against this matrix; even linear sequential scans of the O(t^4 polylog t) matrix for every record, with small mobile query fields, take O(t^8 polylog(t+2)) physical moves. The exception is nonempty EXACTLY when at least one map entry has D(i,j)=1, since the free pair ranges are independent of the four map coordinates within a tile. The positive flag concerns E_H alone, not the strengthened A_H of the preceding semijoin theorem; projected-only constraints remain in the original list. This weaker pruning remains correct and its node census uses |E_H|, which is at most sigma*m^6. No original constraint is dropped. A private zero flag or empty exception prunes the full node.

## 7. Density summation

    sum_t min(M^9/t,R*M^3*t^5)*(1+log(t+2))^A.

## 8. Separation from the preceding guard-semijoin certificate

Let m=q=2^d, digits full binary vectors, with unary zero exclusions. Set g=0,K={1,2,3},H={0,4,5},V={6,7,8}. Keep the earlier main triples {0,i,j} for 1<=i<j<=3 forbidding <a,xi XOR xj>=1, binary inner-product-one forbiddances on pairs among groups 1..8, equality forbiddances on pairs involving 0, and Latin forbiddances x XOR y=z on triples among 1..8 except V. Use the single wide support

    F_H(a,u,v)=1 iff v=a and u!=a.

On V instead forbid tuples with least significant bit of x XOR y XOR z equal to one. Its forbidden density is exactly one half, and every aligned cube of side >=2 contains an allowed tuple. Canonical forbidden-cube count is m^3/2, so every fixed subcubic dyadic cap fails. The private-only constraint is not removed by a semijoin on a nonempty G_H. It is not globally redundant: for sufficiently large d set all groups 1..8 equal to 3 and choose a nonzero a distinct from 3; all other supports pass but V fails. An actual satisfying tuple exists by taking all groups 1..8 equal to 6 and a nonzero digit distinct from 6. Both vectors 3 and 6 have even Hamming weight, and the latter has low bit zero. The primal graph is complete. These are easy SAT controls, not hard instances.

G_H is nonempty for large d: use projected digits x1=x2=x3=6, u=24, v=96 and a=3. The three nonzero vectors 6,24,96 have disjoint supports of weight two. They satisfy the projected binary and Latin constraints; the main triples have zero XOR. The original wide bit is zero, but replacing a by v makes it one. This proves nonempty G_H without requiring that the eventual minimizing map produce that particular exception.

In any free square of side t, rows with a outside the v interval are identically zero, and at most t remaining source digits give distinct rows; hence at most t+1<=2t^2 signatures. The new certificate holds. Isolate zero and the three conditioned digits, then choose one representative per nonempty fiber of the three inner products with the conditioned digits among remaining nonzero sources. At most 12 representatives per context preserve all main triples and conditioned inequalities. The five unconditioned binary inequalities lose at most one partner each with weight m, and every distinct wide replacement loses m-1 free pairs. Thus beta<=12/m, delta_n<=5/m, delta_w<=1/m. The new theorem applies. This example does not require arbitrarily slow actual loss and does not separate from the earlier polynomial-loss algorithm.

For any alternative g,K the earlier lower-bound argument is unchanged: if g=0 but K differs, a main triple has a free other coordinate and distinct nonzero replacements lose m^2/4 weighted entries (one or both other coordinates free); if g>=1 a free binary neighbor among 1..8 gives the same weighted loss. Sublinear capacity therefore cannot yield vanishing full average loss in these alternative configurations.
