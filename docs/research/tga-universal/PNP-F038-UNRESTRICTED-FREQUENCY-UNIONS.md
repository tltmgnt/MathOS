## 1. Result and scope

Use the inherited nine-group normalized selector family with n=19m-7, L=Theta(m^3), raw forbidden support bits F_H of width one through three, and the fixed one-hot/OR/pin/guard extension. Choose compressed group g, three conditioning groups K, and one retained conditioning group j in K. A contextual policy supplies nonempty sets C_c and a map p_c:[0,m)->C_c. All c in [0,m)^K are considered, not only contexts in models. Use the weighted directed losses and wide/narrow split of the parent: wide H={g,u,v} with u,v outside K; all other supports H containing g are narrow. For wide H, Z_H(a,b) is the set of pairs of other digits newly forbidden by replacing a by b; ell_H(a,b)=|Z_H(a,b)|. Narrow losses d_H,c(a,b) use the weight m^(2-|H minus ({g} union K)|). Put

    beta = sum_c |C_c|/m^4,
    delta_n = sum_(c,a,H narrow) d_H,c(a,p_c(a))/m^6,
    delta_w = sum_(c,a,H wide) ell_H(a,p_c(a))/m^6.

These denominators follow from m^4 source/context pairs and weight m^2 per support section.

For retained digit d, source a and representative b define the integer conditional frequency

    N(d,a,b) = #{c: c_j=d, p_c(a)=b}.

For each d,a, sum_b N(d,a,b)=m^2. Let f be a positive integer threshold, and put theta=f/m^2. Call a substitution rare if N(c_j,a,p_c(a))<f. Define gamma_f to be the fraction of source/context pairs that are rare AND have positive loss on some wide support. Define

    U_H(f) = {(d,a,u,v): exists b with N(d,a,b)>=f and (u,v) in Z_H(a,b)},
    lambda_f = sum_(H wide) |U_H(f)|/m^4,
    sigma = beta + delta_n + gamma_f + lambda_f.

The machine enumerates the explicit polynomial-logarithmic-size policy family in Section 3, all 504 choices g,K, three choices j, and dyadic thresholds f=1,2,4,... below m^2 plus m^2. It chooses the minimum computed sigma. There exists a single fixed deterministic original three-symbol single-tape witness program correct throughout this nine-group family, taking, for sufficiently small positive computed sigma,

    O(m^(42/5) polylog(m+2)
      + m^9 sigma^(1/5) (1+log(1/sigma))^A)

physical transitions, with fixed integer A and visited span O(m^3 polylog m). Thus any sequence for which one of these COMPUTABLE policies and a threshold have sigma->0 has eventual exact cubic-stage coverage. No rates, policies, groups or thresholds supplied as advice. The construction is exact outside the promise, with inherited cubic-order time (additive stage slack only).

This theorem is not a greedy approximation guarantee against an arbitrary map optimizing the coupled frequency objective. The score couples contexts, unlike the parent's separable objective. The finite policy family and the sufficient comparison condition below are essential limitations. The previous threshold-union theorem remains a separate available description in U.

## 2. Exact recovery and frequency-weighted bound

Let P={g} union K and let A0 on P be the union of representative membership and all rare source/context pairs having positive wide loss. Every map used below minimizes a nonnegative directed loss containing all supports over its selected set; a representative can map to itself at zero loss, so no representative has positive wide loss under a minimizing map. Hence the envelope density is exactly beta+gamma_f. For each narrow H define the exact exception E_H on H union K by old raw bit zero and substituted raw bit one. Its normalized density summed over narrow H is delta_n, by the weighting identity of the parent. These supports have widths four and five.

For each wide H impose a separate allowed relation U_H(f) on H union {j}, of width four. All branches keep every original constraint. Given an original satisfying tuple, if replacement succeeds its image is a model in A0. If replacement fails and the source/context pair is rare with some positive wide loss, the original model is in A0. Otherwise a wide failure implies membership in U_H(f) because its actual representative has frequency at least f. A failure only on narrow supports belongs to E_H. Conversely every branch model is original. At most 38 branches; SAT and witness existence preserved exactly, not necessarily the full model set. No width-six clause is inserted in the external normalized selector. All branches are searched sequentially.

A useful deterministic bound is

    f |U_H(f)| <= sum_(d,a,b) N(d,a,b) ell_H(a,b).

Indeed union cardinality is at most the sum of cardinalities of all qualifying difference sets, and each qualifying pair has frequency at least f. Hence lambda_f<=delta_w/theta. Thus a computed policy with beta,delta_n,gamma_f->0 and delta_w/theta->0 satisfies the promise even without an exact-union estimate. The exact score may be much better than this bound. In particular sparse frequency-conditioned palettes can make the union small even when a context-free union is dense.

## 4. Generate frequencies and exact score with original-scale span

Inherit the three-context factorized distance computation from the previous notes: for each support H containing g cache contribution tables for |H intersect K|<=1, of at most m^3 entries, and raw bit tables for |H intersect K|=2. The full wide ell tables have m^2 entries each. Initial parsing of canonical bits and guarded pins, sorting and aligned row comparison cost O(m^4 polylog m) sequential multitape bit work and O(m^3 polylog m) span, thus O(m^7 polylog m) physical time by the fixed-track sweep simulation.

For each full context, the inherited mobile O(m^2 polylog m)-block workspace extracts matching slices from stationary original-scale tables in O(m^5 polylog m) physical moves. Form its matrix by aligned sequential addition and select the map by either greedy facility selection or palette minimum scans. These require at most O(m^3 polylog m) local bit steps within O(m^2 polylog m) span, so local sweep overhead also gives O(m^5 polylog m) moves. The palette membership uses only fixed binary arithmetic. Accumulate exact beta and narrow weighted loss by binary counters. Copy the O(m polylog m) map output within original span by per-bit shuttles, O(m^4 polylog m) moves per context. Clear local work. There is no global m^4 map.

Order contexts by retained digit d first. For fixed d there are m^2 contexts; emit their m^3 records (a,b) to a stationary spool, then sort and count duplicates on fixed sequential tapes, adding the d field. This produces the frequency slice N(d,a,b), with at most m^2 entries. Append it to the saved global frequency table of at most m^3 records. Even charging the full O(m^6 polylog m) physical sweep for each d's sort and append gives O(m^7 polylog m), dominated by the O(m^8 polylog m) map generation. Discard the context spool per d. Keep only one policy's frequency table at a time, and keep only the winning policy identifiers and score after evaluating it. Recompute the winning frequency table once if needed. All fields are O(log m) bits; total span O(m^3 polylog m).

For a given f, compute gamma_f by merging the frequency records with wide count tables to mark positive-loss pairs. Sum N(d,a,b) for those with N<f and positive wide loss. This is exactly the number of rare positive-loss source/context pairs, not the number of rare distinct representatives. Missing frequency entries are zero. To compute |U_H(f)| exactly, enumerate (d,a,u,v,b) in lexicographic order with b innermost. There are m^5 records. Process batches of O(m^3) records, merge sorted (d,a,b) queries against frequencies, then merge two raw support queries against original raw bits (old a and new b), retaining candidate IDs and old/new flags, and restore lex order. OR the conjunction of frequency>=f, old zero and new one over b for each projected tuple, retaining a partial OR across batch boundaries. Count true flags; never store a whole width-four table at original scale. A batch costs O(m^3 polylog m) sequential bit steps with that span, including scans of the stationary frequency/raw tables and sorting. There are O(m^2) batches, so time O(m^5 polylog m) bit steps and O(m^8 polylog m) physical moves by the inherited sweep simulation. The constant supports, coordinate choices and logarithmic policy/threshold loops preserve O(m^8 polylog m) total physical preprocessing. No free indexed queries or growing number of tapes.

## 6. Separation: dense context-free unions, sparse retained-coordinate palettes

Let q=2^d, m=q-1, positive digit numerals the distinct nonzero vectors in GF(2)^d. Use the earlier three-context algebraic control: forbid <x0,xi+xj>=1 for triples {0,i,j}, 1<=i<j<=3; forbid <x0,x1> != <x1,xj> for j=4..8; forbid inner product one for binary pairs among groups 1..8. Add the genuinely ternary wide support H={0,4,5} with

    F_H(a,u,v)=1 iff v=a and u!=a.

The graph is complete and the tuple all equal to vector 3 satisfies every constraint (weight two, self inner product zero, and u=a). This is an easy SAT control, not asserted unique or hard.

For distinct a,b the wide loss is exactly m-1: v=b, u!=b. Hence every nontrivial replacement incurs positive wide loss; the previous context-free threshold union is empty below r=m-1, but at r>=m-1 has exactly m*(m-1)^2 entries, normalized density tending to one. For the designated g=0,K={1,2,3}, any sublinear capacity therefore makes either the heavy fraction or the union density bounded away from zero. More generally the previous threshold-union promise fails for every coordinate choice: for g>=1 a free binary neighbor among 1..8 gives narrow weighted loss mq/4 on every distinct replacement. For g=0 and a different K, choose a main triple among 1,2,3 with at least one free coordinate. If exactly one is free its narrow loss is >=m*(q/4-1) for every distinct pair at every context. If both are free its wide loss is exactly q(q-2)/4 for all distinct pairs: the q/4 nonzero sums w with <a,w>=0,<b,w>=1 each have q-2 ordered nonzero decompositions. A threshold below that loss makes every distinct pair heavy; one at or above includes at least that many pairs in each source row of the union, giving nonvanishing density. Other selected raw supports cannot reduce these losses. Thus no previous threshold-union score can vanish on this family.

For the new policy let g=0,K={1,2,3}, retain j=1, and choose s=2^floor(d/2). The aligned palette of positive numerals of the retained digit x1 has fixed high bits and free low bits of dimension floor(d/2), except exclusion of vector zero. For uniformly varying nonzero conditioning vectors x1,x2,x3, the three linear forms on those low bits are independent except on O(1/s) of contexts: for each of seven nonzero combinations, after fixing the other context vectors the low bits of one independent uniform nonzero vector must equal a prescribed value, probability at most q/(s(q-1))<=2/s. Union bound gives at most 14/s. This argument concerns all full contexts, not a distribution of satisfying assignments. On a good context the map from low bits to the three inner products is onto; each fiber has size s/8, and for s>=16 excluding zero cannot empty it. The palette therefore contains a representative for every triple of source inner-product bits. Such a replacement preserves ALL narrow constraints involving g and loses at most m-1 wide pairs. On bad contexts the full weighted loss per source is at most 37m^2. The computed minimum-distance palette map has at most this competing loss; hence delta_n+delta_w=O(1/s+1/m) and beta<=s/m. At threshold f=1 no actual substitution is rare. For each d,a the projected union over all palette b has at most s*(m-1) entries in the u,v domain. Thus lambda_f<=s/m. All other wide sections are empty. The selected score is O(s/m+1/s)=O(m^(-1/2)), so the derived charged runtime is O(m^(89/10) polylog m)=O(L^(89/30) polylog L). The previous threshold-union theorem fails on this family but the new policy succeeds. This is a separation of stated sufficient conditions, NOT a computational hardness result or a claim that retaining a coordinate is necessary for every conceivable context-free map-dependent union algorithm.
