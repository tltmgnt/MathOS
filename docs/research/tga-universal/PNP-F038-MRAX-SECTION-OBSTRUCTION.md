## Constructive track: a section-preserving representation obstruction

Let R be a Boolean relation on d source coordinates. Each source coordinate i has a disjoint finite block of Boolean code bits, a Boolean decoder d_i defined on the codewords that occur, and two fixed codewords c_i(0),c_i(1) decoded to 0,1. Consider any conjunction of four-operation-typed Boolean relations over the code bits and any number of additional existential bits, with one consistent typing across occurrences. Let S be its exact existential projection onto the code bits. Assume (1) decoding every member of S yields a member of R (soundness), and (2) for every r in R, the tuple of fixed representatives (c_i(r_i)) belongs to S (common section). The second condition is stronger than surjectivity of the decoder on S; it does not demand that every choice of representatives lift.

Conjunction preserves a common coordinatewise polymorphism, and existential projection also preserves it: choose a full witness for each input row, apply the common operation on the entire assignment, then restrict. Thus S is closed under the inherited typed operation on code bits. For each i apply its coordinatewise block operation to the three fixed representative codewords corresponding to a,b,c and decode the result. Symmetry and idempotence ensure that this defines a symmetric idempotent ternary Boolean operation on the source coordinate. If a mixed-weight output codeword never occurs in S and the decoder is partial there, assign its value arbitrarily; any triple of rows of R with that weight would yield that codeword in S, so the arbitrary case is never used in the closure proof. The four operations AND, OR, majority and minority (XOR) exhaust these Boolean operations, since their values on weight one and weight two determine them. Applying the block operation to the section lifts of any three R rows and decoding yields a member of R by soundness. Hence R itself has a compatible four-operation typing.

This proves an unconditional no-go theorem for section-preserving typed representations of any untypable relation, with arbitrary auxiliary count, block length or computational budget. Direct existential extension with exposed source bits, coordinate negation, duplication, injective fixed two-codeword encodings, and complete inverse-image encodings are included. Arbitrary changes of global coordinates, context-dependent encodings without a section, disjunctions of different representations, and reductions preserving only satisfiability rather than a local section are not covered. The theorem is not a lower bound on arbitrary SAT algorithms.

For NAE_3, a direct proof of non-typability avoids relying on finite tests. Write a_i,b_i for the values of the operation on coordinate i at weights one and two. For any distinct i,j, take triples of NAE rows with the remaining coordinate pinned to zero, and the i,j pair equal to (1,0),(0,1),(0,1). Preservation forbids a_i=b_j=0. With remaining coordinate pinned to one the same argument forbids a_i=b_j=1. Thus a_i != b_j for every i != j. With three coordinates this forces all a_i to coincide and all b_i to be their complement. Applying the common operation to the three unit vectors gives a constant row, forbidden in NAE. Hence no typing exists.

## Quantitative extension: finite covers by section-preserving charts

Let R_m be the direct product of m independent NAE triples, with 6^m rows. A chart is a relation contained in R_m with a typed section-preserving representation; different charts may use entirely different typings, sections and auxiliaries. Each chart is itself typable by the theorem, so its projection onto every triple is typable (existential projection preserves closure). Since a full NAE projection is impossible, each has at most five rows. Every chart contains at most 5^m rows. An exact cover of R_m therefore needs at least ceil((6/5)^m) charts.

Every five-row subset of NAE admits a compatible typing: for example NAE minus the pattern of packed integer 6 is {1,2,3,4,5} (coordinate 0 is the least significant bit) and is closed under OR,OR,AND on coordinates 0,1,2, checked by the mixed-operation truth table on these five points. Coordinate permutations and complementing all bits act transitively on the six nonconstant patterns and transform OR/AND accordingly, proving the other five cases. Thus products of arbitrary five-row subsets are valid charts, described by m local tables. If each chart omits one uniformly random pattern per block independently, a fixed point is covered with probability (5/6)^m. A union bound proves existence of a cover with at most ceil((m ln 6+1)(6/5)^m) charts. This is a nonconstructive size upper bound, not a polynomial enumeration algorithm.

The cover problem reduces exactly to covering the six-symbol cube by products of five-symbol alphabets: extend every chart's projections to size five, and their product is a valid chart containing it. Any k<=m such products leave a point uncovered: assign a distinct coordinate to each chart and set that coordinate to its omitted symbol. For m<=5, m+1 products omitting the same distinct symbol on every coordinate cover the cube, because a point uses at most m distinct symbols. Hence the minimum chart count is exactly m+1 for 1<=m<=5, and exponential asymptotically by the volume bound. These are representation-size bounds for the specified cover semantics. They do not bound implicit representations, arbitrary branching strategies or deciding satisfiability of these easy product instances.

## Alternative discovery: explicit nonlinear decoding escapes the section hypothesis but fails compositional gluing

Use a four-bit codeword t=(u,v,s0,s1), packed with u and v at bits 0,1. Interpret s=(s0+2s1). Define a single common decoder d(t)=u for s=0 or 3, v for s=1, and u XOR v for s=2. Consider three disjoint code blocks x,y,z and the affine relation y=x XOR 1, z=x XOR 2 (the constants are four-bit masks). It has exactly sixteen rows. At s=0 or 3 the decoded triples are (u,not u,u); at s=1 they are (v,v,not v); at s=2 they are (u XOR v,not(u XOR v),not(u XOR v)). These are all six NAE patterns and no others. Each local code relation is representable using binary equalities/disequalities and is minority-closed, with a consistent all-XOR typing. Thus an affine representation with a nonlinear decoder can have exactly the untypable NAE as its decoded image. This refutes an overly broad assertion that all decodable images of typable relations must be typable. It does not contradict the section theorem: fixing a first-block codeword fixes the entire code tuple. Two representatives for the first source bit can cover at most two decoded patterns, not all six, irrespective of the other blocks' representatives.

Using the same local gadget for NAE(x,y,z) and NAE(x,z,w) with shared code blocks, the first constraint requires z=x XOR 2 and the second z=x XOR 1, inconsistent. The Boolean conjunction is satisfiable (for example (0,1,1,0)). Local existential decoding surjectivity therefore fails to guarantee global gluing. This is an exact counterexample to the specified gadget, not to all nonsection encodings.

There is a further compositional obstruction for fixed uniform typed local gadgets with a common code domain, decoder and operation type tuple for all source variable blocks. Suppose a gadget is sound under decoding and its reuse preserves satisfiability for every conjunction of NAE atoms, allowing repeated variable arguments. Use two variables X,Y and all six NAE atoms whose arguments are X/Y and not all identical. This source has a satisfying assignment iff X!=Y. Any satisfying common-code lift supplies two codewords decoded to opposite bits. Every one of the six NAE patterns then has the section lift using those same two codewords (the six atoms are all ordered mixed tuples). By the section theorem the gadget cannot exist. This conclusion assumes the same typed block structure and local relation on each occurrence; it does not address transformations that change code semantics or typing per occurrence, global preprocessing, or nonlocal witness reconstruction.
