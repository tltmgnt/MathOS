## 1. Result

Use the inherited nine-group normalized family, n=19m-7, L=Theta(m^3), arbitrary selected negative cross clauses of widths 1..3 and fixed one-hot/OR/pin/guard clauses. For k in {5,6,7,8}, let R_k be the minimum, over k-group subsets P, of the number of tuples satisfying all selected cross constraints supported in P. Write rho_k=R_k/m^k.

For each fixed k there is a fixed deterministic original three-symbol one-tape witness program correct for the entire family. Put e=max(6,k)+1/2. For R_k>=1 it has time

    O(m^e R_k^((9-e)/k) polylog(m+2))

and visited span O(m^3 polylog(m+2)). If R_k=0, time O(m^e polylog(m+2)) suffices. In particular, for every fixed 0<epsilon<=k, R_k<=m^(k-epsilon) gives time O(L^(3-epsilon*(9-e)/(3*k)) polylog L), strictly below cubic. Thus ANY polynomial density saving on ANY proper subset of size 5..8 suffices for eventual exact cubic-stage success, without materializing the join or choosing its minimizing support. Together with earlier pair/triple/four-group cutoffs, this covers polynomial sparsity on any support of size 2..8. The program need not know epsilon or R_k.

An additional consequence of the spatial stack is an unconditional O(m^9)=O(L^3) bound for this nine-group family, without the polylogarithmic multiplier, on a single fixed original machine. This gives coverage at stage 3 ceil(log2 L)+c for some fixed nonnegative integer c, NOT necessarily stage 3 ceil(log2 L). No constant or description length small enough for that exact stage is established. This is family-specific, not a general SAT algorithm or P vs NP result.

## 2. Dyadic partition and sound pruning

Let M be the least power of two >=m (so m<=M<2m). Add negative unary exclusions for padded digits m..M-1 in every group. The padded relation is internal; the actual external U input and output remain unchanged. Every original tuple has a unique padded-domain representation and all additional digits are excluded.

A node at scale t, a power of two, represents one product of nine intervals of length t in [0,M), and stores precisely the selected cross records whose specified digits lie in the corresponding intervals, translated to local digits in [0,t). Padding exclusions are translated in exactly the same way. A record not retained is satisfied throughout the node's tile. Hence local satisfying tuples biject to original satisfying tuples in that tile.

At t>=2, test every k-group subset Q (there are at most 126 for k>=5). For each, retain only local records supported inside Q, relabel groups to consecutive indices, and check whether this induced relation is satisfiable. For k=5 use a six-group instance with one unconstrained dummy digit group; for k>=6 use the k-group instance itself. The fixed local one-hot/OR/guard clauses and zero pins implement exactly that induced relation, and the dummy group has no effect on existence. Use the inherited fixed-depth recursive tiling solver, which for each fixed r>=6 and positive rational delta gives time O(t^(r+delta) polylog(t+2)), span O(t^3 polylog(t+2)). Choose delta=1/2, or a smaller fixed depth exponent if the preceding theorem is stated with strict inequality. This supplies an exact SAT checker: on success read the valid local witness; on failure the solver halts on blank/malformed output. No stage clock is imposed on this internal simulation. Generate the local normalized selector from the node records and directly generated fixed bits by sequential per-position scans, at O(t^6 polylog(t+2)) physical cost, included in e. The count of checks is fixed independent of t.

If any test is UNSAT, the full tile is UNSAT and may be pruned. Otherwise enumerate the 2^9 children in fixed lex order, halving each coordinate. For a child with half-width h=t/2 and bit vector d, keep a record exactly when every specified local digit lies in [d_g*h,(d_g+1)*h); subtract d_g*h. This is injective for each support and produces O(h^3) records. At t=1 there is only the all-zero local tuple; directly check every local record (any selected record forbids it). No projection checker or dummy group is needed there. All successful leaves map to original in-range tuples. A valid original model survives every projection test on its unique ancestral path. Consequently this depth-first search is sound and complete on every input in the inherited family, with no sparsity promise required for correctness. It can halt on the first model. On success reconstruct the digits by adding offsets as the stack unwinds, then reconstruct the original auxiliary and pin bits; on failure halt on blank.

## 3. Fixed physical frame stack (not a growing product alphabet)

A central difference from the earlier fixed-depth tiling constructions is that the recursion is implemented as a spatial stack of frames, not by recursively nesting new track alphabets. The following resource details are essential to the O(m^9) conclusion.

Use one fixed finite product alphabet, represented by constant-width binary blocks over the actual physical symbols 0,1,2 (all-2 unoccupied blocks). Components include frame-boundary and cursor markers, local record storage, counters, scratch and the physical symbols of a fixed local solver. The same components are reused in spatially disjoint intervals at every depth. Fixed control runs an iterative push/process/pop dispatcher, and the current frame is determined by boundary markers. There is no input-dependent number of tracks, no extra physical tape head and no globally indexed stack address.

For a scale t frame reserve a contiguous interval of B(t)=O(t^3 (1+log(t+2))^a) blocks, for a sufficiently large FIXED integer a and constant coefficient covering all the local lists, local normalized selector and checker span. The local checker's fixed machine and bound are inherited; its raw simulated tape origin can be centered in reserved scratch with margins on both sides, since a visited interval containing its starting cell has the stated bound. Mark the occupied extents and clear only local scratch after each check. Each checker transition is simulated by a constant number of physical block operations following its own head, not by a sweep over the stack. The frame's preserved records and metadata are on other components. Its setup, raw selector conversion and cleanup fit O(t^6 polylog(t+2)) moves, with binary counters and per-bit shuttles only within the frame. There is a fixed choice of capacity constants large enough for the finitely many inherited local solvers and routines. The visited-span bound is not used as a claim that all scratch is occupied.

All fields in a nonroot frame use LOCAL scale t: the local domain size, digit indices, list lengths, child-choice bits, counters for frame capacity and relative positions. Each needs O(log(t+2)) bits (including polynomial-in-t capacity counters). No global m-bit address, absolute offset, global log m field, or root header lookup is needed at a leaf. Store only the nine-bit child-choice vector at the parent; when a child succeeds its returned local digits are converted by adding the parent's half-width times those bits. Original m and actual pins are preserved outside the root frame, read only for final reconstruction. The finite control contains the same routines at all scales. The fixed degree capacity function can be computed from local binary counters; scanning and creating its delimiters by repeated local counter operations is within O(B(t)^2 polylog(t+2)) moves.

To push a child, translate the records within the parent's bounded interval; even a simple sequential scan and per-record shuttles across that interval cost O(t^6 polylog(t+2)) (there are O(t^3) records and each takes O(log(t+2)) bits). Reserve the child's interval immediately after the parent's end marker and copy its translated list and local header there. Parent loop state, including the current child index, remains stationary. All accesses during push are within the two adjacent intervals, of combined length O(B(t)); no traverse to the root. On completion the child has no live descendants. To pop on failure clear the child's own marked frame and return across the adjacent delimiter; recover the parent header by a scan of its interval, increment the saved child index and reuse the child region. On success transfer the O(log(t+2))-bit local digit vector into the parent's scratch before clearing the child, and add the saved offsets. The number of children is constant; all pushes, pops, initialization, erasure, returns, and success propagation charged to a scale-t node cost O(t^6 polylog(t+2)). Because each recursive call completes and removes its descendants before returning, cleanup does not require a scan over all previously explored nodes. This is an ordinary fixed iterative stack machine on a physical tape, with adjacent-frame shuttles explicitly charged. The stack's maximal spatial extent is the sum of capacities along a halving path, O(M^3 polylog(M+2)), not that times the number of nodes. A constant number of root stationary components adds the same order.

Initial parsing of the actual raw normalized input, record extraction, preserving pins and padding the root use the inherited sequential-bit preprocessing and sweep simulation, O(m^6 polylog(m+2)) physical steps. Local fields can be initialized from the root's M, not from an external advice string. The final witness is written contiguously in an unencoded raw region and the actual head positioned at its first bit, costing O(m^4 polylog(m+2)) by shuttles. These one-time global operations need not be repeated for each frame. One finite machine description implements all of this; its length is independent of m and recursion depth.

## 4. Resource bound by occupied join blocks

Fix a minimizing k-support P for the analysis only. The algorithm tests every k-support at each nonleaf; it does not compute R_k or identify P. At scale t, the P-coordinate intervals form a disjoint partition into (M/t)^k blocks. A block containing a satisfying assignment of P must contain one of the R_k distinct original allowed P tuples, since padding units are included. Thus there are at most min((M/t)^k,R_k) such blocks. The other 9-k coordinates have (M/t)^(9-k) possible block choices. Every node that passes all tests has a nonempty P block, so the number of passing nodes is at most

    min((M/t)^9, R_k*(M/t)^(9-k)).

Every visited nonroot node has a passing parent at scale 2t, and each such parent has exactly 512 possible children. Therefore the number of visited nodes at scale t, including rejected nodes, is at most 512 times the same expression with t replaced by 2t. This is O(min((M/t)^9,R_k*(M/t)^(9-k))) and also covers the single root for R_k>=1. For R_k=0 the root rejects immediately, after at most the fixed number of projection checks. Stopping at the first witness can only reduce the count.

Charge O(t^e polylog(t+2)) per node, including all frame operations, projection checks and child management; the leaf cost is constant. For R=R_k>=1, the two terms of the node-count bound cross at t0=M/R^(1/k), which lies in [1,M]. Ignoring logarithms temporarily, total work is bounded by the sum over dyadic t of

    min(M^9*t^(e-9), R*M^(9-k)*t^(e-(9-k))).

For t>=t0 use the first term (e<9); for t<=t0 use the second (e>9-k). Both are geometric series dominated by the terms at the crossover up to constant factors, including when t0 is not dyadic. Evaluating either term there yields M^e R^((9-e)/k). Restoring a common polylog(M+2) upper bound gives the announced time. The root preprocessing is dominated since e>=6.5. Substitution of R<=m^(k-epsilon) gives exponent 9-epsilon*(9-e)/k in m. The resulting normalized L exponents, for density saving epsilon=1, are:

    k=5: 3-1/6; k=6: 3-5/36; k=7: 3-1/14; k=8: 3-1/48.

For general epsilon multiply each saving by epsilon. Any strict power saving dominates polylogarithmic factors and the fixed description penalty: at j=ceil(log2 L), the fixed program of length ell is eligible eventually and receives 2^(3j-ell)>=L^3/2^ell transitions. Thus the exact cubic inclusion is uniform over all inputs with the corresponding density promise, pins included. No numerical threshold is asserted.

## 5. Unconditional cubic order, not exact cubic clock

Without any pruning promise the number of nodes at scale t is at most (M/t)^9. KEEP the logarithmic cost local to t, rather than bounding it by log M in every frame. For any fixed exponent a' from the per-node implementation,

    sum_(t=1,2,4,...,M) (M/t)^9*t^e*(1+log(t+2))^a'
      <= C*M^9,

because for t=2^h the residual series is geometric 2^(-(9-e)*h) times a fixed polynomial in h, and converges. The one-time root parsing and final reconstruction are also O(m^9). In particular a fixed choice k=5, with e=6.5, solves the whole nine-group family in O(m^9) original transitions. The same conclusion follows by omitting projection checks and using the local child translation/leaf test alone; it is the local-scale spatial stack and amortized geometric sum, not any assertion of universal strict subcubic speed, that removes the logarithmic loss. This improves the earlier family-wide fixed-depth O(m^(9+delta) polylog m) bound. No input-dependent nesting of alphabets or uncharged stack movement is used.

Since L=Theta(m^3), let C' be a fixed constant bounding the actual transition count by C'*L^3 for sufficiently large L. If the fixed description length is ell, choose fixed integer c>=max(0,ell+ceil(log2 C')). Then stage 3j+c allocates at least C'*L^3 transitions. This proves constant additive stage slack, not exact stage 3j. The constants may be very large; neither ell nor C' is computed or optimized here. It does not imply all nine-group SAT inputs succeed at exact cubic, an unbounded failure sequence, or any result about general NP languages. The word "cubic" in the unconditional bound is asymptotic, not a claim of meeting the exact universal allocation.

## 6. Dense four-way joins, sparse five-way join

For m a multiple of 8, m>=16, on every pair i<j forbid exactly those pairs with (x_j-x_i) mod m in [m/4,m/2). On triple (0,1,2) require x2=x1 only when x0 is even; on (0,3,4) require x4=x3 only when x0 is odd. These are selected negative ternary clauses; no other cross constraints. The graph is complete and the all-zero tuple is allowed.

On P={0,1,2,3,4}, exactly one of the two equality requirements is active for any fixed x0. Thus the join contains at most m^4 tuples, even before binary exclusions. Yet every support Q of size 2..4 contains at most one entire special triple, as their union has size five. Let I=[0,m/8). Any tuple with all digits in I satisfies the binary exclusions. If Q contains one special triple, choose the parity of x0 for which the requirement is inactive; at least |I|/4 digits have that parity since |I|>=2. Hence each such join has at least |I|^|Q|/4 entries. This also holds if no special triple is contained. All joins through size four have constant-order density, while rho_5<=1/m. This separates the new density promise from the earlier density promises. It is not a uniqueness or hardness construction, and it may satisfy other independent cutoff tests. Its all-zero witness is easy.

## 8. Disposition

The proper-subset polynomial-sparsity frontier now reaches eight groups. Any unbounded sequence of actual exact-cubic misses in this family must eventually have rho_k>m^(-epsilon) for every fixed epsilon>0 in the applicable ranges and k=2..8, in addition to earlier necessary conditions. The remaining gap for the family-wide O(L^3) bound is the exact fixed-description allocation, not an asymptotic polynomial exponent.
