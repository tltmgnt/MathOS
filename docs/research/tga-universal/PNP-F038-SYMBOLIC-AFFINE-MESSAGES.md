## Symbolic representation and exact operations

Work with the affine parameter direction spaces and checked clause tree of the preceding two-pass semantic repair theorem. Represent a boundary relation on S by a finite union of affine flats of linear functionals on S. Each flat is specified by a consistent reduced system of binary linear equations whose coefficient rows lie in S (represented in ambient coordinates). The empty union is the empty relation; the union containing the empty equation system is the universal relation. All such representations extend freely off S.

To join two pieces, combine their equations and eliminate. If inconsistent, discard. Otherwise restrict to the requested output boundary T, which is contained in the sum of the input boundaries. For a consistent system E on the ambient parameter vector, let K be its coefficient row space and t0 a solution. The exact restriction of its solution relation to T consists of the functionals satisfying equations k(t)=k(t0) for a basis of K intersect T. Necessity is immediate; for sufficiency, glue the specified functional on T to the fixed functional on K, which agree on their intersection, then extend to the ambient vector space. This is polynomial linear algebra per pair. Union distributes over intersection and projection, so pairwise joins give exact union messages. The previous inside/outside gluing proof applies without change. At a leaf, enumerate at most 2^ell signatures (ell<=3 for normalized 3-CNF), encode each allowed signature as a point flat, and project. Obtain exact leaf projections by testing each allowed signature against the outside union. The translation predicates Q_i are then computed by the previous formula and passed through the same symbolic upward recurrence.

Two exact compression operations are implemented. If flat F is contained in G, remove F; check containment by canonicalizing the conjunction and comparing with F. If two distinct flats have the same coefficient row space, they are parallel disjoint cosets. Over GF(2) their union is itself a flat: choose a row whose right-hand sides differ, drop it, and XOR it into every other row whose right-hand sides differ, so the retained equations express the codimension-one kernel of the difference functional. Canonicalize. Each subsumption or merge decreases the number of pieces, and a fixed deterministic ordering makes the operation reproducible. This is a heuristic for compactness but an exact transformation, not a claim to compute a minimum affine cover. At leaves and joins the implementation only enumerates pieces and local signatures, not boundary assignments.

Let P be the maximum number of pieces in any *raw* intermediate union or retained union in the three passes, counting the number of pair trials as at most the product of two retained list sizes. Each projection, canonicalization, containment and merge costs polynomial time in explicit input length L; repeated pair scans require at most polynomial(P) operations. Thus total cost and certificate verification are poly(L,P,2^ell), independent of b except through P and polynomial matrix dimensions. This is an output/intermediate-sensitive upper bound, not a promise that P is polynomial. Store canonical union lists, exact leaf projection/predicate sets, and the final upward lists. The checker recomputes the deterministic local recurrence from supplied neighbors, including normalization, rather than attempting arbitrary equivalence testing of alternative union representations. Nonempty translation root yields a direction by top-down selecting compatible pieces and local signature points, then solving the combined leaf equations. The final direction is checked against all Q_i. Empty root certifies that no translation in this class exists. This extends the previous explicit tables, not arbitrary semantic repair functions.

## High-rank compact family

For m>=1 take independent pairs a_i,b_i. Put both clauses (a_i OR b_i) and (a_i OR not b_i) in each of two identical copies of the formula; place each pair of clauses in sibling leaves, combine the m groups in each half, and join the two halves at the root. Neither sign pair is a complete width-two parity class, so explicit affine extraction leaves them residual. Each half spans all 2m coordinate directions and the separator rank at the half is 2m. Its exact message is the affine flat a_i=1 for all i, with b_i free. Thus an explicit table has 2^m rows, while the symbolic union contains a single flat with m equations. Each local clause starts with three point pieces which compress to at most two. Joining the two clauses of a group yields the line a_i=1 (merge two points if necessary). Joins between completed groups preserve a single flat. Add a fresh c and the unique clause (not a_1 OR c) as a sibling of the entire two-copy tree. This does not change the rank-2m cut. On negating that candidate for semantic repair, the bad models have a_i=1,c=0 and arbitrary b_i. The translation flipping c repairs every bad model. It is a nonvacuous repair instance. The executable tests compact upward messages on the duplicate halves and full symbolic repair/certificate execution on the augmented formula. There is no claim that explicit tables are required by every algorithm or that this easy family is a SAT hardness example.

## Exponential affine-union representation obstruction

For each of m independent triples use NAE, encoded by the positive triple and the negative triple, and duplicate the entire conjunction across the two halves of a clause tree. Both parity classes are incomplete, so the chosen extraction leaves all clauses. The shared boundary of the halves has rank 3m, and the exact feasibility relation is the product of m six-point NAE sets. Any affine flat contained in this product projects onto an affine flat contained in each local six-point set. Such a local flat has at most four points because its size is a power of two and the full cube is not contained. Hence each global flat has at most 4^m points: the projection tuple determines every coordinate. Every piece in an exact union is individually contained in the target, so at least ceil((3/2)^m) pieces are necessary to cover its 6^m points. This is true for all affine-union representations, regardless of basis or merge strategy. A cover of size O(m(3/2)^m) exists: independently choose one of the three local pair-inequality planes for each triple; every NAE point belongs to two of three, so a product plane covers each point with probability (2/3)^m. A union bound on the probability of missing any of the 6^m points proves the claimed cover size. The lower bound is specific to representing this exact message as a union of affine flats, not to arbitrary circuits, factored representations, alternative trees or SAT runtime. A product of local NAE predicates is already a compact circuit.
