## Constructive theorem: exact restriction of a universal rejection statement

For n explicitly indexed variables, let U_n be the ordered pool of all distinct normalized non-tautological clauses of width at most 3, including the empty clause. Its size is sum_{j=0}^min(n,3) 2^j binom(n,j)=O((n+1)^3). A bit vector p selects a subset F_p of this pool. Every normalized 3-CNF is represented after removing duplicates and renaming occurring variables. Conversion and decoding are polynomial in explicit input length. Take n in unary, or dense index size bounded by explicit encoding length. Padding by unused variables does not affect satisfiability.

For any deterministic program M operating on this pool-vector encoding, and unary clock t, use an explicit polynomial bounded computation circuit computing whether M rejects p within t steps. The program need not generate a proof or expose any reasoning. Its input is p, never the independent satisfying witness x. Use definitional Boolean circuit CNF E(p,z) forcing the reject output to one; z comprises computation gate bits, with a unique extension for any rejecting p. A standard bounded deterministic machine simulation constructs such a circuit in polynomial time in |M|+|U_n|+t. Define the reflection CNF

 R = E(p,z) AND AND_{C in U_n}(not p_C OR C(x)).

This is unsatisfiable iff M has no false rejection in the entire pool domain. Crucially, for every rejecting input p*, pin p to p* and every computation gate to the actual rejecting execution. All E clauses become true and the remaining guarded clauses restrict exactly to F_{p*}, in pool order. There are no witness-dependent evaluation gates to simulate. The formula may have width four, which is allowed in the base resolution verifier. This representation explicitly separates computation and satisfiability witnesses.

For ordinary resolution, apply the prior restriction algorithm to any refutation of R. The resulting proof of F_{p*} has no more lines or greater width than the original. This holds for each rejecting input, with no assumption about how M computes. Therefore the shortest reflection proof has length at least max_{p*:M rejects} L_res(F_{p*}) (when the rejecting set is nonempty and M is sound). The lower bound remains true even when M is extremely slow; only the length of the encoded reflection instance and verification cost depend on t.

Define Q_res on 3CNF to accept either an ordinary resolution proof or a tuple containing M,n,t, a resolution refutation of this explicit R, and an actual rejecting run on the normalized input. Q_res is sound, complete, polynomially checkable and polynomially simulated by ordinary resolution: compute the restriction, restrict the supplied proof, and translate renamings back. All variables, unary bounds and trace bits are charged. Ordinary resolution is included in Q_res, so the systems are polynomially equivalent on normalized 3CNF. This is a theorem for this particular pool encoding, not every possible reflection formula or stronger proof calculus. No universal machine compiler is implemented in the finite regression.

By classical superpolynomial resolution lower bounds on 3CNFs (e.g. Tseitin; Ben-Sasson and Wigderson, Short Proofs Are Narrow — Resolution Made Simple, JACM 2001, https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/eccc-reports/1999/TR99-022/), Q_res is not polynomially bounded. More strongly, every correct total SAT decider with clock covering its rejection on all n-variable pool inputs has reflection proofs of superpolynomial length along hard n, irrespective of its actual computational efficiency. A hypothetical polynomial decider would NOT give short resolution reflection proofs. Thus the exact simulation premise of the previous negative-route criterion fails for this encoding and base calculus. This is not a contradiction with the existence of polynomial SAT deciders, and establishes neither P=NP nor P!=NP. A stronger calculus without such a known lower bound would require its own independent argument.

## Alternative track: compact rejection circuit versus explicit core enumeration

For k>=1, form a directed chain of k diamonds. Backbone vertices x_0,...,x_k and two private internal vertices a_i,b_i per segment. Pool clauses are units x_0, not x_k, and implications for edges x_i -> a_i -> x_{i+1} and x_i -> b_i -> x_{i+1}. Every selected subformula is Horn. It is unsatisfiable iff both units are selected and each diamond has at least one complete path selected. Necessity: otherwise set exactly the vertices reachable from x_0 true if its unit is selected, or all false if not; if the negative unit is absent the all-true assignment works. Sufficiency: selected paths force x_k true. The predicate is a linear-size monotone circuit on presence bits: AND of the units and k ORs of two pairwise ANDs. It is an explicit polynomial rejector for this pool. Exactly 7^k presence vectors are rejected (among 16 possibilities per segment, 7 have a complete path). Exactly 2^k minimal rejected subsets exist, one path per segment and both units. A sound explicit positive DNF cover of this rejection predicate requires at least 2^k terms: each term's set of required clauses must itself be rejected, so a minimal rejected subset can satisfy only a term equal to itself. This does not apply to arbitrary circuits, negative-literal DNF without further analysis, or general proofs. It does not prove a SAT runtime lower bound.

Encode each path gate t iff both corresponding presence bits; q iff either path gate; force both unit-presence bits and all q true. Together with guarded pool clauses this is the witness-separated reflection formula for this circuit, without a separate final AND output gate. A compact ordinary resolution proof exists: derive x_0 and not x_k from the forced unit selectors. For each path, resolve its gate-to-presence clauses with guarded implications to obtain the two path implications guarded by not t. Resolve the middle vertex and use the previously derived x_i to infer not t OR x_{i+1}. Resolve the q gate's backward OR clause with its forced unit to get t_a OR t_b, then resolve with the two conditional path conclusions to obtain x_{i+1}. At the end resolve with not x_k. Exactly 7+21k lines in the specified producer, width at most three: six lines for the two guarded units, 21 per segment (eight per path, three for the choice clause and two for the merge), and one final resolution. The reflection proof transfers to every rejected subformula by fixing the circuit gates and presence bits, not by listing the minimal cores. This exhibits genuine compression of reflection evidence over explicit minimal-core covers and confirms that the preceding lower bound is specifically about the worst-case reflection of a decider covering general hard CNFs, not all rejectors.
