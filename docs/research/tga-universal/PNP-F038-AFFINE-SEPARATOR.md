## Basis-independent separators

After provenance-checked extraction F=A AND B, parametrize consistent A as x=x0+Gt using free variables t. Each residual clause C_i has a direction space W_i spanned by the direction rows of the coordinates occurring in it. Choose a supplied binary tree over residual clause occurrences (the executable default recursively bisects the given order). For each node v let W_v be the span of directions of its descendant clauses, O_v the span outside the subtree, and S_v=W_v intersect O_v. Set b=max_v dim S_v and ell=max_leaf dim W_v. The root boundary is zero. All spans, intersections, bases, and restriction maps are computable by polynomial-time Gaussian elimination, even for a dense parametrization. The rank is invariant under invertible changes of free coordinates. No efficient optimal tree-finding claim is made.

Assignments of t are equivalent for residual evaluation to linear functionals on the global direction span; every such functional extends to the ambient space. A message T_v consists of boundary functionals on S_v that extend to a functional on W_v satisfying all descendant clauses (including their affine offsets). Use a basis of S_v to encode a row as dim S_v bits. For a leaf enumerate the 2^dim W_v possible local functionals, evaluate the clause, and restrict successful rows to S_v. An empty residual CNF has a single empty successful leaf. Constant clauses are evaluated normally.

At a binary join with children u,w, let D=W_u intersect W_w. Then D is contained in both S_u and S_w. Also S_v is contained in S_u+S_w: write any s in S_v as a+c with a in W_u,c in W_w; since s is in O_v, a belongs to W_u intersect (O_v+W_w)=S_u and similarly c belongs to S_w. Pair rows of T_u,T_w that agree on D. Their restrictions to the parent boundary are well-defined, because they glue to a linear functional on S_u+S_w and S_v is a subspace of that sum. Any chosen child extensions to W_u and W_w agree on D, since the entire intersection is in their boundaries. Thus they glue to a functional on W_v, satisfying every descendant clause. Conversely every satisfying functional on W_v restricts to a compatible pair. The message recurrence is exact. This argument uses directions, not equality of the names of original variables. It does not treat correlated projections as independent without a gluing proof.

## Time and certificates

There are at most 2^b entries per boundary table. Each binary join examines at most 2^(2b) pairs, with polynomial linear-algebra work per pair. Leaves cost at most 2^ell trials. Including polynomial preprocessing, witness extraction and certificate verification, the cost is poly(L)(1+2^ell+2^(2b)), for explicit total input length L including the supplied tree. For width-three residual clauses ell<=3, so b=O(log L) suffices for polynomial time regardless of the global affine rank or direct-sum component rank. For arbitrary long clauses the stated bound must retain ell. The implementation stores one representative functional for each accepted boundary row, and glues child representatives by solving their union of linear restrictions on the two child spans. At the root, any representative yields a full affine assignment, verified against F.

For UNSAT, the certificate is either a checked inconsistent source-row combination or the complete boundary row sets at all nodes, with an empty root. A verifier recomputes the spans and compares each supplied set with the exact leaf/join recurrence using only the supplied child tables, not the search's witness representatives. For join checks it solves equations on child boundary bases to obtain a representative on their sum; compatibility is exactly consistency of those equations. Missing or extra rows fail verification. The verifier costs the same parameterized bound. The producer and verifier are not claimed polynomial in arbitrary b. Input extraction retains the prior exact provenance verifier. This strengthens the direct-sum result: zero boundary at every split means no shared directions; positive small boundaries allow interactions.

## Connected large-rank family

For m>=3 use variables a_i,b_i, affine equality a_i=b_i (two binary clauses), and two residual triples per cycle edge i->i+1 modulo m: (a_i OR b_i OR a_(i+1)) and (not a_i OR not b_i OR not a_(i+1)). They require adjacent group bits to be opposite. The residual triples do not form a full parity group. Global projected rank m, and the direct-sum algorithm has a single component of rank m. A balanced tree over the given cyclic order of the 2m residual clauses splits into contiguous intervals. Any cut has at most four shared group-bit directions, because crossing occurs only at the two interval endpoints, and splitting within an edge pair may share both endpoints of that edge. Hence b<=4 and ell=2. The solver and certificate producer have polynomial cost on the entire family, including odd cycles (UNSAT) and even cycles (SAT). This is an example of rank-width improvement, not a lower bound for the prior adaptive solver or arbitrary SAT algorithms. The same gluing proof applies to dense non-coordinate affine direction spaces.
