## Constructive track: precise hybrid proof system

Inputs are explicit parity equations a dot x=b over GF(2) and normalized CNF clauses on n Boolean variables. A line is an integer inequality c dot x<=d. Rules: Boolean bounds -x_i<=0 and x_i<=1; input clause inequalities; a parity cut given a subset of source equations (XOR their masks and right sides) and a forbidden pattern f on the resulting support a, with parity(f)!=b; nonnegative integer sums of preceding inequalities; division by a positive integer dividing every left coefficient, rounding the right side down.

The parity cut is
  sum_{i in a, f_i=0} x_i + sum_{i in a, f_i=1}(1-x_i) >= 1.
All solutions of the combined equation differ from the forbidden pattern in at least one supported bit. Equivalently the cut is the clause excluding that pattern, written as an integer inequality. This includes a zero-support odd equation (empty clause). For a fixed explicit row the checker uses only XOR and bit operations; no SAT query or optimization. All rules are sound, by parity addition, Boolean integrality or ordinary integer inequality arithmetic. The checker reconstructs every coefficient, checks indices and divisibility, and accepts a final zero left side with negative RHS. A proof with T encoded steps and total bit length B is checkable in poly(n+B+source length): coefficient bit lengths grow at most by the bit lengths of weights and logarithms of summand counts along the trace, with total growth polynomial in B. No extension variables or reverse arbitrary inequality-to-XOR inference are allowed.

**Completeness (not polynomial boundedness).** Expand each input affine equation into the clauses excluding every opposite-parity assignment on its support. This may be exponential in support width, but is finite and exact. Ordinary resolution is refutation complete on the expanded CNF. Each non-tautological resolution step can be simulated by a bounded number of integer operations and O(n) Boolean-bound references: if parents are A OR x and B OR not x, their inequalities written as sums of literal truth values >=1 add to sum(A)+sum(B)>=1 after pivot cancellation. For every literal appearing once, add its nonnegative truth bound, obtaining 2 sum(A union B)>=1. Divide and round up (equivalently negate and round down) to get the exact resolvent inequality. When the union is empty, the first sum is already a contradiction. Tautological resolvents can be skipped as in ordinary resolution completeness. Therefore every UNSAT input has a finite hybrid refutation; the producer implemented here deliberately uses exhaustive expansion and closure for small regression inputs. It is not a polynomial-time proof search. For CNF inputs alone this system includes ordinary cutting planes and resolution simulation. Polynomial-size refutations for all unsatisfiable CNFs in this system would imply NP=coNP; conversely NP=coNP by itself does not imply this specific calculus is polynomially bounded. A total uniform polynomial-time proof producer for all UNSAT CNFs (with a polynomial clock and sound verifier) would imply P=NP. These are standard consequence statements, not an assertion of either equality.

## Alternative track: exact separation

Given a rational point z in [0,1]^n and a parity row (a,b), a cut is violated iff the weighted Hamming distance from z to an opposite-parity pattern on a is <1. Choose each supported bit nearest to z_i, breaking ties at zero. If its parity is forbidden, this minimizes the distance. Otherwise flip a bit minimizing |1-2z_i|. Any parity-changing flip set has odd cardinality and nonnegative total incremental cost, hence one minimum-cost flip is optimal. Empty support is handled explicitly. Using a common integer denominator D, comparisons use exact integer numerators. For rank r of the span of augmented source rows (a,b), enumerate 2^r combinations of a basis, preserving source provenance masks, and optimize each cut in polynomial time. Thus exact separation costs 2^r poly(L), including rational arithmetic and witness checking. This is rank-parameterized discovery, not a generic efficient cut oracle.

## TECT-X adversarial theorem: NP-complete redundant affine parity-cut separation at a uniform point

Decision problem: given a consistent binary affine system and a rational point z, does any XOR combination of its rows yield a violated forbidden-pattern inequality? NP membership uses the source-row subset and forbidden pattern, checked by XOR and exact rational arithmetic.

Reduction from X3C on a universe of 3q elements, q>=1. Let B be its element-by-triple incidence matrix (each column has exactly three ones). Solve B y=1 over GF(2) by Gaussian elimination. If inconsistent there is no cover; output a fixed consistent no-cut instance (one unconstrained variable at z=1/2). Otherwise let y0 be a particular solution and K a basis of ker B. Introduce one Boolean variable per triple and use the affine equations k dot x=0 for k in K and y0 dot x=1. These rows are independent: By0=1, Bk=0. In particular the affine system is consistent (independent rows give a surjective linear map onto their RHS coordinates). Its derived odd-right-side rows are exactly (y,1) with By=1; its even rows are exactly (k,0) with Bk=0. At the uniform point z_i=1/(q+1) (<=1/2), the distance to a forbidden pattern f on a support of size w is
  [w+(q-1)|f|]/(q+1).
For odd RHS, f=0 minimizes it, so a violated cut exists iff w<=q. For even RHS, forbidden f must have odd positive weight. A nonzero kernel vector cannot have weight one because every column of B is nonzero; thus w>=2. Its distance is at least [2+q-1]/(q+1)=1, so no even row produces a violated cut. The zero even row has no forbidden pattern. Therefore separation is equivalent to finding By=1 with |y|<=q. Every element must be covered an odd positive number of times by the selected triples, requiring at least 3q incidences. Each selected triple contributes exactly three. With <=q selected triples, equality forces each element covered exactly once. Conversely an exact cover has weight q and satisfies the binary system. All construction and encoding costs are polynomial. This proves the stated NP-completeness even for consistent affine systems and uniform rational points. It does not require that the point satisfies all initially supplied parity inequalities; that stronger restricted separation problem is not claimed here. The general RPC hardness is known; this is a self-contained specialization for our exact cut rule.

A cut certificate has a polynomial-length subset/pattern representation and can be checked efficiently, even though exact discovery is NP-complete. This does not show that any useful cut is hard to find on every input, that a proof-producing algorithm must perform exact separation, or that P!=NP. The positive route remains open: a sufficient polynomially discoverable subset of useful cuts might avoid exact separation. The negative route would require a barrier covering all deterministic polynomial SAT algorithms, not just this oracle.

Topology: the relevant algebraic obstruction is the affine coset By=1 of incidence-chain selections and the minimum support needed for a cover. It is not a general topological invariant of SAT. TGA distinguishes an established restricted calculus completeness claim from unresolved uniform proof size and discovery.
