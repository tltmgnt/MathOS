## 1. Result and scope

Use the inherited nine-group normalized selector family, n=19m-7, L=Theta(m^3), actual raw forbidden support bits F_H of widths 1..3 and the fixed one-hot/OR/pin/guard extension. Choose a compressed group g, three conditioning groups K disjoint from g, and a two-element retained subset J of K. Write the full context as (d,e,t), where d,e are the retained digits and t is the remaining digit (order the groups canonically). As in the parent, a wide H containing g has two other groups outside K; all other H containing g are narrow. Use the parent's weighted directed section losses, and write ell_H(a,b) for the number of newly forbidden (u,v) pairs in a wide section. For a deterministic policy selecting nonempty sets C_c and minimizing maps p_c(a) in C_c, let beta=sum_c |C_c|/m^4, delta_n and delta_w be the total narrow and wide weighted losses divided by m^6.

Define the conditional frequencies and projected allowed relations

    N(d,e,a,b) = #{t : p_(d,e,t)(a)=b},
    U_H(f) = {(d,e,a,u,v): exists b with N(d,e,a,b)>=f
                               and F_H(a,u,v)=0,F_H(b,u,v)=1}.

Here f is a positive integer threshold at most m, and sum_b N(d,e,a,b)=m. Let gamma_f be the fraction of (a,c) whose actual representative has frequency below f and incurs positive loss on some wide support. Put lambda_f=sum_(H wide)|U_H(f)|/m^5. The computed score is

    sigma = beta + delta_n + gamma_f + lambda_f.

For the explicit policy family in Section 3, a single fixed deterministic original three-symbol one-tape witness machine can compute the minimum score over all coordinate choices and dyadic f, and solve the original problem in

    O(m^(44/5) polylog(m+2)
      + m^9 sigma^(1/5) (1+log(1/sigma))^A)

physical transitions for sufficiently small positive sigma, for a fixed integer A. Visited span O(m^3 polylog m). Thus any sequence of SAT inputs with a vanishing score among these computable policies has eventual exact cubic-stage coverage. This is a two-retained extension of the partial-projection interface, not a dominance theorem over every one-retained score or optimization over arbitrary maps. It does not establish recovery for arbitrary vanishing wide average loss. The original one-retained solver remains available as its own fixed description.

The key new resource step is pair-sliced map regeneration in O(m^5 polylog m) physical time per retained pair, not O(m^6); the cost of exact width-five union generation is charged separately. Neither a global m^4 frequency table nor a global m^5 restriction table is stored.

## 2. Exact recovery and the frequency bound

For a wide section let Z_H(a,b) be its actual old-zero/new-one pairs. As in the parent, retain all original constraints in every branch. Use an envelope A0 on {g} union K consisting of selected representatives and rare positive-wide-loss source/context pairs; use exact narrow exceptions E_H on H union K; and use U_H(f) on H union J for each wide support. Widths are four or five, including the new projected wide relations. A representative has zero minimum total directed loss, so it cannot have a positive wide loss: the two parts of A0 are disjoint. Its density is beta+gamma_f. The sum of normalized narrow exception densities is delta_n by the parent's weighted counting identity.

For any original solution, replace its g digit by its actual representative. If this succeeds the resulting model belongs to the representative envelope. If it fails and the substitution is rare with positive wide loss the original belongs to A0. Otherwise, any violated wide support proves membership of the original tuple in U_H(f), since its representative has N>=f; if there is no violated wide support a narrow exception contains the original. All branches are subsets of original models, so this is an exact witness/existence reduction, not necessarily preservation of all models. At most 38 branches suffice, with unchanged external normalized clause syntax and pins.

The inequality

    f |U_H(f)| <= sum_(d,e,a,b) N(d,e,a,b) ell_H(a,b)

follows by bounding a union by the sum of contributing sets and weighting each by N>=f. In particular with theta=f/m,

    lambda_f <= delta_w/theta.

This can be used as a sufficient condition instead of computing exact unions; the machine below computes the exact score. The normalization differs from the one-retained case: each (d,e,a) fiber has m contexts, not m^2.

## 3. Finite uniform policy selection

For each of 504 choices g,K and three choices of retained J, consider the unconstrained full weighted directed facility-greedy policy from the parent, selecting the smallest positive round minimizing k*m^2+F_k, where F_k is the sum of minimum distances to the chosen centers. Use deterministic tie rules (least digit when adding, earliest selected representative on minimum-distance ties). Also use palette policies based on aligned dyadic intervals of positive numerals 1..m (actual digits plus one). The interval center label is the first or second retained digit plus one, or 1+((d+e) mod m) for the sum policy. For every dyadic interval width from 1 to the least power of two >=m+1, C_c is the entire nonempty palette; the map minimizes full weighted directed loss with least-digit ties. The sum palette of width 1 is the singleton modular-sum policy. All these are fixed uniform algorithms, not arbitrary supplied palettes. There are O(log m) policies. For every policy evaluate f=1,2,4,... below m plus m. Minimize the exact integer score m^6 sigma (the union contribution is m*sum |U_H|), using a fixed tie rule. Keep only the winning policy and score. No coupled-frequency greedy approximation claim.

For a given policy, its computed score can be compared directly against the desired sufficient condition. The old one-retained policy family is not lost (its previous fixed program still exists), but a threshold that is good when one digit is retained need not yield the same score with two digits retained. At f=1 refining the projection never increases normalized union density for the same map, and gamma=0 in either case.

## 4. Pair-sliced map generator: improved physical resource bound

This section is the principal new physical derivation. It relies on the fixed sequential-tape sweep and mobile carry-shift routines inherited from earlier notes, not random-access unit-cost operations.

For H containing g let I=H intersect K, O=H minus ({g} union K). For |I|<=1 compute weighted distance contribution tables by aligned comparisons, with at most m^3 entries per support, sorted in candidate-major order (representative b,source a) within each I slice. Cost O(m^4 polylog m) sequential bit steps for each support, O(m^3 polylog m) span. For |I|=2 the support is a triple, and retain its explicit raw bits rather than an m^4 distance table. There are only 37 supports. The full raw selector parsing, actual pin extraction, sorting and these caches cost O(m^7 polylog m) physical moves via the inherited fixed-track sweep. For each choice of J, arrange the tables with I equal to the unretained singleton on separate fixed stationary tracks aligned by t and the common (b,a) order; their record widths are O(log m) and can be padded to the same fixed-per-input width. Tracks are fixed in number, not one per digit or context. All other caches occupy separate stationary components.

For a single retained pair d,e, extract all fixed contribution slices (I empty, or singleton retained) and all needed raw triple vectors (both retained, or one retained and one unretained). The latter require at most m^2 bits per support, since t varies and each raw vector has m bits. These, fixed slices and scratch occupy O(m^2 polylog m) blocks. Scan the stationary O(m^3 polylog m) span with this mobile workspace, filtering keys and appending matches, using the inherited one-block carry shift and local scans. Cost O(m^5 polylog m) physical moves. No full all-context matrix is stored.

Then traverse the aligned unretained-singleton streams in order, one t slice at a time, keeping the fixed data in the mobile workspace. Each stream has m^2 entries per t. Collect the current slice(s), move the entire workspace along the stationary streams, and pause at the slice boundary. Moving it across the total O(m^3 polylog m) stream extent costs O(m^5 polylog m) over ALL t for this pair, even charging a workspace scan per stationary record; it is not repeated for every t. If no such table has varying I, use a stationary dummy stream or just the same local interval repeatedly. The fixed pair caches travel with the workspace; do not shuttle them back to a global anchor between contexts. Extract the relevant raw triple vector(s) from the cached O(m^2)-bit tables, form their contributions, combine the aligned arrays and retain both the full and narrow weighted distance matrices. This takes O(m^2 polylog m) sequential bit steps locally, including sorting/merging or sequential scans of raw-vector records; a local O(m^2 polylog m) sweep overhead gives O(m^4 polylog m) physical moves per t.

The old generic sweep for greedy selection would charge O(m^5) per t. Instead keep the distance matrix stationary in this LOCAL interval in candidate-major order, and use a second, smaller mobile component containing the length-m residual array, label array, selected flags and counters, all O(m polylog m) blocks. For one greedy round traverse the candidate-major matrix (O(m^2 polylog m) blocks) comparing each distance with the corresponding residual, summing the candidate score and remembering the best unused candidate. Sequential scans of the length-m mobile arrays suffice for each record, even without indexed lookup. Shift the smaller component along the matrix with the inherited carry-shift procedure, preserving the matrix and the pair caches on their other components. Thus one complete scan costs O(m^3 polylog m) original moves. A second scan updates residuals and labels for the winning column and marks the chosen digit; rewind and repeat. Initialization, loop state, deterministic rerun to the best round and extraction of the final maps cost O(m^4 polylog m) per t across m rounds. This nested mobile workspace uses a fixed number of product components: the smaller array shifts only during the paused local computation and all of its reserved extent can subsequently travel with the outer workspace. Its span never exceeds a fixed multiple of the local O(m^2 polylog m) interval. Palette minimum scans and exact narrow-loss summation by map-aligned scans fit the same bound. Per-context score and map output have O(m polylog m) bits; even per-bit shuttles over the original O(m^3 polylog m) span cost O(m^4 polylog m) per t, including returning to the paused marker. Keep the m contexts' outputs in a stationary spool of O(m^2 polylog m) bits, including set membership. At pair completion sort/count (a,b) records into N(a,b) with at most m^2 entries, using O(m^2 polylog m) bit steps and even the global O(m^3 polylog m) sweep overhead, i.e. O(m^5 polylog m) physical time. Store one pair's maps, scores and frequencies, then reuse the spool.

All per-t local costs summed over m contexts are O(m^5 polylog m); add the initial extraction and full aligned-stream scan at the same order. The pair generator is consequently O(m^5 polylog m) physical time, with original-scale span. Recompute it deterministically whenever needed; do not retain the outputs for all m^2 pairs. The asymptotic improvement relies both on aligning the t-varying stationary slices and on the smaller mobile residual array. Merely replacing the global sweep with a generic local sweep would not suffice.

## 5. Exact projected union rows and score census

For fixed d,e and source a, with the pair frequency slice available, construct a wide union row over (u,v). Read/copy the source raw row and the frequency flags N(a,b)>=f into mobile O(m^2 polylog m) scratch along with an initially zero (u,v) OR accumulator. Extracting these from stationary original-scale records costs O(m^5 polylog m) physical moves by mobile scanning; a scan of the pair frequency slice fits the same cost. Scan all candidate raw rows of the wide support in (b,u,v) order. For a qualifying b, OR old-zero/new-one into the accumulator at each (u,v); otherwise leave it unchanged. Even a full scratch scan and a carry shift per stationary record costs O(m^5 polylog m) over the O(m^3 polylog m) raw span. Count the resulting flags or copy only a requested subrectangle, then clear the row scratch. Only m^2 flags are materialized, never m^5. This is exact regardless of overlapping differences. The row generator has O(m^5 polylog m) physical cost, no free indexed lookup.

For each policy and threshold, enumerate retained pairs. Generate their maps/frequencies in total O(m^7 polylog m). Count rare positive-loss occurrences by matching each (a,b) frequency against the saved wide ell counts, multiplying by its count when N<f and some ell>0. Even using O(m^2) bit-step sorted merges and global sweeps per pair costs O(m^7 polylog m) total. Accumulate beta and narrow weighted losses from pair outputs. Count all union rows, m^3 rows across d,e,a per wide support, costing O(m^8 polylog m) physical moves. Discard a pair's output after its rows are counted. Coordinate choices and support count are constant, threshold and policy loops logarithmic. Thus the exact minimum score is computed within O(m^8 polylog m) physical moves and O(m^3 polylog m) span, without storing any global m^4 frequency array. This is a census of the actual chosen maps, not a probabilistic estimator.

## 6. Coarse width-five flags with bounded regeneration

Pad to M, the least power of two >=m, with unary exclusions for invalid digits. Let b0 be the largest power of two with b0^5<=m^3, so b0=Theta(m^(3/5)). (b0 is a scale, not a representative digit.) For each wide support and each projected coarse tile on its five coordinates (d,e,a,u,v), enumerate real d,e pairs in that tile. Regenerate that pair's maps and frequency slice using Section 4, at O(m^5 polylog m) physical cost per pair occurrence. For every real source a in its tile interval, generate its entire union row by Section 5. Keep only the b0-by-b0 subrectangle on u,v, with zeros for padding. Append local flags with their translated five-coordinate keys to a stationary spool. Even shuttling each emitted bit across original span costs O(m^3 b0^2 polylog m) per row, dominated by O(m^5 polylog m). Discard the row, then the pair, when no longer needed. All local flags, at most b0^5<=m^3, fit original-scale span. The parent sequential builder sorts by dyadic path and OR-merges prefixes to create a stationary restriction tree; O(m^6 polylog m) physical cost per projected tile. No map or row generation occurs during local recursive traversal.

Count pair occurrences: each of m^2 real retained pairs occurs in exactly one retained-pair block, and is regenerated for each of the (M/b0)^3 combinations of source and other-digit intervals. Thus pair regeneration costs

    O(m^5 * m^2 * (M/b0)^3 polylog m)
      = O(m^10/b0^3 polylog m).

Count row occurrences: each of m^3 real (d,e,a) triples occurs in exactly one three-coordinate block, and is regenerated for each of (M/b0)^2 other-digit interval choices. Thus row generation costs

    O(m^5 * m^3 * (M/b0)^2 polylog m)
      = O(m^10/b0^2 polylog m).

Tree building adds O(m^6*(M/b0)^5 polylog m)=O(m^8 polylog m). Substituting b0=Theta(m^(3/5)) gives respective exponents 41/5, 44/5 and 8. The maximum 44/5 is strictly below nine. This is the new width-five union resource bound; a naive all-representative expansion of all width-five flags with global sweep would reach m^9 polylog m.

For the envelope and narrow exceptions, process a coarse K block, regenerate each real retained pair within it once, then retain only the maps whose unretained t lies in its chosen interval. Attach rare positive-loss flags by the pair frequency counts and wide ell tables before discarding the full pair spool. This needs at most m*b0^3<=m^3 saved records per K block. Across all K blocks there are m^2*(M/b0) pair occurrences, so O(m^8/b0 polylog m) physical work. Preserve the block maps across all relevant projected and private tiles and branches. The previous batch query interface evaluates each envelope/narrow tile's b0^k<=m^3 candidates by sequential sort/merge against the saved maps and raw records; tree construction is O(m^6 polylog m) per projected block, O(m^8 polylog m) in total. This schedule avoids storing all pair frequencies simultaneously and avoids recomputing them at each full nine-coordinate tile.

For every tree (wide or narrow/envelope), reuse it over all private-coordinate coarse blocks. Extract the original width-three records with the inherited mobile b0^3 workspace, O(m^3 b0^3 polylog m) per full tile and O(m^12/b0^6 polylog m)=O(m^(42/5) polylog m) total. All root initialization, padding and cleanup are included. Combined preparation is dominated by m^(44/5) polylog m. Use one fixed reusable set of tracks encoded by constant-width blocks of actual 0,1,2. No input-dependent alphabet, tapes or simultaneous global tables.

## 7. Local solver, local logarithms and exact clock

Inherit the stationary-tree worker for width k<=5: the immutable projected flags reside in a preorder subtree of O(t^max(3,k) polylog(t+2)) extent at scale t. Only O(t^3) original forbidden records are copied into the corresponding reserved child frame. Private-coordinate alternatives reuse that frame sequentially after clearing, and active ancestors are distinct. Navigation, shuttles, translation, pushes/pops and return of successful digits cost O(t^8(1+log(t+2))^A) per full node, including the fixed 512 child choices. Zero flag prunes; at t=1 accept iff flag true and no forbidden record remains. The local physical head stays in the worker interval, without reading global map data. Local field lengths are functions of t, not m. Reconstruct original digits, actual pins and fixed auxiliaries once and output contiguous n raw bits with physical halt head at the first bit, or halt on blank if no branch succeeds.

For branch density rho=R/m^k, passing nodes at t are at most min((M/t)^9,R*(M/t)^(9-k)). Visited descendants are within a fixed factor by the passing-parent condition; charge mandatory coarse roots separately, O(m^9/b0 polylog m), within preparation. For R=0 no descendants are visited. The dyadic sum with local cost t^8 is bounded by

    sum_t min(M^9/t, R*M^(9-k)*t^(k-1))*(1+log(t+2))^A
      = O(m^9 rho^(1/k)(1+log(1/rho))^A),

using geometric tails on both sides of t0=M/R^(1/k). The log is local, not a global log m multiplier. All branches have k=4 or 5 and rho<=sigma; their fixed count gives the displayed theorem. Unpruned dyadic summation gives O(m^9), so correctness also holds outside the promise. If sigma->0 the time is o(m^9)=o(L^3). For the actual fixed program description ell, the stage 3 ceil(log2 L) allocates at least L^3/2^ell transitions once eligible. The little-o bound eventually fits it; no compiled program, numerical threshold or actual U stage run is claimed.

## 8. Fixed-policy projection separation (not a separation of all algorithms)

For arbitrary m>=2, on one wide support take F_H(a,u,v)=1 exactly when v=a and u!=a; all other raw supports may be zero. This family is SAT (all latent digits equal suffice). Use the fixed singleton policy p_(d,e,t)(a)=(d+e) mod m independent of a,t, which belongs to the sum-palette family. For a distinct replacement the loss set is {v=b,u!=b}, of size m-1, and sets for distinct representatives are disjoint in v. Narrow loss is zero; beta=1/m, delta_w=(m-1)^2/m^3=Theta(1/m).

Retaining only d, for every d,a every b has frequency m. For any one-retained threshold <=m the union contains m^2*(m-1)^2 tuples on its four coordinates, so normalized density (m-1)^2/m^2 tends to one. For a threshold >m all positive-loss substitutions are rare, giving gamma=(m-1)/m. The same holds retaining only e or only t (when t fixed the modular sum is uniformly distributed across d,e). This is a statement for this FIXED policy. Retaining both d,e, all m contexts use one b; at f=m there are no rare substitutions. The five-coordinate union has m^2*(m-1)^2 entries, hence normalized density (m-1)^2/m^3=Theta(1/m). The computed minimum two-retained score is O(1/m), giving O(m^(44/5) polylog m)=O(L^(44/15) polylog L) including the density term m^(44/5) times local logs. This demonstrates the benefit of retaining two coordinates without claiming that every possible one-retained policy fails on this easy family: a constant representative would also give a small context-free union. No computational hardness or strict separation of the full prior sufficient-condition class is asserted.

## 10. Disposition

Two retained conditioning coordinates now have an explicitly charged projected-width-five union generator, without global frequency storage. The new sufficient condition is vanishing score for the listed computable policies. Full three-context average-loss recovery remains open: a map can distribute its tiny positive losses across too many representatives even conditioned on two digits. A future direction is the structured full width-six predicate or a direct local solver avoiding global projection, with explicit physical costs. General unconditional exact cubic coverage, unbounded B_3, general SAT and P vs NP resolution remain OPEN. Prior finite interval 52..233 and reported Lean census unchanged, not recounted.
