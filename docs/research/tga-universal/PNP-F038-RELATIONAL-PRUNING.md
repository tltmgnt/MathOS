## Certified bounded-order necessary relations

For each node v let U_v be the exact feasible boundary relation on S_v. Fix a local enumeration rank h and a relational order k>=1. Store a summary (E_v, L_v): an affine equation system E_v (or false), and a list L_v of small predicates (W,P), with W a direction basis contained in S_v of rank at most k and P a set of allowed functional signatures on W. Every genuine feasible extension must satisfy both E_v and all predicates. This is a necessary condition only.

At a leaf or subtree of full direction rank <=h, enumerate exact projected rows on S_v as in the preceding hull method. Store their affine hull, and for every subset of at most k rows of the chosen independent S_v basis, store its exact coordinate projection if it is not universal. Empty relation has the false summary. At a larger join, combine child affine equations. Refine this affine system using the union of child small predicates by the propagation rule below. Project the refined affine system to S_v. For each carried predicate on W, project its allowed signatures to W intersect S_v and retain any nonuniversal resulting predicate. Deduplicate identical (scope,rows) pairs; do not assert that projections of multiple predicates commute with their conjunction. This relaxation forgets cross-predicate correlations, but every actual feasible extension satisfies every retained consequence. Induction proves soundness of all summaries. The chosen basis is part of the algorithm; this is not the set of projections onto every rank-k linear subspace. The number of retained predicates is bounded by the sum of the small-node source tables, not the number of all subspaces of the ambient vector space.

## Polynomial affine propagation of small nonlinear constraints

Given an affine feasible region E and a small relation (W,P), compute P' consisting of rows z in P for which E AND (signature_W=z) is consistent. If P' is empty, reject E. Otherwise every solution must satisfy the affine hull of P' on W. Intersect E with this hull. Repeatedly sweep the list until no rank increases, or a contradiction is detected. A strict refinement of a nonempty affine set increases its equation rank by at least one; thus there are at most n+1 sweeps including the final unchanged sweep (and at most n strict rank increases). All tests are linear consistency checks; each predicate has at most 2^k rows. Every step is a sound necessary restriction, never a proof that a nonempty remaining affine region is satisfiable. This rule can extract affine consequences of nonlinear predicates in the context of existing affine restrictions, even when their standalone affine hull is the full cube.

For a requested parent signature, refine the combined child equation system plus the parent pins by the carried predicates before enumerating its remaining linear fiber. Leaves enumerate only exact permitted local signatures. Reuse the earlier memoized recursive exact search and its certificate checker, parameterized by the summary and fiber functions. A negative root must exhaust the remaining fiber or derive a contradiction; a positive root is checked against all source predicates. The checker regenerates the summaries and every local refinement using the fixed algorithm; no unverified pruning is accepted. The prototype has no physical resource sandbox or full repair-projection orchestration.

If N is the total explicit source/tree encoding length, at most N small nodes each contribute at most sum_{j<=k} binom(dim S,j) tables, each of at most 2^k rows. Each larger node carries at most that total number, because each predicate projects to one predicate and no new cross-product scopes are created there. Thus preprocessing and per-visited-state pruning cost 2^max(h,ell) N^O(1) plus N^O(k+1) 2^O(k), where ell is maximum leaf rank; use the bound for preprocessing, and only the latter term for a large-node fiber refinement. Including exact search, a bound is N^O(k+1) 2^O(k+max(h,ell)) (1+|V|+sum q_v), where V are visited states and q_v counts enumerated surviving linear candidates. This is polynomial preprocessing for fixed k and h=O(log N), not an unconditional polynomial search bound. Increasing k at fixed h weakly strengthens the information available in this scheme, but no globally optimal summary or minimum-width decomposition is claimed.

## Strict separation on a 3-CNF family

Fix r>=3. For m independent blocks of r shared bits, the left half enforces NAE_r (exclude the all-zero and all-one patterns) via one positive and one negative length-r clause, split into width-three chains using fresh private variables when r>3. The right half enforces all bits of each block equal, using r-1 adjacent equalities, each encoded by the four width-three clauses from the preceding hull obstruction with fresh private variables. All source scopes occur singly or as two clauses differing in a single literal; no complete parity pattern is extracted. Each completed left block has full direction rank 3r-6 for r>=3; a completed right block has rank 3r-2. Choose h=3r-2 and group the complete block subtrees in each half; the half boundary is the coordinate span of the rm shared bits. At every small completed left block (or small union of complete blocks), the affine hull of the boundary is the full cube and every projection onto at most r-1 coordinate bits is full: any proper partial assignment extends to a nonconstant word. Products of the blocks have the same property for subsets of at most r-1 coordinates. Thus for k=r-1 no nontrivial carried left predicates survive at block boundaries. Larger left nodes retain only full hulls and no predicates. The right affine hull is the exact equality system (local exact summaries followed by independent joins). The root fiber therefore has exactly 2^m signatures, each incompatible with NAE_r. This specified search must exhaust all root candidates, even when the root's own small-subtree summary happened to be false (the query policy refines from its children).

At order k=r, the exact small-block summary retains the full NAE_r relation, and its scope stays inside every ancestor boundary on the left half. At the root the equality hull allows only the two constant words on this scope, so P' is empty and the entire fiber is rejected with zero candidate trials. This gives a strict hierarchy between adjacent orders for this fixed summary/search policy, with polynomial preprocessing in m for each fixed r. The preceding NAE triple obstruction is precisely the r=3 case. The construction is an easy contradiction for stronger reasoning systems; it is not a SAT runtime lower bound, a lower bound against arbitrary rank-k linear predicates, or a proof that all algorithms of bounded order fail. Exact coordinate projections alone, even if computed with unlimited effort on the left message, cannot expose the missing all-equal patterns at order r-1. The example explains why higher-order constraints matter but does not prove that all necessary correlations can be recovered economically.
