## Factored representation

Replace a single union of affine flats by a conjunction of factors, each itself an affine union on a coefficient direction space K_j. A represented set of ambient functionals satisfies every factor. The K_j of a retained message are in direct sum; hence choices of local satisfying functionals glue without expanding their Cartesian product. No factor constrains directions outside its scope. The empty conjunction is true; a separate false sentinel represents the empty relation. A universal factor is omitted.

The preceding affine-union lower bound for independent NAE triples is therefore not an obstruction to this representation: keep one constant-size union per triple, rather than expanding their product. The problem is exact joins and projections in the presence of dependencies across factors.

## Exact join and projection without global expansion

Combine the factor lists of the input messages (they need not remain mutually independent). Let K be the sum of their scope spaces, and T the desired output direction space. First compute U=K intersect T by Gaussian elimination. The input conjunction is a constraint only on K; its exact projection onto T is the inverse image under restriction to U of its projection onto U. Indeed compatible functionals on K and T glue iff they agree on U, and the result extends to the ambient space.

Choose a basis of K greedily from the input scope rows and express all input rows and a basis of U in those coordinates. Build connected groups of basis indices by joining all coordinates touched by each factor, and all coordinates touched by each individual basis row of U. (The latter step is essential: projection can couple otherwise independent factors.) Each original factor and each U basis row now belongs to one group. The group direction spaces are independent coordinate spans of the chosen basis. For each group, enumerate the product of its local affine-piece lists, intersect the equations, discard inconsistent combinations, and project the remaining flats onto the span of its assigned U rows. Compress the resulting local union using the earlier exact subsumption and parallel merge operations. If any group has no feasible piece, return false. A group with no U rows contributes true iff it has a feasible piece. Otherwise retain the nonuniversal local projected union as one factor. Since all group spaces are in direct sum, local choices glue to a global functional on K. Conversely any global satisfying functional restricts to an allowed local choice. The output factors live in independent subspaces of U and represent the exact projection. A witness for the input conjunction is obtained by choosing a feasible combined flat in each group and solving the union of its equations. No arbitrary global independence assumption is needed.

The choice of U basis may create unnecessarily large groups. The theorem is exact but does not optimize grouping or claim a canonical minimum factorization. Simply projecting each original factor separately is unsound in general, because shared output linear combinations may correlate different factors.

## Computational cost and certificates

If group g contains input factors with piece counts p_i>=1, write H_g=product_i p_i. With explicit encoding length L, all linear algebra is polynomial in L and the actual message descriptions. Sequential local combination and exact compression need at most polynomial time in H_g, since compression only decreases the piece count. Thus a join/projection costs poly(L, input description size) times a polynomial in the sum_g H_g, and does not require the product across different groups. Equivalently, in the complete semantic-repair computation, let H be the maximum sum of local products over any operation (including witness reconstruction and local projection tests), and P the maximum total number of represented pieces over a message. Time and certificate checking are poly(L,P,H,2^ell), where ell is the leaf rank. This is an intermediate-sensitive bound, not a guarantee that P or H is polynomial.

## High-rank family overcoming the affine-union representation barrier

Take m independent NAE triples with positive and negative width-three clauses; duplicate the conjunction in two halves of a supplied tree, pairing the two occurrences within each triple. The specified affine extraction leaves every clause residual (each parity class is incomplete). The two halves have a boundary of rank 3m. Their exact feasible relation contains 6^m points and any flat contained in it has at most 4^m points, hence an exact unfactored affine union needs at least ceil((3/2)^m) pieces, as previously proved. In the new representation each triple contributes one factor of at most six point pieces (and may compress further). The two halves therefore need at most 6m pieces in total per half message and all group products in their joins are bounded by constants per triple. This is an exponential representation separation on the identical message, not an arbitrary SAT runtime lower bound.

Add fresh c and a candidate clause (not a_1 OR c), as a sibling of the two-half tree. On complementing this clause, actual bad models have a_1=1,c=0 and satisfy all NAE triples. Flipping c is a valid nonvacuous translation. All scopes during inside/outside passes remain in the disjoint triple spaces except the first triple coupled to c. In the supplied tree all scope and separator bases are coordinate subspaces, so grouping cannot mix distinct triple blocks; the exceptional first block has dimension at most four. Consequently each local group has bounded rank and bounded number of input factors (at most one per block in each retained input, plus a local signature point); every intermediate piece product is bounded by a constant. The full repair computation is polynomial on this family, despite rank 3m and exponential affine-union requirements at the unchanged half messages. This bypasses the earlier representation obstruction, not a lower bound for all symbolic forms or an unrestricted polynomial SAT theorem.
