## 2. Exact rank certification, no random access

Total payload of all row strings at this scale is m*M^2=O(m^3), and keys add only fixed logarithmic factors. Process squares sequentially on fixed sequential bit tapes. Maintain an echelon basis with increasing leading pivot positions, each row of length t^2. To reduce a candidate, scan basis rows in pivot order, read the corresponding pivot bit of the working row and, when it is one, XOR the basis row into it. Rewind working strings as required. Each basis row comparison, pivot access and XOR costs O(t^2 polylog m) sequential bit steps; at most r such rows when current rank r is within cap. If a nonzero remainder survives, insert it by pivot order with a sequential copy/merge. This maintains an echelon basis; rank is exactly the number of nonzero basis rows. Stop and reject the certificate as soon as rank exceeds the cap. For alpha=1/4 use integer comparison r^4<=4^4*t. Store at most the cap plus one rows, and in any case at most m real rows. The total per-scale bit cost is O(m^3 t^alpha polylog m), with O(m^3 polylog m) span. Sum scales and apply inherited original-scale sweep simulation to get O(m^(6+alpha) polylog m) physical work, at alpha=1/4 O(m^(25/4) polylog m). This is polynomially subcritical. No field operations beyond exact XOR and fixed integer counters. This rank routine need not store a coefficient vector for every global raw row during certification.

## 3. Moving linear basis and coefficient maps

For a wide branch use the parent's reversed stationary architecture. Build the stationary private tree on V, with immutable existence flags for all original V-contained supports, including private padding exclusions. Keep all other original selected records (including P-contained constraints and projected padding units) as ordinary moving singleton lists, O(t^3) per full tile. Nothing is discarded.

At side t the moving exception frame has an echelon basis B_1,...,B_r of the restricted original wide row space on its free square. Store its r*t^2 bits, with r<=c*t^alpha, and at most t^4 map records on the local (g,K) four-coordinate cube. Each valid source/context record contains its four local digits and TWO length-r coefficient vectors l,h giving the original source row and actual replacement row as GF(2) combinations of the same basis. Do not store the original representative's global digit inside the recursive frame. Keep the span of ALL real raw rows, even if some are unused by the current map. Padded source/context digits have no map entry. Padded free columns are zero in every basis row. Thus the exact exception bit at a map record and local free column j is

    (1 - XOR_i(l_i * B_i[j])) * XOR_i(h_i * B_i[j]).

This is a factorization of a Boolean predicate, not XOR of the original CNF clauses. ## 4. Exact positivity with charged mobile coefficient queries

The rank certificate need not bound the number of distinct coefficient vectors or their pairs. In particular do NOT form a full matrix of all possible coefficient pairs (which would be exponential in rank). Transpose the local basis to column-major form by sequential sorting of the r*t^2 bit records with local keys, or maintain a second local column copy. Even charging local sweep over frame extent, the sort fits O(t^(6+2alpha) polylog t). This is dominated by the final local bound.

For each of at most t^4 map entries, mark its position, copy the two coefficient vectors into a mobile component of O((r+1) polylog(t+2)) extent and scan the column-major basis. For a column, compute the two dot products by aligned XOR accumulators on those coefficients and the column vector. Sequential scans of the small mobile workspace per stationary record suffice even without indexed coefficient access. Carry-shift that component along the stationary column stream and return to the marked map entry. A full pass across the entire local frame extent O(t^(4+alpha) polylog t), even charging a full O(t^alpha polylog t) scratch scan/shift at every block, costs O(t^(4+2alpha) polylog t). Copying the coefficients by per-bit shuttles and rewinding also fit this bound. The actual basis scan alone is O(t^(2+2alpha) polylog t). Maintain only an OR of the old-zero/new-one tests; no materialized old/new row for each map entry, no global-address lookup. Repeat until a passing pair is found or all entries exhausted. The test costs O(t^(8+2alpha) polylog(t+2)) physical moves and detects EXACT nonemptiness of E_H on the current projected cube, independently of residual constraints. A private zero flag or empty E_H prunes. A valid restricted original model cannot be pruned by either.

    l' = l U, h' = h U   over GF(2).

## 8. Growing-rank separation from distinct-row cardinality

Let m=2^d, with digits all binary vectors. For sufficiently large d take k=floor(log2 d), q=2^k<=d, and let f(u) be the k-bit integer extracted from bit positions 0,4,8,...,4(k-1) of u (least significant position is zero). These positions exist for sufficiently large d. Choose a nonempty aligned interval J of v digits, for example [0,m/2^l) with positive integer l growing arbitrarily slowly and l<d. Define the genuine wide support

    F_H(a,u,v) = bit_(f(u))(a) * 1{v in J}.

In an aligned u interval of side t=2^s, only its low s bits vary. At most ceil(s/4) selected bit positions can vary, so there are at most 2^ceil(s/4)<=2*t^(1/4) distinct feature labels. Every nonzero column in the local row matrix is the coordinate projection on the a vectors at one such label; other columns are zero. Its GF(2) rank is at most that number. This proves the new certificate for every aligned square, including t=1. For the square with u,v in [0,t) at t=2^(4k) and J containing [0,t), all q labels occur and every q-bit assignment to those coordinates is realized by a real a. Hence there are exactly 2^q distinct row patterns and rank exactly q. For sufficiently large d, q grows faster than any constant multiple of k, and 2^q/(t^2)=2^(q-8k) diverges. This fails ANY fixed-constant quadratic distinct-row cap, despite the local rank certificate. This argument is algebraic, not an enumeration at enormous m.

Embed this wide support in the previous nine-group control with g=0,K={1,2,3},H={0,4,5}. Retain unary zero exclusions in every group, main triples {0,i,j} for 1<=i<j<=3 forbidding <x0,xi XOR xj>=1, binary inner-product-one forbiddances among 1..8, equality forbiddances on pairs involving 0, and Latin forbidden triples x XOR y=z among groups 1..8 except {6,7,8}, where use the previous private low-bit parity forbiddance. Only H is active wide for the designated choice. Isolate zero and the three conditioned digits, and select one representative per nonempty fiber of the three inner products on the remaining nonzero sources. At most 12 representatives, beta<=12/m, delta_n<=5/m, and delta_w<=|J|/m=2^-l since newly forbidden pairs must have v in J. The family is SAT for sufficiently large d: take groups 1..8 all equal to vector 6 (nonzero, even self dot, low bit zero, no Latin violation), and choose nonzero a!=6 with bit_(f(6))(a)=0. Main triples then pass and the wide bit is zero, whether or not 6 belongs to J. All binary pairs are constrained, so primal graph is complete.

For alternative g,K, the earlier lower bound on weighted full loss is unchanged: if g=0 but K differs, some main triple has at least one free other coordinate, and distinct nonzero replacements lose m^2/4 weighted entries by independence of the two binary linear forms. If g>=1, a free binary neighbor among 1..8 yields the same weighted loss. Nonzero-to-zero loses m^2 on its unary bit. Apart from selected representatives and zero sources, a constant normalized loss follows. Thus no alternative coordinate choice admits vanishing capacity and full loss. The new rank-based promise strictly covers a family not covered by the preceding row-cardinality promise, even with alternate choices. It is not a containment theorem in the opposite direction: a set of O(t^2) distinct rows can have large rank. The loss bound is only an upper bound; it does not prove an arbitrarily slow *optimal* loss rate, or exclude the preceding polynomial-loss or other solvers. Easy SAT control, no computational hardness claim.
