## 1. Result

Use the inherited nine-group normalized selector family, n=19m-7, L=Theta(m^3), arbitrary selected negative cross clauses of widths 1..3 and the fixed one-hot/OR/pin/guard extension. Let F_H(z) denote the actual raw selected forbidden bit. Choose a group g and two distinct conditioning groups K={h,j} disjoint from g. For each context c in [0,m)^K, allow a nonempty representative set C_c and map p_c(a) into that set. Define

    d_c(a,b)=sum_{H containing g} m^(2-|H minus ({g} union K)|)
                   * #{z on H minus ({g} union K):
                        F_H(a,c,z)=0, F_H(b,c,z)=1},
    beta=sum_c |C_c|/m^3,
    delta=sum_c sum_a min_{b in C_c} d_c(a,b)/m^5.

In F_H use only those conditioning digits actually in H. Each signature section has total weight m^2. There is ONE fixed deterministic original three-symbol single-tape witness program, correct throughout the family, such that along any sequence of SAT inputs admitting beta->0 and delta->0 for some choice of g,K (possibly changing per input), its runtime is o(m^9)=o(L^3), and hence it eventually fits the exact cubic stage. The machine requires neither the conditioning groups, maps, sets nor convergence rates as advice.

More precisely, it computes a score sigma=beta+delta for its own sets/maps and achieves, for sufficiently small positive sigma,

    O(m^(42/5) polylog(m+2) +
      m^9 sigma^(1/5) (1+log(1/sigma))^a)

physical transitions, for a fixed integer a, and visited span O(m^3 polylog m). The bound remains exact outside the promise with unconditional O(m^9) time (constant additive stage slack, not unconditional exact stage). The two-context setting produces genuine width-five exception conditions; no such conditions are inserted as width-five external clauses. The main resource improvement is that restriction data stay stationary rather than being copied between recursive frames.

## 2. Exact existence recovery

Let S={(a,c):a in C_c} on P={g} union K. For each H containing g let P_H=H union K and

    E_H={z on P_H: F_H(z_H)=0 and
                   F_H(z_H with g replaced by p_(z_K)(z_g))=1}.

Every P_H has width 3..5. By counting the missing context digits and the other free digits with the weights above, the sum of the normalized densities |E_H|/m^|P_H| equals delta; |S|/m^3=beta. All branches keep the original constraints and impose membership of their P projection in the relevant allowed relation A. A satisfying original tuple either survives replacement (its image is an original model in the S branch) or violates some support containing g after replacement (the original tuple is in that E_H branch). Conversely every branch model satisfies the original constraints. This is an exact existence/witness reduction, not necessarily a union of the entire original model set. Under unique SAT a successful replacement necessarily gives the same model. There are at most 38 branches.

## 3. Factorized adaptive distance calculation

A naive extension of the parent's one-context selection could use O(m^6 polylog m) multitape bit time and thus lose the desired slack after a sweep simulation. Instead factor each weighted directed distance into its 37 support contributions. For H containing g put J=H intersect K, j0=|J|, and s=|H|-1-j0. The contribution depends only on the assigned J digits, a,b, and the m^s free contexts. Its weight is m^(2-s).

For j0=0 or 1, store the contribution table indexed by (J assignment,b,a), with candidate-major (b,a) order inside each context. The number of entries is m^(j0+2)<=m^3 per support. The weighted comparisons require O(m^(j0+2+s) polylog m)=O(m^(|H|+1) polylog m)<=O(m^4 polylog m) bit operations, using aligned sequential rows, one candidate row copied to scratch, rewinding it for each source row and accumulating a binary count. Sort original raw bits, including explicit zero bits, by context, digit and free context to provide these row scans. The constant number of support tables fits O(m^3 polylog m) span. For j0=2 the support is exactly {g,h,j}; do NOT materialize an m^4-entry distance table. For a fixed pair of context digits read its length-m raw bit vector by a sequential scan of the original stream; its contribution is m^2 precisely when source bit is zero and candidate bit one. Generate the m^2 contributions by aligned sequential scans of that vector (a candidate bit is saved in control for one row).

For each of the m^2 full contexts, sequentially filter the saved support tables to the relevant context slices, and combine the candidate-major arrays by sequential merge/addition. Including scans of the original raw stream and all cached tables, this costs O(m^3 polylog m) bit time per context and O(m^3 polylog m) span. Run the parent's directed greedy facility selection on the resulting matrix: start with all residuals 37m^2, add an unused candidate minimizing the summed residual after the addition, update maps, and choose the smallest positive round k minimizing k*m^2+F_k, where F_k is residual sum. In round one assign labels even if a distance equals the initial bound. The sequential matrix scans, selected flags, residual array and rerun to recover the chosen map take O(m^3 polylog m) bit time per context as in the parent. Store only one full map/set collection O(m^3) records and the best score, rerun the selected group/conditioning pair if needed. Do not retain all matrices for all contexts. Enumerate all 9*binom(8,2)=252 choices (order the two conditioning groups canonically). All choice loops have fixed dimension.

The total is O(m^5 polylog m) multitape bit time and O(m^3 polylog m) span, including canonical normalized parsing, pins and original record extraction. The inherited sweep simulation of fixed sequential bit tapes charges O(m^8 polylog m) original physical moves. No indexed free lookups. Each integer is polynomial in m and has O(log m) bits.

The same marginal-gain averaging proof as in the previous two representative notes gives F_k<=F_*+37m^3 exp(-k/h_*) for a competitor of size h_* within one context. Taking k=ceil(sqrt(h_*m)) and averaging over the m^2 contexts (using concavity of the square root and exp(-x)<=1/x for x>=1) gives the computed normalized score bound

    sigma <= 38 sqrt(beta_*) + 1/m + delta_*

for any competitor choice and sets, with minimizing maps. Thus the computed score tends to zero on the stated promise. This selection argument is independent of the resource bound for the subsequent higher-width branches.

## 4. Stationary projected tree: a bounded batch membership interface

Consider a restriction A on a fixed support P of size k=3..5, with R allowed entries and rho=R/m^k. Suppose membership of a batch of O(m^3) candidates can be evaluated in O(m^3 polylog m) sequential multitape bit time and span with stationary original-scale data of the same size. This is not an unrestricted oracle. Here for S it follows from merging (a,c) membership queries with the chosen sets. For E_H enumerate candidates, reject padding, emit (a,c,ID) map queries, merge with the saved map, emit old and new raw-bit queries on H, sort and merge against actual raw bits, restore by ID, and mark exactly old-zero/new-one. The raw and map streams each have O(m^3) records; all keys and IDs have O(log m) bits. Fixed sequential external sorting suffices.

Pad to M, the least power of two >=m. Choose b as the largest power of two with b^5<=m^3, so b=Theta(m^(3/5)) and b divides M. Partition P into coarse blocks of side b, and for each such block build a stationary complete 2^k-ary dyadic restriction tree. Only that one P block is materialized at a time. Enumerate its b^k<=m^3 candidate tuples and determine membership by the batch interface above. Leaves store the Boolean membership flag, including zero for invalid padding. Every internal node stores the OR of all descendant flags. Store the tree in preorder, with a header and reserved ORIGINAL-record frame region at each node, then all its disjoint child regions. Restriction flags, topology and markers are immutable during the worker traversal. The frame region is separate writable storage on a fixed product component; it holds O(t^3 polylog(t+2)) blocks at a node of local side t. All data use local scale fields, not global offsets. The header identifies depth/side and its fixed-size child-choice interface; no absolute tape addresses are required. The complete subtree extent at side t is

    S_k(t)=O(sum_(u<=t, dyadic) (t/u)^k*u^3 polylog(u+2))
           =O(t^max(3,k) polylog(t+2)).

For k=3 an extra local logarithm suffices. For k=4,5 the dyadic sum is dominated by the small levels up to a constant if local log factors are retained. In all cases S_k(b)=O(m^3 polylog m).

A concrete sequential builder emits for each candidate its dyadic prefix records carrying its membership flag (including zeros), sorts by preorder path, OR-merges identical prefixes, then emits a header and a reserved frame region of the prescribed local capacity per distinct node. Lex order on the strings of child labels with a proper prefix preceding its extensions gives preorder; each key has O(log m) bits. Delimiters identify node header, frame end and subtree end; subtree end markers can be inserted during the sequential output pass by the common-prefix length of successive paths, or by keeping a small local generation stack. Each region's polynomial capacity can be generated using binary counters and sequential writes in time linear in its length times polylog m. Total generation O(m^3 polylog m) bit time/span, including candidate queries and building the full tree. Fixed-track sweep simulation and copying into the reusable worker region cost O(m^6 polylog m) physical moves. There are (M/b)^k P blocks, so the one-time tree building cost summed over blocks is at most O(m^8 polylog m). No full global width-five table is ever built.

## 5. Reuse the stationary tree over all private-coordinate coarse blocks

For each fixed P coarse block, enumerate coarse blocks for the other 9-k coordinates. For each full coarse tile extract only the translated selected original width<=3 records (including padding units). Use the inherited mobile carry-shift workspace of O(b^3 polylog m) blocks on a separate component, scanning the stationary O(m^3 polylog m) raw record stream and checking interval containment. Each record translates injectively; at most O(b^3) records are retained. One-block shifts, scans, appends and returning the bounded list to the tree root cost O(m^3 b^3 polylog m) physical moves: shifting workspace across the original-scale span costs this amount, and even charging O(b^3 polylog m) per append adds only O(b^6 polylog m). Alternatively copy the resulting b^3-record list by per-bit shuttles over the global span, included in the same bound. This step does NOT repeat the full batch restriction evaluation or copy the b^5-sized tree for every private block. Root initialization, counters and final cleanup within the worker region are dominated by this cost, since b^5<=m^3. All external raw data, original m, coarse offsets and pins stay stationary and are not read during local recursion.

The total original-list preparation cost over full tiles is

    O((M/b)^9*m^3*b^3 polylog m)
      = O(m^12/b^6 polylog m)
      = O(m^(42/5) polylog m).

This and the stationary tree preparation solve the first resource obstruction of the previous naive width-five extension. The crucial reuse is across private-coordinate blocks, not just across recursive children.

## 6. Physical recursive traversal without copying restriction records

At a local full nine-coordinate node of scale t, the active original-record frame is the reserved frame of its P-projected tree node. Test the stationary OR flag: zero safely rejects the entire node. If t=1 and the flag is one, accept exactly when the original forbidden record list is empty (any retained record is violated by the unique local all-zero tuple). Otherwise enumerate all 512 full child bit vectors in fixed order. The P restriction of the vector determines which of the 2^k stationary child subtrees to use. Translate the original forbidden records by ALL nine interval choices and copy the retained records into that child's reserved frame. Other private-coordinate choices may use the SAME child frame later, sequentially after the prior visit completes. The stationary flag is identical for those choices and is never overwritten. A root-to-leaf path uses distinct P-projected nodes at successive depths; therefore every active ancestor frame remains distinct, even when many different private-coordinate prefixes share a projected node. The worker visits no siblings concurrently. At completion clear the writable original-record frame, not the immutable restriction tree; all descendant frames have already been popped. Save only local child-choice bits at the parent, return successful digits through the frames by adding the corresponding half-width offsets. No global offsets need to be carried inside small frames.

Physical navigation uses the static subtree delimiters: from a parent header, skip child subtrees in order using a local depth counter to find the requested projected child header. Return to the parent by scanning backward with matching delimiter depth, or use temporary parent/child cursor markers during the push and pop (each cursor can be distinguished by its position relative to the active subtree). A scan and any counter operations can be bounded by the length S_k(t) times a polynomial in log(t+2); all counters are local. The scratch slots and the stationary topology occupy fixed components of a single finite product alphabet encoded by constant-width binary blocks over actual 0,1,2. Parent and child record fields have local widths O(log(t+2)). To copy a record or its bits between the marked cursors, shuttle only within the parent's subtree interval, preserving the remaining components. There are O(t^3) records, and at most a fixed number of child operations; translation, initialization, navigation, copying, clearing, returning and successful digit propagation thus cost

    O(t^3*S_k(t) polylog(t+2)) = O(t^8 polylog(t+2))

per full node, including all child pushes and pops, since k<=5. For t=1, the frame and its payload are constant sized, with constant work. In particular this estimate does NOT square the width-five tree extent. The only copied lists are width<=3 original records; the width-five information is stationary and accessed by traversing its physical delimiters. No global root lookup, global address field, extra head, or input-dependent number of tracks. The combined active scratch and stationary tree occupy the preallocated O(b^max(3,k) polylog b) extent. Reused outer stationary records and scratch add O(m^3 polylog m). Following the worker's physical head at constant block overhead is the inherited product-component simulation, not a global sweep for every worker step.

Correctness follows because each stationary flag says exactly that the projected tile contains some A tuple, regardless of private coordinates, and local original records are precisely the selected forbidden tuples whose specified digits fit their intervals. Every original satisfying tuple restricted to A survives on its unique ancestral path, and every accepted singleton leaf has both properties. Stop on first witness, reconstruct original digits from local offsets and saved coarse offsets, reconstruct fixed auxiliary and actual pin bits and emit contiguous raw n-bit output with halt head at the first bit. The once-only O(m^4 polylog m) output shuttles are dominated above. On exhaustion halt on blank.

## 7. Local-log occupancy amortization

Across all coarse tiles, at side t<=b the number of flag-positive full nodes is at most min((M/t)^9,R*(M/t)^(9-k)), by counting disjoint projected blocks that contain an allowed tuple. Every visited node strictly below b has a flag-positive parent at 2t, so the count of all visited nodes (including rejected ones) is at most a constant times the same bound. Coarse roots are visited regardless of flag; charge their work separately by O((M/b)^9*b^8 polylog b)=O(m^9/b polylog m), also O(m^(42/5) polylog m). If R=0 no descendants are visited. For R>=1 sum the descendant costs, extending to all dyadic scales through M:

    sum_t min(M^9/t,R*M^(9-k)*t^(k-1))*(1+log(t+2))^a.

With t0=M/R^(1/k), the terms below the crossover fall geometrically as t decreases (k-1>0); above it they fall geometrically in t with a fixed polynomial in the dyadic scale index from the local logarithms. The same local-log summation as in the parent gives O(m^9 rho^(1/k)(1+log(1/rho))^a). No log m factor multiplies the density term. For k<=5 and rho<=sigma sufficiently small this is at most O(m^9 sigma^(1/5)(1+log(1/sigma))^a), by monotonicity near zero. Sum over the fixed number of branches and add the O(m^8 polylog m) adaptive selection. Without density saving the full descendant traversal is at most O(M^9 sum_t t^(-1)*(1+log(t+2))^a)=O(m^9) because the sum is dyadic. This proves the displayed main bound, including the exactness claim outside the promise.

On beta_*,delta_* ->0 the computed sigma tends to zero. ## 8. Separation from every one-context directed promise

Let q=2^d, m=q-1, identify digits with distinct nonzero vectors in GF(2)^d. Set g=0, conditioning groups 1,2. Select the forbidden triple bits on {0,1,2} exactly when <x0,x1+x2>=1. For each j=3..8 select forbidden triples on {0,1,j} exactly when <x0,x1> differs from <x1,xj>, and on {0,2,j} when <x0,x2> differs from <x2,xj>. Among groups 1..8 select forbidden binary pairs exactly when inner product is one. No other cross clauses. Every pair belongs to a constrained support, so the primal graph is complete. The tuple with every vector equal to the weight-two vector 3 satisfies all constraints. This is an easy SAT control, not asserted unique or hard.

For fixed nonzero c,d in groups 1,2, partition the nonzero vectors a by the pair of bits (<a,c>,<a,d>). Choose one representative from each nonempty fiber. At most four representatives suffice; every raw bit involving group 0 is preserved exactly under the replacement. Thus beta<=4/m, delta=0. The new adaptive machine has sigma=O(m^(-1/2)) and time O(m^(89/10) polylog m)=O(L^(89/30) polylog L) by the conservative score and main bound. This control is covered already by a polynomial saving, but separates the number of contexts needed by these sufficient conditions.

For any one-context choice for group 0, consider just the main triple. Distinct nonzero a,b are linearly independent over GF(2). There are exactly q/4 vectors w with <a,w>=0,<b,w>=1, all nonzero. If one of groups 1,2 is fixed to c, the other can equal w+c for at least q/4-1 of those w (exclude its zero vector); the section weight m gives directed loss at least m*(q/4-1). If the fixed conditioning group is outside the triple, each such w has exactly q-2 ordered decompositions into nonzero vectors x1,x2, giving loss q(q-2)/4. For any group g>=1 and any one conditioning group, choose a binary neighbor among groups 1..8 distinct from g and the conditioning group; its section alone gives weighted directed loss mq/4 for distinct nonzero a,b. These lower bounds apply to every ordered pair of distinct digits, at every context, for q>=8. Let D_min be the minimum of the three bounds. For arbitrary one-context representative sets with average relative size beta_1, every digit outside its selected set loses at least D_min; hence delta_1 >= (1-beta_1)*D_min/m^2. The latter factor tends to 1/4 as q grows. No one-context sublinear capacity and vanishing loss promise is possible for any group, although two contexts have zero loss and constant capacity per context. The statement separates these specific representation promises, not all earlier sufficient conditions or all algorithms.

## 10. Disposition

Two-context directed compression now has a vanishing-score cutoff, including width-five exceptions with bounded local materialization. The stationary tree makes the copied data volume depend on the original clause width, not the higher restriction width. The next research question is whether width-six restrictions from three contexts can be handled by stronger projection reuse or a different stationary representation: direct S_k(t)=O(t^6 polylog t) gives t^9 per node, while b<=m^(1/2) makes current full-tile extraction cost m^9 polylog m. Neither obstruction is a lower bound on other algorithms. General nine-group unconditional exact cubic coverage, unbounded B_3, general SAT and P versus NP resolution remain OPEN.
