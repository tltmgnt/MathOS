## 1. New sufficient condition

Use the inherited nine-group normalized selector family n=19m-7, L=Theta(m^3), original selected negative cross-clause bits F_B of widths one through three, and actual one-hot/OR/pin/guard extension. Choose g and three conditioning groups K, and h in K. Contextual representative sets C_c are nonempty and p_c(a) lies in C_c, with selected representatives mapped to themselves. Use beta=sum_c |C_c|/m^4 and delta=delta_n+delta_w=sum_(c,a,B containing g) d_B,c(a,p_c(a))/m^6, where d is the parent's weighted directed loss. A wide support H={g,u,v} has u,v outside K. Its directed difference is Z_H(a,b;x,y)=(1-F_H(a,x,y))*F_H(b,x,y). Write ell(a,b)=sum_H |Z_H(a,b)| over the at most ten wide supports.

Retain the parent's finite deterministic map policy family (full-average greedy and six-block-splice-penalized greedy), choices g,K,h, and canonical representative self ties. For each key (a,d) where d=c_h, let f_(a,d)(b) count the m^2 remaining contexts assigned b. For dyadic k<=m^(1/32), or k=0, put the k most frequent labels and self in a head palette P_(a,d). Break count ties by increasing digit. The tail consists of actual positive-frequency labels not in P. Choose a common integer threshold r from zero, powers of two up to 10m^2, and 10m^2 itself. For each key the low tail consists of its tail labels with ell(a,b)<=r; the high tail is the remainder. Form the exact union

    U_(H,a,d)(x,y) = OR_(b in low tail) Z_H(a,b;x,y).

Define n_low and n_high to be the numbers of context assignments to those label sets (sum their frequencies), and v=sum_H |U_(H,a,d)|. Route the low tail through the union if v<=n_low, else through a heavy envelope. Always route the high tail through the envelope. The resulting per-key charge is

    n_high + min(n_low,v).

For keys routed to the envelope the union is unused and set to zero. Write gamma for the normalized fraction of heavy assignments, eta for the sum of densities of retained width-four union relations (denominator m^4), and lambda for normalized wide loss of head assignments (denominator m^6). Thus gamma+eta is exactly the sum of the per-key charges divided by m^4. Select the minimum computed score

    Q = beta + delta_n + gamma + eta + (k+1)^13 lambda

over the finite policy family, choices and k,r. Use exact integer scores m^6 Q. No input-supplied maps, palettes, thresholds or rates.

There is ONE fixed deterministic original three-symbol one-tape witness program correct throughout the family. Under the inherited physical constructions its time for sufficiently small positive computed Q is

    O(m^(42/5) polylog(m+2) +
      m^9 Q^(1/6) (1+log(1/Q))^A),

with visited span O(m^3 polylog m), A fixed and implementation-dependent. Hence Q->0 implies eventual exact cubic-stage witness coverage, with no rate condition.

Here is a useful explicit sufficient condition strictly weaker than tightness for a given generated map sequence. Take r=10m^2, so there is no high tail. For a fixed k let

    tau_k = (1/m^4) sum_(a,d) min(
       sum_(b outside head) f_(a,d)(b),
       sum_H |union_(b outside head, f(b)>0) Z_H(a,b)|).

If the policy/coordinate choices have beta,delta_n,delta_w->0 and lim_(k->infinity) limsup_(m->infinity) tau_k=0, then Q->0. The choice of policy/coordinates may vary with the input. Since tau_k<=gamma_k of the parent, every earlier tightness promise is covered. Even k=0 may suffice if the union of actual nonself wide differences has small average size, regardless of how many replacement labels occur or how diffuse their frequencies are. The thresholded score can be smaller still. This does not prove tau_k tightness from beta,delta alone, and the quantifier concerns maps generated by the enumerated policies, not arbitrary existential maps.

## 2. Exact original witness recovery with an overapproximation

Use the representative relation S on {g} union K augmented by all heavy assignments. Its density is beta+gamma, since selected representatives map to self, which is in every head. Keep each ordinary narrow exception E_B on B union K unchanged. For each wide H include two branches:

    exact head guard E_H^head(a,c,x,y)
      = 1{p_c(a) in P_(a,c_h)} * Z_H(a,p_c(a);x,y);

    tail-union guard U_H(a,d,x,y)
      = retained U_(H,a,d)(x,y), with d=c_h.

All guards are internal restrictions. Every branch enforces ALL actual original support bits, padding exclusions and actual fixed extension. There is no new external clause syntax or altered verifier. A union guard may include tuples for which the actually selected replacement is not the label that generated the union bit. This enlargement is intentional: any satisfying tuple found under the guard is still an ORIGINAL model. We do not assert equality of the union branch and the actual tail-exception model sets.

Given an original satisfying tuple, substitute g by its selected representative. If the result satisfies, it lies in S. Otherwise, if the assignment is heavy, the original lies in S. If a narrow support is newly violated, the original is in its ordinary exception. In all remaining cases a wide H is newly violated. For a head assignment it is in E_H^head; for a retained low-tail assignment its selected b occurs in the union and thus it is in U_H. This proves existence/witness equivalence. It does not preserve all original models under substitution. There are at most 48 branches (one envelope, 27 narrow supports, two branches for each of ten wide supports). The sum of head-wide densities is lambda and the sum of union densities is eta. Narrow exception densities sum to delta_n. Every branch density is at most Q.

The per-key comparison is exact in the normalized score, not a heuristic exchange between unlike quantities. A heavy assignment represents one tuple on four coordinates (a,c), whose normalized contribution is 1/m^4. A union bit represents one tuple on four coordinates (a,d,x,y), also with normalized contribution 1/m^4. Sum over supports, without pretending their union relations are disjoint. High-tail assignments are charged once, not separately per wide support. A retained low-tail label with zero wide loss needs no nonzero union bit and causes no wide exception. Narrow exceptions remain available in either case.

## 7. Diffuse greedy-map control: tightness genuinely unnecessary for these maps

Take m=2^d, binary vectors as digits; let J=[0,m/2^l) where l positive and tends slowly to infinity with l<d. Put zeta=|J|/m. Use unary zero exclusions on all groups and one narrow support {0,1,2} forbidding a!=f(c1,c2), with

    f(c1,c2) = 1 + ((c1+c2) mod (m-1)).

Use one wide H={0,4,5}, F_H(a,u,v)=1{v in J}*<a,u>_2. All other cross supports empty. Choose g=0,K={1,2,3}. This is a simple satisfiable family (choose nonzero c1,c2, a=f, v outside J, other digits nonzero). Not a hard SAT instance or complete primal graph. All raw bits on the full digit domain, including zero, are defined. For distinct nonzero a,b, the wide loss is m*|J|/4; from zero to nonzero it is m*|J|/2; toward zero it is zero. The narrow ternary loss is m^2 exactly when the original a=f and b!=f. Nonzero-to-zero also incurs m^2 unary loss. Other g-containing losses zero.

At any fixed context, let b0=f. The first full-average greedy facility minimizes summed source loss. All nonzero targets have the same summed wide loss (one self, m-2 distinct nonzero sources, one zero source), and no unary loss. Target b0 uniquely minimizes narrow loss, because all other nonzero targets incur m^2 at source b0. Zero target incurs m^2 for each nonzero source, plus m^2 from the ternary, larger for sufficiently large m. After selecting b0, any extra nonzero target can improve only its own source by at most m*|J|/4; adding zero improves at most source zero by m*|J|/2. In particular every marginal improvement is strictly less than facility penalty m^2. Later marginal improvements cannot increase by the residual-minimum property. Hence the optimal positive greedy round has exactly C_c={b0}, p_c(a)=b0 for every source. This proof concerns the actual unpenalized generated map, not a chosen low-loss competitor. It yields beta=1/m, delta_n=0, delta_w=(m-2)*zeta/(4m)+zeta/(2m)=zeta/4.

For every distinguished h in K, condition on (a,c_h). If h is 1 or 2, as the other argument varies through m real digits, each f label occurs at most twice; the third conditioning digit is irrelevant. If h=3, for each fixed c1 again every f label occurs at most twice as c2 varies. Thus every replacement frequency is at most 2m among the m^2 remaining contexts, and even adding self to top k captures at most 2(k+1)/m of the assignments. For every fixed k, gamma_k->1, so these unpenalized maps fail the preceding tightness condition, for every distinguished context. Nonetheless at k=0, r=10m^2, every union for the single H is supported on v in J, so eta+gamma<=zeta and lambda=0. The new score is <=1/m+zeta and tends to zero at any rate of zeta decay. This is a strict relaxation of the sufficient CONDITION ON THESE GENERATED MAPS, not a separation from the previous entire adaptive solver: another g,K or penalized policy may already solve this easy family. Nor is it a counterexample to existence of some tight low-loss maps. This distinction is essential.

## 9. Disposition

The new per-key charge substitutes compact union geometry for label-frequency tightness where beneficial, preserves the preceding head-palette theorem, and covers diffuse generated maps with vanishing union mass. It leaves unresolved the case of small average loss but many low-frequency differences with large combined support on most keys. Next investigate whether such diffuse union mass can be split by a second conditioning coordinate or by shared-column decomposition without introducing critical width-six transfer or a global logarithmic rate restriction.
