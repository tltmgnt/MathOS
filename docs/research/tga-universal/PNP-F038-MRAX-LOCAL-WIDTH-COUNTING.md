## Constructive track: mixed pigeonhole family

For p pigeons and m holes, let x_ij be Boolean assignment bits. Use the affine equations XOR_j x_ij = 1 for each row, and pairwise exclusion constraints on pairs of bits within each row and each column. In complement coordinates d_ij=1-x_ij, every exclusion is a positive binary clause d_ij OR d_kl, and the affine right-hand sides become 1 XOR (m mod 2). These are explicit polynomial-size systems. The affine subsystem alone is consistent (choose a singleton per row); the positive-clause subsystem alone is consistent (all d=1). Together, row parity and exclusion force exactly one selected hole per pigeon, and column exclusion forces injectivity. Thus SAT iff p<=m. For p=m+1 the conjunction is UNSAT despite both subsystems being separately feasible. Nonhomogeneous equations are allowed here; the claim is not a lower bound for the preceding graph-derived homogeneous subclass.

## TECT-X adversarial theorem: local semantic proof width

Define a pigeon-support semantic inference system. A fact with support S (a set of pigeon indices) is an arbitrary relation on their complete rows of m Boolean bits. Initial facts may be any sound consequences of the affine subsystem restricted to S and all exclusion clauses on S. The independent global affine equations have precisely that projection onto S. An inference may use facts whose combined support U has size <=k, and derive any relation on a subset S of U that is a semantic consequence of their conjunction, optionally including all original constraints on U. This allows exact arbitrary algebraic reasoning, arbitrary computation within U, and projection/forgetting; it does not permit combining facts with a larger union support or global counting over all pigeons. A refutation is an empty relation. This is a specified restricted semantic width system, not resolution or Res(XOR) in general.

Take p=m+1 and 2<=k<=m. For each S of size <=k let J_S be the complete table of injections from S into the m holes, expressed as one-hot rows. These are precisely the solutions to the source constraints supported on S. Every initial fact contains J_S. Inductively, for an inference with combined support U and conclusion S subset U, any injection on S extends to an injection on U because |U|<=m. Its restrictions satisfy all premises by induction; hence its restriction belongs to the conclusion. Consequently every derived relation contains J_S and no refutation exists. The argument is independent of number of rounds, fact count, local computational power or choice of local affine coordinate basis. In particular, a system of overlapping exact tables on every <=m pigeon scope has a nonempty compatible family, whose projections onto every intersection agree, although the global relation is empty. The same family is an invariant against any sound pruning that only uses those projection/extension rules. A refutation in this system requires support width at least m+1. Explicitly materializing the table on m pigeons uses m! rows. This factorial size is a statement about that table representation, not a runtime lower bound for all local procedures or for SAT. For k=1 the pairwise input facts with support two are outside the system; the meaningful comparison starts at k=2.

Topology: J_S forms a compatible family under projection, but no global section exists. Pairwise agreement of projections and extension to any allowed small scope do not guarantee a global witness. The obstruction is cardinality, not a claim of a universal homological invariant.

## Alternative discovery: compact counting certificate with explicit inference rules

Use integer linear inequalities in the original x coordinates. The parity axiom with odd right-hand side implies -sum_j x_ij <= -1 because the all-zero Boolean row violates it. This is a special checked affine-to-counting inference, not an arbitrary oracle for implied inequalities. Each positive binary clause on d supplies x_u+x_v<=1. Use nonnegative integer combinations of previous inequalities and division by a positive integer when every left coefficient is divisible by it, rounding the right side down. These are sound over integer x.

For one column, start with its first two pigeons' pair inequality sum_{i<=2} x_ij<=1. Given sum_{i<=k} x_ij<=1 for k>=2, add k-1 copies of it to the k pair inequalities x_ij+x_(k+1)j<=1. The result is k sum_{i<=k+1} x_ij<=2k-1. Divide by k and round down to obtain sum_{i<=k+1} x_ij<=1. Iterate through p pigeons and each column. Add all column upper bounds and all parity-derived row lower bounds, giving 0<=m-p, an explicit contradiction if p>m. Row exclusion constraints are not needed for this counting refutation, but they make the exact local injection tables transparent.

For p=m+1 the producer writes p odd-row steps, m binom(p,2) column-pair steps, 2m(p-2) combination/division steps and one final sum. The number of lines is O(mp^2+p); the weighted predecessor references and integer encodings have polynomial total length. Dense vectors have polynomial bit size (coefficients at most p in these proofs; the checker performs exact integer arithmetic for supplied traces). The checker reconstructs each inequality from the actual affine/positive-clause input and earlier lines, verifies nonnegative weights and valid divisibility, and accepts only a final zero left side with negative right side. It does not rely on the claimed answer or on a SAT oracle. For p<=m a verified one-hot witness is obtained directly; a proposed negative counting certificate cannot pass. This gives a proof-system separation for this family: local semantic refutation width grows linearly in m, whereas a specific affine-plus-integer-counting calculus has uniformly constructible polynomial certificates. It does not show that every mixed contradiction has such a certificate, nor does it establish a general proof-size lower bound for stronger algebraic systems.
