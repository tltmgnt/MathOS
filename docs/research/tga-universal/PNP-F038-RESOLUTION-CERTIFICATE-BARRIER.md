## Explicit forced-choice family

For a connected simple graph G=(V,E) of maximum degree three, with a designated vertex v0 of degree at most two, use one Boolean variable per edge. At each vertex impose the XOR of incident edge bits equals a charge c_v. At v0 include an additional variable z in the XOR. Encode each parity equation by the clauses excluding its violating assignments (width at most three, at most four clauses per vertex).

Summing all equations over GF(2) cancels the edge variables, yielding z=XOR_v c_v. Conversely, setting z to that parity leaves an even total effective charge. Set all nontree edges to zero and process a rooted spanning tree from leaves to root, setting the edge to the parent equal to the remaining charge at the leaf and updating the parent's charge. The even total makes the root equation hold. Thus the formula is always satisfiable and exactly one branch is satisfiable. On the promised explicit family the selector and witness constructor are polynomial-time, not merely black-box query algorithms. In fact for the graphs below the equation scopes are distinct, so group the CNF clauses by their variable sets, recover each charge from the parity of its forbidden patterns, recover graph incidence from the scope groups, and use the above algorithm. With graph and charge data already given, the algorithm uses O(|V|+|E|) elementary operations. With explicit CNF parsing and sorting, charge polynomial overhead. No ability to recognize arbitrary CNF as XOR is presumed.

## Self-contained width obstruction

Fix the wrong z and simplify the input formula; this is exactly a Tseitin CNF on G with odd total effective charge (no added extension variables). The full collection of vertex equations is inconsistent, but every proper subset is consistent: assign charges arbitrarily at omitted vertices, toggling one omitted charge to make total parity even, and solve on a spanning tree as above.

For any clause C define mu(C) as the minimum number of vertex equations that logically imply C. It exists because the full inconsistent system implies every clause. Initial clauses have mu<=1; the empty clause has mu=|V|. Resolution is sound, so mu(resolvent)<=mu(parent1)+mu(parent2); weakening cannot increase mu. In any resolution refutation with |V|>=4, trace backwards from the empty clause while a predecessor has mu>2|V|/3. The trace terminates at a derived clause with mu>2|V|/3 and no such predecessor. At that inference at least one predecessor has mu>|V|/3 and mu<=2|V|/3. Thus some proof clause C has a minimum support U of vertex equations with |V|/3<=|U|<=2|V|/3.

For every edge e crossing U, suppose its variable is absent from C. Remove its endpoint v in U. Minimality gives an assignment satisfying all equations of U except v and falsifying C. Toggle e if needed to satisfy v; it changes no other equation of U and does not change C. This contradicts implication by U. Hence every crossing edge occurs in C, and width(C)>=|delta(U)|. The reasoning also permits weakening and tautological clauses (the latter have mu=0).

## Explicit degree-three graph family and size transfer

For integer d>=2 start from the d-dimensional hypercube Q_d on N=2^d vertices. Replace each vertex by a path of d ports, one for each coordinate. For each cube edge in coordinate j connect the corresponding ports of its two endpoints. The graph is connected, has M=dN vertices and E=(d-1)N+dN/2 edges, maximum degree three, and an endpoint port v0 of degree two. Therefore its selector CNF has O(dN) variables and clauses.

The cube edge cut of any subset A has size >=min(|A|,N-|A|). One elementary induction splits the cube into equal halves; if the two half-set sizes are a,b, the within-half cuts are at least min(a,N/2-a)+min(b,N/2-b) and the matching cut at least |a-b|. Their sum is at least min(a+b,N-a-b), as seen by the cases a,b on the same or opposite sides of N/4. The base d=1 is immediate.

Let U contain between M/3 and 2M/3 port vertices. Let h be the number of mixed paths (having both in- and out-of-U ports). If h>=N/(12d), each contributes at least one internal cut edge. Otherwise at least N/3-h>=N/4 entire paths are in U, and at least N/4 entire paths outside U. The original cube cut from the full-in paths is at least N/4, and at most dh<N/12 edges go to mixed paths. At least N/6 external edges then cross U. In either case |delta(U)|>=N/(12d). Thus the minimum refutation width of the wrong branch is Omega(N/d).

Invoke the Ben-Sasson--Wigderson size-width theorem (general DAG resolution): for an unsatisfiable CNF with n variables and initial maximum width w0, its minimum refutation size is exp(Omega((w-w0)^2/n)), where w is minimum refutation width. Source: Short Proofs Are Narrow -- Resolution Made Simple, JACM 48(2), 2001, https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/eccc-reports/1999/TR99-022/ and https://www.math.ias.edu/~avi/PUBLICATIONS/ABSTRACT/bw02.pdf . Here n=E=Theta(dN), w0<=3, giving for sufficiently large d the lower bound exp(Omega(N/d^3)). Equivalently, with n the wrong-branch variable count, exp(Omega(n/log^4 n)), superpolynomial in the explicit CNF bit length O(n log n). This is a weaker explicit family bound than standard expander-Tseitin exponential bounds, chosen to make the graph and expansion derivation elementary and self-contained. The only imported asymptotic proof-complexity theorem is the size-width transfer.

Therefore a universally correct selector architecture that is REQUIRED to provide an ordinary resolution refutation of every infeasible rejected branch cannot have a polynomial worst-case output bound. This remains true with arbitrary internal computation, adaptive queries, or nonuniform circuits producing the certificate, if the output is an explicit resolution proof on the original restricted CNF. It is not a lower bound for selectors alone, extended resolution, arbitrary proof systems, or solvers using stronger certified preprocessing. A short parity contradiction certificate exists for this family (sum the vertex equations). The family itself has an efficient explicit-input selector, so the separation is between certification mechanisms, not between P and NP.

## General proof-system boundary

For a fixed sound and complete polynomial-time proof verifier for UNSAT, polynomial proof lengths alone give NP=coNP if they hold universally; no polynomial-time proof search follows. Conversely NP=coNP yields some polynomially bounded proof system. A total uniform polynomial-time proof-producing algorithm for every unsatisfiable input, with a polynomial-time sound verifier, would imply P=NP: run it and accept UNSAT only when the verifier accepts. If P=NP, some such verifier and producer exist (the verifier can itself decide UNSAT and accept an empty certificate). These equivalences quantify over suitable proof systems, not ordinary resolution. Excluding one fixed system cannot settle them.
