## Constructive factorization

Extract the affine equations A and residual CNF B with the previous source partition verifier. Reject inconsistent A by an XOR combination of source rows. Otherwise compute an affine parametrization x=x0+G t of all models of A using reduced row echelon form; t consists of the free original variables. Each coordinate of x is a constant plus a binary linear form g_i(t). For each variable i occurring in B, take its direction row g_i. Their span has dimension r, the projected affine dimension from the preceding note.

Choose a basis of this span greedily from the residual coordinate rows. Express every residual coordinate row uniquely in this basis. Call the resulting coefficient vector h_i. The values z of the basis forms are freely and independently assignable: the basis rows are independent linear functionals, hence the map t -> z is surjective. Each residual literal is evaluated by a constant XOR the parity of h_i dot z (and negated for negative literals). This is exact, not a relaxation or a heuristic variable substitution.

For each residual clause, join all basis coordinates occurring in the support of any of its h_i. Compute connected components by union-find or equivalent bitset merging. Clauses whose support union is empty are constants and are tested separately. Every other clause depends on exactly one component. Let r_j be the number of basis coordinates in component j. Independently enumerate its 2^r_j assignments, checking every assigned clause. If all components and constants succeed, combine local assignments into z, solve the independent basis-value equations in t, reconstruct x and verify the original CNF. If a component has no satisfying assignment, the whole formula is unsatisfiable.

Total computation and certificate construction take poly(L) times (1+sum_j 2^r_j) for explicit input length L, including extraction, elimination, coefficient computation, component construction and witness reconstruction. Preprocessing is polynomial without enumerating the affine space; enumeration is restricted to the individual components. The certificate checker can recompute the polynomial preprocessing deterministically; a negative certificate is either an inconsistent affine combination, a false constant clause, or an exhaustive table of one falsified residual clause index for each assignment of one component. The verifier checks exact table length, component membership and each falsification. Soundness follows from affine parametrization and clause-local dependence; no claimed short certificate for arbitrary r_j. A bound max_j r_j=O(log L) suffices for polynomial time even if r is linear in L's variable count.

## Basis-invariant maximal direct-sum separation

Consider any partition of the nonconstant residual clauses into groups whose spans of direction rows W_j are in direct sum. Every basis row was selected from a residual coordinate row, and each nonzero such row belongs to exactly one W_j (if it occurs in multiple groups, their intersection would be nonzero). The selected basis rows within each W_j span W_j: uniqueness of coordinates in the direct sum implies a vector in W_j cannot involve basis rows from another W_j. Thus all coordinates used by a clause lie in the basis subset for its group. No component produced above crosses such a group. Conversely the produced components have direction spans in direct sum, since their basis-coordinate supports are disjoint. Their spans are exactly the spans of their own basis coordinates: each basis row occurs in at least one residual clause and its representation is a unit vector. Hence the computed clause partition is the finest among decompositions based on direct-sum separation of the original clause direction-row spans. It is independent of the greedy basis choice, except for labels and constants. This is not a claim of maximal separation after arbitrary logical simplifications or other variable transformations.

## Connected-input family

For m>=2 take m groups of three distinct variables a_i,b_i,c_i, with affine equalities a_i=b_i=c_i encoded by two binary clauses per equality and a positive residual clause (a_i OR b_i OR c_i). For consecutive groups add private bridge bits d_i with affine equation d_i=a_i XOR a_(i+1), encoded by four width-three parity clauses. The full original primal graph is connected. The residual clauses have distinct scopes from the extracted parity groups. The projected affine dimension is m because each group's common value is independent and the bridges do not constrain it. The direct-sum components each have dimension one, so only 2m local assignments are examined instead of a global enumeration of 2^m possible interface values. The solver returns the all-one group values and zero bridges. Replacing a residual group clause by its negative counterpart changes its chosen bit but not the decomposition. Adding both positive and negative triples to a group makes it locally infeasible without affecting affine extraction (one forbidden pattern of each parity is present, fewer than the required four). This example illustrates a smaller certified search bound, not a lower bound for the previous adaptive algorithm, which may already solve it efficiently.

## Scope and obstruction

A transformation that is polynomial-time, preserves satisfiability, and always produces a polynomial-length instance in this logarithmic maximum-component-rank domain would imply P=NP by composition. No such transformation is established. Generic explicit 3-CNF is not guaranteed to have the decomposition; affine extraction may be empty and a single component may have dimension n. The result improves the structural solver, not the unrestricted polynomial-clock hypothesis. It also does not assert that the direct-sum components are the only useful decomposition (separator methods or stronger logical deductions may split further).
