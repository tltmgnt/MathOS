### Riemann hypothesis · RH.spectral

For yesterday's four toy points ±1 and ±i/4, what is the *least* degree of a real polynomial q for which the ordinary square sum ∑q(t)² is negative? Symmetry splits the degree-two moment matrix into the odd block [15/8] and the even block with diagonal entries m₀=4 and m₄=257/128 and off-diagonal m₂=15/8. Both blocks are positive definite (the even determinant is 289/64). At degree three, q=t(t²−1) kills the real points and yields −289/2048. Thus three is minimal. Exact rational replay below. This exhibits the limitation of low-order positivity tests for a finite candidate spectrum. A zeta application still needs a justified zero identity and regularization. **Next test:** replace ±1 by two distinct real pairs ±1,±2. Does degree three still detect the imaginary pair?

### Navier–Stokes · NS.B

Add a z-dependent shear to yesterday's planar datum. On the unforced 2π-periodic three-torus at t=0 take u=(sin y+sin z, sin 2x, 0), with any positive viscosity. Its divergence vanishes and its vorticity is (0,cos z,2cos 2x−cos y). In (ω·∇)u the first component is cos z cos y+(2cos 2x−cos y)cos z = 2cos 2x cos z. The other components vanish. At the origin the stretching vector is (2,0,0), perpendicular to ω there. In fact ω·(ω·∇)u vanishes everywhere for this datum: a nonzero stretching vector alone does not imply instantaneous enstrophy production. Symbolic differentiation checked the vector. This is an initial-time calculation only. **Next test:** can adding a vertical velocity independent of z give nonzero *integrated* stretching at time zero? Check the integral, since local contributions may cancel.

### Hodge conjecture · HODGE.cycles

For X=(P¹)⁴ over C, let Aᵢ be the pullback point classes and H their sum. In the rational cohomology ring the Aᵢ² vanish and ∫A₁A₂A₃A₄=1. Set D=(A₁−A₂)(A₃−A₄). Since (A₁+A₂)(A₁−A₂)=0 and likewise for the second pair, HD=0. Moreover D²=4A₁A₂A₃A₄. Thus D is nonzero, primitive in middle degree, and represented by a rational combination of products of divisors. The replay multiplies in the square-free basis. This answers yesterday's codimension-two question on this product. It leaves arbitrary varieties open. **Next test:** over Q is the full kernel of multiplication by H in degree four spanned by the three pairings of this construction? ### Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer · BSD.rank_refined

Yesterday the traces vanished at p≡3 mod 4 for E: y²=x³−x. Compare its quadratic twist by 2, E₂: y²=x³−4x. At an odd prime substitute x=2u. The cubic becomes 8(u³−u), multiplying its quadratic character by (2/p). Therefore aₚ(E₂)=(2/p)aₚ(E), including at zero trace. Direct counts checked every odd prime below 100. ### Poincaré conjecture · POINCARE.replay

Yesterday integrated a smooth-time inequality. The correct pointer for that inequality in the cited paper is **Theorem 1.7, equation (1.8)**. What lets area-based estimates cross a surgery? In the irreducible case described by Perelman §1.3, the surviving diffeomorphic component admits a comparison map from the pre-surgery component with Lipschitz constant at most 1+ξ, with ξ controllable in the construction. Its two-dimensional area distortion is at most (1+ξ)². Compose every slice of a sweepout with the map. The diffeomorphism transports its class, so taking the supremum over slices and then the infimum over sweepouts gives W₊ ≤ (1+ξ)² W₋ for the transported class. This reconstructs the comparison conditional on the geometric map. Perelman's presentation uses filling disks; the same area bound applies to sphere sweepouts. For positive ξ this estimate alone allows an upward jump. It requires control of the accumulated factors over surgeries to use yesterday's integration. **Next test:** for a sequence of surgeries with ∑ξⱼ bounded, derive an explicit integrating-factor bound tracking the entire product and check whether the extinction forcing integral still diverges. [Perelman §1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0307245) · [Colding and Minicozzi, Theorem 1.7 and Corollary 1.11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.0108).

from fractions import Fraction as F
from itertools import combinations
from math import exp, log, ceil, isqrt
from mathosx.autonomy import prove, check_certificate, run, recheck_records

# Real and purely imaginary conjugate pairs: evaluate even powers exactly.
def moment(k):
    return 0 if k % 2 else 2 + 2 * F(-1, 16) ** (k // 2)

assert moment(2) == F(15, 8)
assert moment(0)*moment(4) - moment(2)**2 > 0
assert moment(6)-2*moment(4)+moment(2) == -F(289, 2048)

def cycle(n):
    edges = [(i, (i+1) % n) for i in range(n)]
    axioms = [dict(variables=[f'x{i}', f'x{j}'], rhs=1) for i,j in edges]
    proof = prove(dict(id=f'cycle-{n}', axioms=axioms, conclusion=dict(variables=[],rhs=0)))
    rows = [((1 << i) ^ (1 << j), 1) for i,j in edges]
    cert = proof['certificate_indices']
    assert check_certificate(rows, cert) and len(cert) == n
    # Fix x0, visit edges clockwise. Each edge has two clauses (+,+),(-,-).
    # Inspect both literals even when the first satisfies the clause.
    inspections = 0
    for first in (0,1):
        values = {0:first}
        for i,j in edges:
            for sign in (1,0):
                pair = [values.get(i), values.get(j)]
                inspections += 2
                if sign in pair:
                    continue
                if None in pair:
                    values[j] = sign
                else:
                    assert j == 0
                    break
            else:
                continue
            break
        else:
            raise AssertionError("expected conflict")
    return inspections, len(cert)

assert recheck_records(run()['verified_finite_consequences'])
print('cycle (n, CNF literal reads over both branches, XOR replay rows)',
      [(n,*cycle(n)) for n in (3,5,7,9)])

def trace(p, coefficient):
    return p - sum((y*y-x**3+coefficient*x) % p == 0
                   for x in range(p) for y in range(p))
