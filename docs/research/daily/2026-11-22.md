### Riemann hypothesis · RH.spectral

Yesterday's reflected pair has zeros at s=1/4 and 3/4. Write the spectral coordinate t=(s−1/2)/i. Then t=±i/4 and the power sum of order two is −1/8. For a finite-dimensional self-adjoint operator A the same power sum is tr(A²)=∑‖Aeⱼ‖²≥0 in an orthonormal basis. Thus the toy polynomial fails an explicit necessary positivity check despite its symmetry. A positive second moment alone cannot force reality: adjoining t=±1 makes the sum positive while retaining the imaginary pair. This finite trace argument would require justified regularization and a zero identity to transfer to zeta. The [fixed dilation obstruction](../TECTX_RH_SPECTRAL_DILATION_OBSTRUCTION.md) also excludes a simple spectral candidate by its counting law; its 12 regression tests passed. ### Navier–Stokes · NS.B

Yesterday’s single shear had zero nonlinear term. On the periodic three-torus take the divergence-free initial datum u=(sin y, sin 2x, 0), independent of z. Its vertical vorticity is ω=2cos 2x−cos y. Direct differentiation gives u·∇ω=−3 sin(2x) sin y. In the unforced vorticity equation the nonlinear contribution to ∂ₜω is consequently (3/2)[cos(2x−y)−cos(2x+y)]. Diffusion preserves the original frequencies at time zero; the two new wave vectors (2,±1,0) come from transport. This planar example has zero vortex stretching. ### Hodge conjecture · HODGE.cycles

Move from yesterday’s projective plane to X=P¹×P¹ over C. Let A and B denote fibres of the two projections. Distinct parallel fibres are disjoint and transverse opposite fibres meet once, giving A²=B²=0 and A·B=1. The Segre hyperplane class is H=A+B. The algebraic class D=A−B satisfies H·D=0 but D²=−2, so it is nonzero and lies outside the span of H. This explicitly constructs a primitive rational (1,1) class as a difference of cycles. It is a divisor example; higher codimension is the outstanding difficulty. Next: on (P¹)⁴ test whether (A₁−A₂)(A₃−A₄) is a nonzero primitive codimension-two class for H=∑Aᵢ.

### Poincaré conjecture · POINCARE.replay

Yesterday used the exact radius ODE for the round sphere. For a nonround homotopy sphere, Colding and Minicozzi use a min–max width W. On smooth flow intervals their upper derivative inequality gives d[W(t)(t+C)^(−3/4)]/dt ≤ −4π(t+C)^(−3/4), for a suitable C>0. Integration gives an upper bound W(0)C^(−3/4)−16π[(t+C)^(1/4)−C^(1/4)] for the rescaled width. The right side becomes negative in finite time, whereas a width defined from areas is nonnegative. This reconstructs the extinction comparison once the geometric inequality and surgery control are supplied. It does not reconstruct those hypotheses. Next: identify the precise comparison across a surgery time that permits chaining the smooth-interval estimates.
