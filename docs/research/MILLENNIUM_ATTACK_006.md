# Millennium research attack 006 — the diagonal divisor on a curve self-product

**Date:** November 18, 2026  
**Track:** **Hodge Conjecture**, following MathOS P-versus-NP (003–004) and Riemann (005) attacks.  
**Status:** BOUNDED_RESEARCH / NOT_CERTIFIED / NO_HODGE_SOLUTION.  
**Claim:** A known **obstruction to a naive cycle-generation strategy**, proved on paper from standard intersection-theoretic facts and checked as exact linear algebra. No novelty or full Hodge proof is asserted.

The [Clay Mathematics Institute's official statement](https://www.claymath.org/millennium/hodge-conjecture/) asks whether, on **every smooth complex projective variety** and **in every codimension**, each rational Hodge class is a rational combination of algebraic cycle classes. The **codimension-one** case is already a classical theorem, the [Lefschetz (1,1) theorem](https://encyclopediaofmath.org/wiki/Hodge_conjecture); no aspect of that result is claimed as a MathOS discovery.

## Target a tempting but insufficient approach

A natural proposal is to try to generate all algebraic divisor classes on a product variety solely from divisor classes pulled back from each factor. We test that approach on the product of a smooth complex projective curve with itself. **It fails even for a codimension-one algebraic class on an elliptic-curve product.**

That failure does not refute the Hodge conjecture: the additional class is itself represented by an explicit algebraic subvariety, the diagonal.

## Geometric setup and proof

Let \(C\) be any smooth connected complex projective curve of genus \(g\ge0\), and \(p\in C\) a point. On the smooth projective surface \(X=C\times C\), define the algebraic curves/divisors

\[
F_1=C\times\{p\},\qquad F_2=\{p\}\times C,\qquad
\Delta=\{(x,x):x\in C\}.
\]

**Classical geometric inputs (not formalized by the Lean file):**

1. Each \(F_i\) is a fiber of a product projection with trivial normal bundle. Hence \(F_1^2=F_2^2=0\).
2. Every distinct pair among \(F_1,F_2,\Delta\) meets transversely at the single point \((p,p)\). Thus \(F_1\cdot F_2=F_1\cdot\Delta=F_2\cdot\Delta=1\).
3. Restrict the tangent bundle of \(X\) to the diagonal: \(T_X|_\Delta\simeq T_C\oplus T_C\). The diagonal tangent subbundle is the diagonal \(T_C\); therefore \(N_{\Delta/X}\simeq T_C\). By the self-intersection formula and degree of the canonical bundle,

\[
\Delta^2=\deg N_{\Delta/X}=\deg T_C=2-2g.
\]

Relative to \((F_1,F_2,\Delta)\), the intersection matrix is

\[
M_g=\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&1\\
1&0&1\\
1&1&2-2g
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\det(M_g)=2g.
\]

The determinant identity follows by direct expansion for every nonnegative integer \(g\).

### Theorem: the diagonal is not generated by factor fibers when genus is positive

For \(g\ge1\), the numerical divisor class \([\Delta]\) is not in the rational span of \([F_1]\) and \([F_2]\) in \(N^1(X)_\mathbb Q\). In particular, it cannot be in their span in rational cohomology \(H^2(X,\mathbb Q)\).

**Proof.** Suppose \([\Delta]=a[F_1]+b[F_2]\) for rational \(a,b\). Intersect with \(F_1\) and \(F_2\):

\[
1=\Delta\cdot F_1=b,\qquad
1=\Delta\cdot F_2=a.
\]

So \(a=b=1\). Intersect with \(\Delta\):

\[
2-2g=\Delta^2
=(F_1+F_2)\cdot\Delta=2.
\]

This forces \(g=0\), contradicting \(g\ge1\). Thus no such rational combination exists numerically. A cohomological relation would imply the same intersection relations and is also impossible. \(\square\)

Indeed, \(\det(M_g)=2g\ne0\) for \(g\ge1\), so all three classes are independent over \(\mathbb Q\).

### Genus-zero contrast

For \(g=0\), \(C\simeq\mathbb P^1\). The diagonal on \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\) has divisor bidegree \((1,1)\), so its class **does** equal \([F_1]+[F_2]\). The matrix determinant vanishes and the formal null vector is \((1,1,-1)\). The computed null vector alone is **not** a general proof of geometric equivalence: the genus-zero equivalence also uses the known divisor-class structure of \(\mathbb P^1\times\mathbb P^1\).

## Formal and computational scope

[HodgeDiagonalIntersections.lean](../../formal/HodgeDiagonalIntersections.lean) explicitly encodes the **genus-one intersection matrix as input**, not a formal definition of a smooth variety, Chow group, Hodge structure or intersection theory. It proves the following **linear-algebra consequences**:

- *MathOS.elliptic_intersections_detect_zero*: zero pairings against three generators imply all integer coefficients vanish.
- *MathOS.elliptic_diagonal_not_in_fiber_span*: after clearing denominators, no nonzero multiple of the diagonal matches an integer combination of the fibers on all three pairings.
- *MathOS.elliptic_intersection_matrix_determinant*: the genus-one determinant is exactly 2.
- *MathOS.genus_zero_null_vector*: the genus-zero model has the indicated degeneracy.

The Lean file does **not** verify the underlying geometric intersection numbers, the existence of a Hodge class or the full Hodge conjecture.

The standard-library-only [hodge_diagonal_006.py](../../mathos/research/hodge_diagonal_006.py) checks exact integer matrices for genus 0 through a requested bound, computes \(\det(M_g)=2g\), verifies rational pairings, and reports the forced fiber coefficients \(a=b=1\) and third-pairing discrepancy \(2g\). The [adversarial tests](../../tests/test_hodge_diagonal_006.py) cover rational scaling, genre-zero degeneracy, positivity of the determinant for positive genus and explicit no-prize-claim fields.

## Why this matters without solving Hodge

The naive strategy—**pull back factor divisors and assume they generate all divisor classes on products**—misses the diagonal even for an elliptic curve product. Products can require additional geometrically meaningful cycles and correspondences, already in the easy codimension-one case.

This **does not** contradict the Hodge conjecture: the diagonal is itself algebraic, and the codimension-one Hodge conjecture is already proved by Lefschetz. The full rational conjecture concerns potentially primitive classes in higher codimension on **all** smooth projective varieties. The MathOS obligations *HODGE.primitive_cycles* and *HODGE.reduction* remain OPEN.
