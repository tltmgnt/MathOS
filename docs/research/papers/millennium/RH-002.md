# Arbitrarily delayed polynomial detection of nonreal pairs in finite symmetric spectra

**MathOS Millennium milestone RH-002 · 2026-11-24 · Unreviewed bounded technical note**

## Abstract

For m≥1, S={±1,…,±m,±iε}. Can the minimum degree of a real polynomial with negative ordinary square sum grow with m? Exact cases m=1,…,6 produce minimum degrees 3,5,7,9,11,13. ε=1/2 works through m=5; ε=1/4 works for m=6. ## 2. Mathematical argument and method

Write q=e+o by parity. The cross terms cancel in the sum over S, and the even part has real values on S, so its square sum is nonnegative. If deg q≤2m, write o(t)=t p(t²), deg p≤m−1. Let ℓ_j be the Lagrange basis on nodes j². Interpolation gives p(−ε²)=Σ_j ℓ_j(−ε²)p(j²). Weighted Cauchy–Schwarz bounds ε²p(−ε²)² by B(ε)Σ_j j²p(j²)², where B(ε)=ε²Σ_j ℓ_j(−ε²)²/j². B is continuous and B(0)=0, hence B<1 for sufficiently small ε. Then the odd square sum is nonnegative. The degree-(2m+1) polynomial t∏(t²−j²) vanishes at the real points and has a strictly negative square sum on ±iε. This proves the minimum is exactly 2m+1 under B<1. The code halves rational ε until the strict bound is certified.
