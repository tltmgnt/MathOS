## 2. Mathematical argument and method

Let S={±1,±2,±i/4}, and Q(q)=Σ_{z∈S} q(z)^2 for real polynomials q. Symmetry cancels cross terms between even and odd parts. Even polynomials take real values at every point, so their square sums are nonnegative. For odd q of degree ≤3, Q is governed by the 2×2 Gram matrix with entries m2,m4,m6, where m_{2j}=2(1+2^{2j}+(-1/16)^j). Its first diagonal entry is 79/8>0 and determinant is 130267/1024>0, so it is positive definite. Hence all q of degree ≤4 have Q(q)≥0. The degree-five q(t)=t(t²−1)(t²−4) kills every real point; its square at i/4 is strictly negative and its value at −i/4 is the conjugate. This gives the exact minimum degree.
