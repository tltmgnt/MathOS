## 2. Mathematical argument and method

Use n=4k+1 or 4k+3, n≥5; edge e_i=(i,i+1 mod n). Visit odd interior edge indices first in ascending order, then even interior indices, then e_0,e_(n−1). Count two literal reads per clause; positive clause first; branch on x0=0 and 1. After the first full scan, vertices 0,1,n−1 are assigned. While fronts are separated, each full scan extends both fronts by two vertices: the odd edge assigns the first new vertex and the subsequent even edge assigns the next. Thus after j full scans the assigned intervals are [0,2j−1] and [n−2j+1,n−1], up to the final meeting. For n=4k+1, after k full scans the two central vertices are unassigned; the odd block assigns both, and conflict occurs at e_2k, the 3k-th edge in the order. For n=4k+3, after k full scans four central vertices remain. The next scan assigns the outer two in its odd block and inner two in its even block, leaving central odd edge e_(2k+1) already visited. Conflict occurs there in the following scan, at position k+1. Each full scan costs 4n reads per branch. For a conflict at edge position h, the two branches together cost 8(h−1)+6 reads on that partial scan because complementing the branch swaps the failing clause. Total reads are 8nk+24k−2 for n=4k+1 and 8n(k+1)+8k+6 for n=4k+3. Both are 2n²+O(n). A queue-based inequality propagator can visit the two incident edges once per assigned vertex, at most 4n edge visits over both branches; this comparison is an edge-visit bound, not the same literal-read metric.
