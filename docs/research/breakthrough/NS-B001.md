# A concentration obstruction to subcubic enstrophy control

**NS-B001 | Breakthrough cycle 1 | 24 November 2026 | Unreviewed technical note**

## 1. Direct target and verdict

Target: NS.critical_bound, route NS.B (unforced periodic three-dimensional Navier–Stokes). The candidate shortcut was a universal instantaneous inequality E'(0) ≤ C(1+E(0)^α) for some α<3, with C allowed to depend on viscosity, domain and a fixed upper bound M on initial kinetic energy, but not on the datum otherwise. The result below falsifies that candidate. It does not falsify global smoothness or imply a singularity.

## 2. Exact localized positive-production seed

Write E=∫|ω|², D=∫|∇ω|², S=∫ω·(ω·∇)u and K=∫|u|²; integrals are unnormalized. The periodic identity E'/2=S−νD holds for smooth unforced solutions. Start with the smooth 2π-periodic mean-zero field U=(sin y+sin z, sin 2x, sin x+sin(x+y)). Its divergence vanishes. Set a=∂x U₃, b=∂y U₃, C=cos 2x, d=cos y, Z=cos z. Then ω=(b,Z−a,2C−d), and direct multiplication gives ω·(ω·∇)U=bZ(6C−d)−ab(2C+d). The first term averages to zero in z; ab=cos x cos(x+y)+cos²(x+y), whose pairings with 2C and d are respectively zero and 1/4. Therefore the mean stretching of U is −1/4, and that of V=−U is +1/4.

On R³, extend V periodically. Since it has finitely many nonzero divergence-free Fourier modes, the periodic vector potential A=curl(−Δ)⁻¹V satisfies curl A=V and is smooth with all derivatives bounded. For R=2πN choose a smooth product cutoff χ_R equal to one on [−R,R]³ and zero outside [−R−1,R+1]³, with derivatives through order three bounded independently of R. Define W_R=curl(χ_R A). This field is compactly supported, smooth and divergence-free. In the inner cube it equals V; in the outer layer the velocity and derivatives through order two are uniformly bounded by the product rule. The volume of that layer is O(R²). Hence S(W_R)=(2R)³/4+O(R²)>0 for sufficiently large R. The bulk identity follows because the inner cube is a union of whole periods. Fix one such W=W_R. Then E(W), D(W), K(W) are finite, and E(W)>0.

## 3. The concentration theorem

For any fixed viscosity ν>0 choose b₀>νD(W)/S(W), and write F=b₀W. Thus c=S(F)−νD(F)>0. Embed the rescaled field u_λ(x)=λF(λ(x−x₀)) into the fixed 2π-periodic torus for sufficiently large λ, with the entire support strictly inside one fundamental cell. It is smooth, divergence-free and mean-zero (a compactly supported curl). By changing variables,

K(u_λ)=λ⁻¹K(F); E(u_λ)=λE(F);

S(u_λ)=λ³S(F); D(u_λ)=λ³D(F).

For the local smooth solution with this initial datum, E'(0)=2cλ³. For any M>0, K(u_λ)≤M for all sufficiently large λ. Thus for any fixed C₀≥0 and real α<3, the ratio 2cλ³/[1+(λE(F))^α] diverges to infinity. In fact E'(0)=2cE(0)³/E(F)³ along this family. No universal subcubic instantaneous bound with those dependencies can hold. The proof uses concentration of admissible smooth initial fields; the argument is not a time evolution or a blow-up construction. Cubic sharpness has prior art (Lu and Doering, 2008); this is a project-specific reconstruction and an additional energy-budget audit, not a literature novelty claim.

## 4. Alternative conditional continuation lemma

Let C_S be a valid periodic mean-zero Sobolev constant, ||ω||₆≤C_S||∇ω||₂. At time t split the Frobenius magnitude g=|∇u| at a measurable threshold B(t)≥0. Hölder gives S≤B(t)E+||g·1_{g>B(t)}||_{3/2} C_S²D. If the tail norm is at most ν/(2C_S²) almost everywhere and ∫₀ᵀB(t)dt is finite, then E'/2≤B E−νD/2 and Gronwall yields E(t)≤E(0)exp(2∫₀ᵗB). A uniform enstrophy bound then supplies continuation by standard periodic strong-solution theory. This is conditional: the critical L^(3/2) strain-tail smallness and an integrable threshold are not known for arbitrary admissible evolution. Concentrated data above make it unsafe to assume such a condition follows from kinetic energy alone. No attempt is made to assert the necessary time-uniform estimate from this inequality.
