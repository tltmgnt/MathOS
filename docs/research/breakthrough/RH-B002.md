# A coercive local Weil operator and the first prime-coupling obstruction

**MathOS RH-B002 | Breakthrough cycle 2 | 24 November 2026 | Unreviewed technical note**

## Abstract

After RH-B001 ruled out finite-order prime-diagonal Fredholm regularization, we move to the actual, unconditional Riemann–Weil explicit formula. An elementary analytical estimate establishes Q(f)≥(2/5)||f||₂² for all smooth complex tests supported in a log-coordinate interval of length 1/1024. The form has a fixed semibounded self-adjoint realization on each bounded window. An explicit negative mixed term for two short windows separated by log 2 demonstrates why local positivity is not additive. The local positivity result is substantially weaker than established small-support results; this note makes no literature novelty or RH solution claim.

## 1. Definitions and exact arithmetic form

For f∈C_c^∞(R), use q(z)=∫f(x)e^(izx)dx, h(z)=q(z) conjugate(q(conjugate(z))), and R_f(r)=∫f(x+r) conjugate(f(x))dx. On the real axis h=|q|² and its Fourier transform with negative exponent equals 2πR_f. In the classical explicit formula, write the nontrivial zeros of completed xi as ρ=1/2+iz_ρ, including all multiplicities and complex z_ρ. Let Q(f)=Σ_ρ h(z_ρ). The formula, with the even part of h if needed by symmetry, gives

Q(f)=h(i/2)+h(−i/2)+(1/(2π))∫_R w(t)|q(t)|²dt−Σ_(n≥2) Λ(n)/√n [R_f(log n)+R_f(−log n)],

where w(t)=Re ψ(1/4+it/2)−log π and Λ is the von Mangoldt function. All expressions are well defined for these compact smooth tests; their Fourier transforms decay rapidly in fixed horizontal strips. The formula does not assume RH, and the zero sum includes possible off-line zeros. Translation of f leaves h and R_f unchanged, so one may center a single support interval at zero.

## 2. Explicit elementary positivity on a short window

For a=1/4 and real y, the classical digamma series gives

Re ψ(a+iy)−ψ(a)=Σ_(n≥0) y²/[(n+a)((n+a)²+y²)].

Every summand is nonnegative and increases with |y|. The exact quarter-value formula ψ(1/4)=−γ−π/2−3log 2, together with γ<1, π/2<1.6, log 2<0.7 and log π<1.2, yields w(t)>−6 everywhere. At |t|≥256, y=|t|/2≥128, the first 64 rational terms at y=128 already sum to more than 7 (exact arithmetic certificate in the accompanying code). Thus w(t)>1 in the high-frequency region.

Let L=1/1024 and support f in a centered interval of length L. By Cauchy–Schwarz |q(t)|²≤L||f||₂²; by Plancherel ∫|q|²/(2π)=||f||₂². The low-band Fourier mass for |t|≤256 is at most (256L/π)||f||₂². Using the two bounds on w, the multiplier term is therefore at least [1−7·256L/π]||f||₂². Each |q(±i/2)|≤√L exp(L/4)||f||₂; hence the absolute value of the two pole terms is at most 2L exp(L/2)||f||₂²<4L||f||₂². All prime terms vanish because |log n|≥log 2>L and R_f is supported in [−L,L].

Combining the estimates and using π>3 proves

Q(f)≥[1−7·256L/3−4L]||f||₂²=(317/768)||f||₂²>(2/5)||f||₂².

This is a bound on the actual arithmetic Weil form, not a finite Gram approximation or synthetic spectrum. The known small-support positivity range in prior literature is much larger than the interval used here.

## 3. Fixed-window self-adjoint realization

Fix any bounded interval I and identify L²(I) with its zero extension in L²(R). By the classical digamma asymptotics, w(t)=log(|t|/(2π))+O(1/|t|) at large |t|. The shifted positive Fourier multiplier form with weight w+7 is closed on the weighted Fourier-norm domain of zero-extended functions; this follows from completeness of weighted L² and the continuous embedding into ordinary L². Restrict initially to C_c^∞(I) and take its closure in this form norm (no assertion is needed that it is the entire maximal weighted domain). This is densely defined in L²(I).

The pole terms are bounded quadratic forms since the interval is bounded. Only finitely many prime-power shifts contribute, and each translation compressed to I has operator norm at most one. They therefore form a bounded self-adjoint perturbation of the multiplier form. The standard representation theorem for closed semibounded symmetric forms produces a fixed self-adjoint operator A_I. For the short window above, A_I≥(2/5)I in the quadratic-form sense by closure.

Existence of A_I is not a Hilbert–Pólya identification: its eigenvalues have not been identified with zeta-zero parameters. For arbitrary window length its positivity is not established.

## 4. Prime coupling between two short windows

Take the centered interval of length L as above and translate f by d=log 2, writing g(x)=f(x−d). Their supports are disjoint. Denote by Q(f,g) the polarized sesquilinear form (linear in f). The off-diagonal archimedean kernel at nonzero separation r is

−Σ_(n≥0) exp[−(2n+1/2)|r|]=−exp(−|r|/2)/(1−exp(−2|r|)).

Indeed the constant terms in the digamma partial sums vanish between disjoint supports by Plancherel; the inverse Fourier kernel of 4(n+1/4)/[t²+4(n+1/4)²] is exp[−2(n+1/4)|r|]. Termwise passage follows from dominated convergence in the Fourier pairing of the smooth tests (partial multipliers grow at most logarithmically plus a constant), or from uniform convergence on the separated supports. The two poles give kernel 2cosh(r/2).

The distance r between the supports satisfies 1/2<|r|<1, using 1/2<log 2<0.7 and the chosen L. Thus the archimedean kernel has absolute value <2 and the pole kernel <4. Their mixed contribution has absolute value at most 6L||f||₂² by Cauchy–Schwarz. Among the prime-power shifts only the shift at log 2 contributes to Q(f,g), exactly −(log 2)/√2 ||f||₂². The other shifts miss both supports because log 3−log 2>1/3. Since (log 2)/√2>1/4,

Re Q(f,g)≤(−1/4+6L)||f||₂²<0 for f≠0.

Hence Q(f+g)=Q(f)+Q(g)+2Re Q(f,g) is strictly less than the sum of the local positive energies. This does not prove that Q(f+g) is negative.

## 5. The remaining gluing lemma

For separated windows with positive diagonal realizations A and B bounded below, the cross form is a bounded operator C (smooth separated kernel plus finitely many shifts). Block positivity is equivalent to ||A^(-1/2) C B^(-1/2)||≤1: substitute x=A^(1/2)f, y=B^(1/2)g into the form and apply the standard two-block Schur criterion. Sufficiency follows from 2|⟨x,Ty⟩|≤||x||²+||y||²; necessity by testing x=−Ty and the density of the form ranges. It is a concrete conditional criterion, not a demonstrated bound on C for arbitrary intervals. Adjacent windows require additional treatment of the singular kernel. A strictly positive uniform lower bound on all support lengths is not assumed.

The next mathematical attack is to estimate the genuine normalized cross operators, including prime shifts and the archimedean kernel, for expanding intervals, or establish where a proposed gluing bound fails. RH.operator and RH.zero_identity remain open, as does global Weil positivity.

Executed exact rational checks: the 64-term digamma partial sum exceeds 7; the coercivity floor and strict negative cross upper bound hold; five finite Schur-complement control matrices agree with the exact determinant criterion. The matrix fixtures are algebraic controls, not measured Weil spectra. The derivations rely on classical theorems; no machine-checked analytic proof or independent review is claimed.
