# Four-window Weil coupling defeats a fixed-floor gluing certificate

**MathOS RH-B003 | Breakthrough cycle 3 | 24 November 2026 | Unreviewed technical note**

## Abstract

The true Weil quadratic form is locally coercive on sufficiently short intervals, as reconstructed in RH-B002 using the unconditional explicit formula. We now derive a four-window obstruction to a particular global gluing certificate: replace each local diagonal form by the previously guaranteed lower bound 317/768 times the norm squared, then control the separated cross terms. On four translates of one short test spaced by log 2, the actual off-diagonal form is already too large for this replacement. This is not a negative direction for the actual Weil form and not a disproof of RH.

## 1. Setup and definitions

Use the Fourier convention and unconditional Riemann–Weil explicit formula from RH-B002. Fix L=1/1024, any nonzero complex f∈C_c^∞(−L/2,L/2), and f_j(x)=f(x−j log 2), j=0,1,2,3. The four supports are disjoint. Put M_jk=Q(f_j,f_k)/||f||₂² for the polarized Weil form Q (linear in its first argument), so the diagonal is a common real number d=Q(f)/||f||₂². Prior work gives d≥c₀=317/768. It is not an equality or an upper bound.

## 2. Exact cross-prime contributions

For distinct j,k the distance between support centers is m log 2 with m=|j−k|∈{1,2,3}. The prime-power sum has only the shift n=2^m contributing, because for n≠2^m the logarithmic distance from log(2^m) is at least log(1+1/8)>1/9>L. For example the inequality log(1+x)>x/(1+x) for x>0 follows by integration. The shift pairs the translated copies exactly. Its normalized contribution to M_jk is therefore −c_m with c_m=log 2/2^(m/2), since Λ(2^m)=log 2.

The smooth separated archimedean kernel is −exp(−|r|/2)/(1−exp(−2|r|)); the pole kernel is 2cosh(r/2). As 1/2<|r|<3 for these supports, their absolute values are bounded by 2 and 6 respectively. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the normalized smooth remainder by 8L=1/128. The simple bounds log 2>2/3 and √2<3/2 yield c_1>2/5, c_2>1/3, c_3>2/9. Hence Re M_jk<−b_m, where

b_1=2/5−1/128, b_2=1/3−1/128, b_3=2/9−1/128.

These bounds are uniform over all nonzero smooth f in the chosen short interval and do not require information about individual zeta zeros.

## 3. Failure of the fixed-floor certificate

Let C be the actual 4×4 Hermitian off-diagonal matrix (zero diagonal, entries M_jk for j≠k). For the unit constant vector v=(1,1,1,1)/2,

v*Cv < −(1/2)(3b_1+2b_2+b_3)=−5881/5760.

The rational magnitude is greater than c₀=317/768. In particular the matrix c₀I+C has a strictly negative quadratic form along v. Even retaining the exact off-diagonal terms cannot make the replacement diagonal c₀ sufficient. The corresponding absolute row-sum certificate also fails: an interior row has sum at least 2b_1+b_2=2131/1920>c₀.

The genuine matrix is dI+C, not c₀I+C. The actual d may be considerably larger than c₀, and may compensate the cross terms. No negative direction for the full Weil form follows. This result eliminates one sufficient-certificate strategy, not global positivity or any other sharper local-to-global approach.

## 4. Research decision

Future work must use the actual frequency-dependent local operator A, rather than replacing it by a fixed scalar floor, and must exploit cancellations or relative bounds in normalized cross-window operators. Adjacent supports also require singular-kernel treatment. Existing small-support positivity literature is stronger than our local scalar estimate. After three attempts, the recommended decision is to pause this narrow gluing strategy pending a substantive new relative-form estimate and rotate the active challenger. RH.operator and RH.zero_identity remain open; no spectral correspondence with multiplicities has been obtained.
