# Excluding the compact concentrating path by the actual momentum equation

**MathOS NS-B003 | Breakthrough cycle 3 | 24 November 2026 | Unreviewed technical note**

## Abstract

The concentrating path constructed in NS-B002 satisfies both global scalar balances but has not been shown to satisfy the actual Navier–Stokes equations. We now exclude that exact path as a nonzero unforced periodic solution using classical positive-time spatial analyticity. A second necessary condition follows from harmonic pressure moments. The results identify structural restrictions not captured by the two scalar balances. They do not exclude noncompact or evolving concentrations, and do not prove global regularity.

## 1. Statement and assumptions

Let F be a nonzero smooth compactly supported divergence-free vector field on R³. Fix a,ν>0 and λ₀ sufficiently large so that the dilated support fits strictly inside a fundamental cell of the 2π-periodic torus. For λ'=aλ³ consider u(t,x)=λ(t)F(λ(t)(x−x₀)) on its finite preconcentration interval, periodically extended. The registered objective is NS.critical_bound (route NS.B). We prove this exact path cannot be a smooth unforced Navier–Stokes solution on any nonempty open time interval. The NS-B002 amplitude selection is a special case.

## 2. Use of actual PDE regularization

The classical spatial analyticity theorem for strong periodic Navier–Stokes solutions with zero force and positive viscosity (Foias–Temam Gevrey regularity) gives real analyticity of the velocity at positive times after a smooth initial slice, locally in the smooth lifespan. If the proposed path were an exact smooth solution, choose an interior time and apply that local result starting from an earlier slice. At that time the velocity vanishes on a nonempty open subset outside its dilated support. The identity theorem for real analytic functions on the connected torus implies it is identically zero, contradicting F≠0. This is an application of an established theorem, not a new analyticity theorem. It excludes even an arbitrarily short positive interval of exact compact-profile dynamics. It does not furnish a quantitative bound on near-compact concentration.

## 3. A separate pressure-moment constraint

Suppose the same smooth path satisfies the actual momentum equation. At an interior time the pressure is constant outside the support's enclosing cube, since the time derivative, convection and Laplacian vanish there and hence grad p=0 there. The cube may be chosen strictly inside a cell; its exterior on the torus is connected. Subtract the exterior constant and lift the velocity and pressure to compactly supported smooth functions on R³ in that cell.

Writing u for that time slice, take the divergence of the actual equation to obtain −Δp=Σ_ij ∂i∂j(u_i u_j). For any harmonic polynomial h of degree two, compact support and two integrations by parts give

0=−∫p Δh=Σ_ij ∫u_i u_j ∂i∂j h.

Choosing h=x_i x_j (i≠j) forces the integrated off-diagonal velocity products to vanish; choosing h=x_i²−x_j² forces all three integrated diagonal squares to be equal. Thus the integrated tensor ∫u⊗u is (K/3)I, where K=∫|u|². The scaling of this tensor is λ⁻¹∫F⊗F, so the same isotropy is necessary for F. Scalar balance identities alone do not include these directional conditions. Isotropy is not sufficient for a solution, and is redundant once the exact compact-support path is excluded by analyticity. It is a separate diagnostic for attempts to approximate such profiles with actual pressure fields.

## 4. Profile residual and the outstanding gap

Differentiating the scaled velocity and applying the spatial chain rule yields, in rescaled coordinates y=λ(x−x₀), the momentum terms λ³[a(F+y·grad F)+(F·grad)F−νΔF]. The pressure gradient must cancel this residual. Its curl has the same compact support and scales as λ⁴. The scalar energy and enstrophy balance checks do not test the vanishing of that entire curl. The actual smooth equation forbids an exactly compact moving profile, but a sequence of analytic profiles may concentrate while its analyticity radius tends to zero. No uniform radius estimate, residual coercivity theorem, or universal L⁴_t L⁶_x estimate follows from qualitative analyticity.

## 5. Evidence and limitations

The workflow ran eight algebraic exponent examples and four elementary diagonal tensor moment controls, plus candidate-intake checks. These are regression checks, not measured pressure moments, PDE simulations, or machine verification of the analyticity theorem. The analytical arguments above are the basis for the scoped conclusion. The executed metadata dossier used an alternative stationary elliptic-regularity explanation; this paper uses the directly sourced periodic positive-time theorem instead, without relying on that alternative. Both are qualitative routes, not global continuation estimates.

Next objective if the track is resumed: derive quantitative rigidity for approximate concentrating profiles, with constants controlled by available norms rather than the unknown critical norm. The three-attempt review recommends pausing this narrow self-similar compact-profile route and rotating effort to another active obligation.
