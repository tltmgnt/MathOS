# A zero-free barrier for finite-order prime Fredholm regularization

**RH-B001 | Breakthrough cycle 1 | 24 November 2026 | Unreviewed technical note**

## 1. Direct target and verdict

Target: RH.zero_identity, route RH.spectral. Earlier MathOS work established the ordinary prime-diagonal Fredholm identity det(I−A_s)=1/ζ(s) in Re s>1 and its trace-class boundary. Here we test whether finite-order Schatten regularization repairs that route across the critical line. The answer is negative for this family: every such regularized determinant is holomorphic and nonvanishing throughout its own Schatten half-plane. This is a scoped operator-theoretic obstruction, not a proof or disproof of RH.

## 2. Independently defined self-adjoint operator

Let H on ℓ² of the rational primes be multiplication by log p with maximal domain D(H)={x: Σ_p(log p)²|x_p|²<∞}. The real diagonal maximal multiplication operator is self-adjoint, with compact resolvent because log p→∞. No zeta zero location is used. For s=σ+it, σ>0, set A_s=exp(−sH), the diagonal operator with eigenvalues a_p=p^(−s). It is compact and ||A_s||=2^(−σ)<1. For integer k≥1, its singular values are p^(−σ), so A_s belongs to Schatten class S_k iff kσ>1: convergence follows from the integer p-series and divergence at or below one follows from the classical divergent sum of reciprocals of primes.

## 3. Exact analytic obstruction for every k

Define D_k(s)=det_k(I−A_s) by its usual diagonal product with factors (1−a_p)exp(Σ_{j=1}^{k−1}a_p^j/j). Since |a_p|<1, each logarithm is given by the analytic power series, and

log D_k(s)=−Σ_p Σ_{j=k}^∞ p^(−js)/j.

For a compact subset of σ>1/k, use its minimum σ₀>1/k and bound the absolute series by Σ_p p^(−kσ₀)/[k(1−2^(−σ₀))], which converges. Normal convergence proves the log is holomorphic there. Its exponential is therefore holomorphic and nonzero on the entire half-plane σ>1/k. The reciprocal is likewise holomorphic and nonzero. The omitted-prime log tail beyond integer P≥2 has the explicit bound P^(1−kσ)/[k(kσ−1)(1−2^(−σ))], by comparison with the integer tail integral. This is an analytic bound; finite float evaluations are only regression checks.

On the common domain σ>1/k, subtracting the two logarithms gives D_(k+1)/D_k=exp(P(ks)/k), where P(z)=Σ_p p^(−z) for Re z>1 is the prime zeta function. Thus changing finite regularization order in their common domain only multiplies by a nonvanishing analytic factor. On σ>1, the Euler product also yields

D_k(s)=ζ(s)⁻¹ exp(Σ_{j=1}^{k−1}P(js)/j).

One cannot simply continue the separate prime-zeta counterterms as single-valued holomorphic functions everywhere: their analytic continuation and possible singularities require independent analysis. The displayed Euler-region factorization is not claimed outside that domain.

For k≥3 the domain contains the critical line. It is classical that ζ has zeros on the critical line. No D_k or its reciprocal for this prime-diagonal heat family can reproduce the corresponding zero divisor of completed xi on its domain. Any additional scalar factor restoring that divisor must carry the missing zeros; multiplying by a holomorphic nonvanishing factor cannot fix the mismatch. This rules out the proposed finite-order repair, not all arithmetic operators. Nor does it rule out a different identity involving an independently justified nontrivial correction.

## 4. Executed checks and next decision

Four finite complex parameter cases with prime cutoff 200 were executed, including three in the critical strip for higher orders. The checks compare a direct eigenvalue product, a power-series logarithm, the adjacent-order correction, and an omitted-prime tail bound. There are 12 grouped floating controls. They are not interval certificates, a numerical zero search, or a proof of the infinite identity. The proof is the convergence and nonvanishing argument above.

Next: stop increasing the regularization order for this diagonal family. Investigate whether the archimedean and prime contributions in a genuine Weil explicit-formula quadratic form can be represented by a fixed operator and establish positivity without assuming RH. A global zero correspondence with correct multiplicities and a spectral reality implication remain unproved. RH.zero_identity and RH.operator stay open.
