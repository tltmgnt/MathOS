# TGA-PROCTOR-501 — Mathematical Research Proctor Curriculum

**MathOS Curriculum Academy | Advanced professional preparation | 12 weeks | 96–120 hours**

## Course charter

Prepare a proctor to supervise the MathOS TGA full-target agent, audit mathematical claims and evidence, administer fair learner assessments and retain an accountable research trail. This is a new proposed course, complementary to the existing 24-week Agentic Clay research apprenticeship, not an accredited qualification or a certification of a Millennium proof. The learner may be a mathematician, research engineer or supervisor. Entry requires the proof-analysis course or documented equivalent, Python 3.11, basic proof literacy and one relevant mathematical specialization. An instructor may document an equivalent preparation route. The AI can give practice feedback; an accountable human proctor must assess actual learner work. Independent expert review is nonblocking for research continuation, not a reason to fabricate proof or learner validation.

## Weekly delivery

Each week: 2 hours reading and seminar; 3–4 hours supervised practical work; 2 hours independent calculation; 1–2 hours written evidence and feedback. Weeks 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 and 12 include a module assessment. Provide accessible written/oral alternatives, extended time where appropriate, and an equivalent no-code demonstration with a provided machine report when programming is not the assessed skill. Exact algorithm execution is still required for the practical capstone; a supervised operator may assist with access. Learner records stay private. Do not commit names, grades, protected assessment prompts or secrets.

### Week 1 — Target literacy
Outcome: Distinguish official problem, modeled route, open leaf and scoped result.

Lab: Extract RH and BSD core statements from CLAY_FULL_TARGETS and U003; identify quantifiers and the refined BSD distinction.

Required evidence: Two target cards with exact assumptions, source locator, missing theorem and one non-implication.

Proctor boundary: Reject any claim that all seven problems are currently unsolved; Poincaré is externally established.

### Week 2 — AND/OR graph semantics
Outcome: Compute minimal completion and blocking sets by hand and by code.

Lab: Use A AND B OR C as a toy route, then run joint_gap and compare hand-derived counts.

Required evidence: Route graph, eight modeled joint sets, seven common leaves, minimal transversal explanation.

Proctor boundary: Do not interpret modeled counts as mathematical necessity or percent solved.

### Week 3 — Proof anatomy and quantifiers
Outcome: Locate an unsupported inference and state its exact missing hypothesis.

Lab: Trace the U002 tail-Hankel implication and identify the unbounded d quantifier; contrast a fixed finite test.

Required evidence: Numbered proof outline, dependency labels and one repaired statement.

Proctor boundary: Finite positive matrices cannot certify every dimension.

### Week 4 — Adversarial mathematics
Outcome: Produce a mathematically decisive negative control for a supplied claim.

Lab: Reproduce U003 synthetic negative Hankel form and the Kummer identical-pairing control; explain scope.

Required evidence: Exact calculation, expected output, observed output and falsification boundary.

Proctor boundary: Synthetic spectra are not actual xi zero data; square of a Hodge class does not imply its algebraicity.

### Week 5 — Agent role supervision
Outcome: Evaluate planner, deriver, adversary and synthesizer outputs independently of agreement.

Lab: Run offline TGA agent once; annotate four role handoffs and draft an adversarial critique of a proposed lemma.

Required evidence: Role trace, critique, remaining gap and suggested next attack.

Proctor boundary: Offline fixture is not live AI research; no model agreement establishes proof.

### Week 6 — Tool requests and exact evidence
Outcome: Validate finite mathematical requests and reproduce results independently.

Lab: Use check_request for a rational determinant, Hankel form and CNF; include malformed request and oversize rejection.

Required evidence: Inputs, exact answers, rejection controls and explicit evidence scope.

Proctor boundary: Do not execute model-authored commands; a bounded UNSAT result is not a general lower bound.

### Week 7 — Checkpoint and provenance
Outcome: Detect inconsistent source hashes, state transitions, calls and candidate classifications.

Lab: Resume an offline batch; inspect state.json; use proctor audit on a copied checkpoint and mutate one evidence field.

Required evidence: Pinned commit, file digests, call accounting, accepted structural audit and rejected mutation.

Proctor boundary: Do not modify original research records or public proof register; a hash is not authorship authentication.

### Week 8 — Deployment and incident control
Outcome: Configure budgeted research and recover safely from failure.

Lab: Explain main-only live workflow and secret handling; simulate interrupted and malformed-response attempts using offline tests.

Required evidence: Runbook, budget calculation, interruption report and safe resume decision.

Proctor boundary: No live API required; no secret in submissions; no automatic paid schedule.

### Week 9 — Specialist track practicum
Outcome: Audit a research argument in one of six unresolved tracks or reconstruct Poincaré as an external benchmark.

Lab: Use a track-specific exercise below; compare a source theorem with the precise hypothesis and find a limitation.

Required evidence: Two verified source locators, theorem statement, dependency matrix and substantive skeptical challenge.

Proctor boundary: A known special case is not a universal result or literature novelty.

### Week 10 — Formal proof and acceptance
Outcome: Separate checked formal terms, unproved axioms, source hypotheses and natural-language plans.

Lab: Prove a small supporting lemma by hand; if using Lean, record exact statement, toolchain, assumptions and actual checker output. Inspect a deliberately missing assumption.

Required evidence: Small lemma proof, assumption audit and verification boundary; explicit NOT_PERFORMED if no formal checker ran.

Proctor boundary: Never treat sorry/admit, an invented axiom, or an AI verdict as a kernel proof.

### Week 11 — Research dossier and moderation
Outcome: Assemble evidence and defend a pass/revise decision for research process quality.

Lab: Audit one fresh agent packet, reproduce permitted checks, document failed attempts and classify remaining gaps.

Required evidence: Markdown/DOCX research dossier, evidence manifest, feedback and revision plan.

Proctor boundary: Do not award proof status or expose private learner records.

### Week 12 — Proctored capstone
Outcome: Independently operate and critique one full bounded research batch.

Lab: Closed-AI oral scope questions, open-resource practical audit, then explain the first unresolved theorem and next falsifiable attack.

Required evidence: Observed session record, final dossier, independent calculations and oral defense.

Proctor boundary: A course pass is not Clay certification, proof validity or independent educational validation.

## Seven specialist tracks

Choose one in week 9. The source labels refer to the existing repository research notes or probes. Each learner must verify actual source hypotheses; no track requires a novel theorem or solving an open prize problem.

**Riemann — U002-RH / U003.** State the sparse-shift Hankel criterion; demonstrate a negative form on synthetic moments and distinguish it from actual xi evidence.

**P versus NP — PNP-B005 / B006.** Prove the disjoint-block solver reduction; explain why overlapping gates and unrestricted runtime remain unresolved.

**Navier–Stokes — TGA-U001 / NS-B002.** Derive the conditional Gronwall continuation inequality and distinguish it from a universal critical bound.

**Yang–Mills — TGA-U001 / YM transfer probe.** Prove the dense-sector spectral estimate implication; exhibit why one-channel decay is insufficient.

**Hodge — U002-HODGE / U003.** Explain the determinant-square cycle and Kummer detecting cycle, including why the family does not cover general K3 surfaces.

**BSD — BSD-B006 / TGA-U001.** Explain finite primary Sha and the Selmer corank sequence; identify the missing universal rank theorem and all-prime support distinction.

**Poincaré — External result ledger / simplicial sphere probe.** Reconstruct one documented proof dependency and explain why mod-2 homology does not characterize S³. No new unsolved conjecture.

## Assessment and mastery standard

Six module assessments are worth 10 points each; the final capstone is worth 40, for 100 total. The proctor applies the following published rubric. For each criterion give full points for precise, reproducible, correctly scoped evidence; roughly half for a substantive but incomplete attempt; zero for absent or invalid evidence. Record the actual points and written rationale, not only an aggregate.

**M1 (weeks 1–2) — Target and topology audit (10).** Exact statement and scope (4); AND/OR derivation (4); source traceability (2).

**M2 (weeks 3–4) — Proof and falsification audit (10).** Proof dependency analysis (4); exact negative control (4); scope (2).

**M3 (weeks 5–6) — AI and computational evidence audit (10).** Role critique (4); exact tool replay (4); tool boundary (2).

**M4 (weeks 7–8) — Provenance and operations audit (10).** Checkpoint audit (4); mutation/recovery (4); secret and budget controls (2).

**M5 (weeks 9–10) — Specialist and formal-proof audit (10).** Mathematical reasoning (4); source/assumption audit (4); verification boundary (2).

**M6 (weeks 11–12) — Dossier and proctor conduct audit (10).** Reproducible dossier (4); revision quality (4); ethical scope (2).

**Capstone (40):** oral scope and quantifiers (10); supervised practical replay and provenance (10); mathematical adversarial analysis (10); final dossier and defense (10). Require total ≥80, every module ≥7/10, capstone ≥30/40, and all critical gates satisfied. This is a proposed local training mastery standard, not an empirically calibrated score. The human proctor records PASS, REVISE or NOT_ASSESSED with evidence references and rationale. A revision retains the original artifact and feedback, then re-assesses affected criteria using equivalent tasks. No software test awards a learner a pass.

Critical gates (all mandatory): No false mathematical or educational certification; No fabricated source, checker run, review or evidence; No secret or identifiable learner data exposure; No execution of arbitrary model-authored commands; No unauthorized change to protected proof register. If any gate fails, stop progression, preserve evidence safely, document remediation and re-assess. An honest unsuccessful research attempt is not a critical failure.

## Proctor session protocol

Before: pin source commit; freeze permitted materials, rubric and alternate task; explain scope and accommodations; distinguish practice from the assessed session; verify offline environment and no exposed credentials. During: observe learner's own work, record start/end and materials, ask for the first unsupported inference, require independent explanation and actual reproduction; log deviations. The oral scope section is unaided by AI; the practical section permits source, calculator and agent tools, with disclosure of all AI contributions. Do not use invasive surveillance. After: compare artifact hashes and exact output; record score rationale and feedback; offer a documented revision and appeal to another accountable assessor. Keep personal records in a private permission-controlled location; publish only consented deidentified aggregates.

Suggested capstone time: 20 minutes unaided oral questions, 70 minutes practical audit and mutation test, 30 minutes dossier defense. Accommodations may change timing without changing competency criteria.

## Executable practical lab

From repository root (on the agent/proctor branch), use Python 3.11:

```sh
python -m mathos.research.tga_agent --attempts 2 --max-calls 8 --output-dir artifacts/proctor-lab
python -m mathos.research.tga_agent --attempts 1 --max-calls 4 --output-dir artifacts/proctor-lab --resume
python -m mathos.research.tga_proctor --checkpoint artifacts/proctor-lab/state.json
python -m mathos.research.tga_proctor
python -m unittest discover -s tests -p 'test_tga_proctor.py' -v
```

The proctor tool checks the source-bound checkpoint structure, fair target selection, role/call accounting and exact replay of recorded bounded evidence. It reports only structural consistency or rejection. It never authenticates a learner or model, proves a universal theorem, changes the register, calls a paid model or assigns a grade. Read-only audit on copies for mutation exercises. The plan-only command validates the curriculum manifest and source references; it does not assess learner competency.

## Private assessment record template

Course ID; learner pseudonym/private ID; proctor ID and relationship/conflicts; session date and duration; pinned source commit; approved accommodations; permitted materials and AI disclosure; artifact references and hashes; M1–M6 points and rationale; four capstone points and rationale; critical gate evidence; total; proposed PASS/REVISE/NOT_ASSESSED; remediation or appeal; accountable proctor sign-off. Do not place this completed record in the public repository.
